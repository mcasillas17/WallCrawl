# Gluteal tendinopathy (greater trochanteric pain syndrome)

General exercise information for planning around a condition. This is not treatment.

**Overall evidence grade: low.** Structured data is in `gluteal_tendinopathy.json`.

## What the evidence shows

- **Education plus exercise is the best-supported approach.** In the LEAP trial (204 people), it beat corticosteroid injection and wait-and-see on global improvement at 8 weeks and 52 weeks. Pain was lower than injection at 8 weeks, with no difference at 52 weeks (Mellor 2018).
- **Reviews are favourable but cautious.**
  - Kjeldsen 2024 (6 RCTs, 733 patients) supports exercise as first-line treatment, but notes that this rests on few trials.
  - Bremer 2025 "cautiously" recommends exercise and education. Its "moderate" rating means findings from a single high-quality trial.
  - Cordeiro 2024 rates certainty low to very low.
  - Wang 2025 ranks exercise highly for pain, but with substantial heterogeneity. The extracted text did not include its GRADE ratings.
- **It is unclear which part works.** McMillan 2022 (132 postmenopausal women) found no difference between tendon-specific and sham exercise when all groups received education on compression and load. No individual exercise is proven better than another. In a 30-person pilot, isometric and isotonic programmes both showed improvement, with no untreated control (Clifford 2019).

## Is the diagnosis contested?

Partly. GTPS is a non-specific umbrella term covering bursitis, snapping hip and gluteal tendon disorders (ISHA 2023). There is no consensus on the best clinical tests (Grimaldi 2015). In one small study, 88% of asymptomatic women had tendon changes on MRI (Ganderton 2017), so imaging findings do not establish the cause of pain.

## Movements to avoid or modify

| Movement | Action | Strength of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Stretches pulling the hip into adduction (iliotibial band, across-body piriformis or glute stretches) | Avoid | Expert opinion |
| Loaded exercise crossing the midline or dropping the pelvis | Modify | Expert opinion |
| Heavy single-leg squats and similar lifts | Modify | Expert opinion |
| Lying on the painful side, or with the top leg across the body | Modify | Expert opinion |
| Clamshell, with or without a band | Modify | Expert opinion, plus EMG in healthy adults |
| Long, tempo or hill running and plyometrics | Modify | Expert opinion |
| Hill walking, stairs and long walks | Modify | Expert opinion |
| Prolonged single-leg balancing | Modify | Expert opinion, extrapolated from daily activities |
| Fast progression or strengthening through significant pain | Modify | Expert opinion, using untested trial programme rules |

The trial pain rule allowed up to 5/10 during strengthening, only if it eased afterwards and did not increase overnight or the next day (Mellor protocol 2016, Clifford 2019). Increased night pain may mean load was too high (Grimaldi and Fearon 2015).

Compression education was part of the effective programmes, but no trial tested it alone.

## Red flags

Stop the plan and seek medical assessment for:

- **Possible fracture:** weight-bearing hip or groin pain after a fall or rapid activity increase, especially with osteoporosis. Pain linked to repetitive weight-bearing exercise may be a stress fracture.
- **Possible joint infection (urgent):** sudden hip pain without injury that makes weight bearing difficult, especially with diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, recent joint surgery, a prosthesis or age over 80.
- **Possible bone disease:** deep unrelenting pain, possibly worse at night, with a cancer history or unexplained weight loss.
- **Possible osteonecrosis:** pain on walking with risk factors such as corticosteroid or heavy alcohol use, smoking, lupus, sickle cell disease or HIV infection.
- **Possible inflammatory disease:** heat, redness or swelling in several areas, morning stiffness over 1 hour, or symmetrical joint signs.

Ask a clinician to check for another source when pain spreads below the knee, comes with low back pain, or comes with groin pain, morning stiffness, lost motion, locking or giving way.

## Limitations

- **Guidelines:** the searches found no formal practice guideline, NICE guideline or Cochrane review specific to this condition. The search was not exhaustive. The closest documents are an expert consensus (ISHA 2023) and a local NHS fact sheet.
- **Consensus method:** ISHA's questions were generated by expert opinion and its panel completed online surveys without open discussion. It reports limited high-quality research.
- **Generalising:** participants were mostly women, many postmenopausal. Results may not transfer to men or younger athletes.
- **Doses:** LEAP included supervised sessions and specialist equipment that an app cannot reproduce. Some values in its flattened table were ambiguous, and "BD" is defined only as "bi-daily". The Clifford programme comes from a small unblinded pilot.
- **Red flags:** no source provides a condition-specific list. The list combines a differential-diagnosis table with a general hip pain review, and the action wording was written for this app.
- **Reading depth:** Kjeldsen 2024, McMillan 2022, Grimaldi 2015, Moore 2019 and Ganderton 2017 were read as abstracts. The other sources were read as extracted full text, with figures not viewed. Doses should be checked against the originals before they ship.

## Verification

An independent citation check opened all 15 sources and confirmed that each exists and matches its record. All 3 catalog IDs exist and are the same movements.

**Removed:**

- **Supine isometric abduction with belt or band:** the listed dose (1 set of 3-5, 5-10 s holds) appears in neither block of the LEAP table. The 5-10 s hold sits with 10 reps x 1-2 sets, and 3-5 reps x 1 set sits with the 5-15 s hold.
- **Glute bridge cue "keep the knees apart":** it is not in the cited source.
- **Squat cue "do not let the knees collapse inward":** it is not in the cited source.

**Corrected:**

- **Fast progression or strengthening through significant pain:** changed from RCT to expert opinion, because the pain limit was a programme rule and was not tested as a variable.
- **Loaded exercise crossing the midline:** removed the ISHA 2023 citation, because ISHA addresses adduction in sitting, standing and sleeping rather than loaded exercise.
- **Citations:** corrected author names for Cordeiro 2024, added the PMID for Grimaldi and Fearon 2015, and added missing volume, issue or page details for four others.

**Could not be verified:**

- Page numbers for Moore 2019 were not in its Europe PMC record.
- The LEAP table was checked only as XML, so sets, reps and frequency for standing isometric abduction stay unset.
- Figures in the PDFs were not viewed.
- The source defines "BD" only as "bi-daily".
