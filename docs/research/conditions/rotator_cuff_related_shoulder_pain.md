# Rotator cuff-related shoulder pain: literature review

**Overall evidence grade for exercise guidance: low.** Guidelines strongly endorse exercise as first-line care, but evidence for any specific programme, dose, load or movement restriction is weak. No study found tests overhead or pressing exercise as a treatment or restriction variable in this condition. This is general information for working around a condition, not diagnosis or treatment.

## Scope and search log

**Scope.** Adults with rotator cuff-related shoulder pain (RCRSP), including subacromial pain and tendinopathy, with attention to overhead and pressing exercise. The label is contested: it is an umbrella covering subacromial pain or impingement, tendinopathy, and partial-thickness and atraumatic full-thickness tears. [Lewis 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.math.2016.03.009) lists the cause and source of symptoms and a definitive diagnosis as unresolved, and guidelines use narrower terms such as "rotator cuff tendinopathy" ([Desmeules 2025](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2025.13182)) and "subacromial pain" ([Pandey 2025 (BESS)](https://doi.org/10.1177/17585732251374282)). The AAOS guideline is mainly about tears, so it applies only indirectly to tear-free pain.

**Record.** 85 sources: 29 systematic_review, 18 rct, 10 guideline, 10 biomechanical, 6 observational, 4 narrative_review, 3 rct_protocol, 3 consensus_statement, 2 scoping_review. By access: 61 abstract_only, 24 full_text.

**Not searched or not read.** NICE CKS returned HTTP 403 and was not read. WHO was not searched separately, and no NICE or WHO document specific to rotator cuff pain turned up. The Cochrane Library site was not queried directly; Cochrane reviews came through journal filters. All PMIDs were checked against PubMed metadata.

## Evidence summary

### Guidelines and consensus

- [Desmeules 2025](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2025.13182) (JOSPT, full text): active exercise, which may include motor control and/or resistance training with various loads, is Grade A initial treatment (rec 25). It gives no sets, repetitions or frequency. High versus low load showed no significant pain difference (short term SMD -0.15, 95% CI -0.93 to 0.62; medium term SMD -0.19, -0.49 to 0.11; very low certainty). One RCT (n=102) favoured higher dose for pain with activity (MD -1.6/10, -2.7 to -0.5; low certainty). Clinic versus home exercise did not differ (MD -0.31/10, -0.66 to 0.03; 3 RCTs, n=478; low certainty). Eccentric results were mixed: no significant disability benefit and, in one of two analyses, a medium-term pain benefit (SMD -0.62, -1.11 to -0.13; low certainty). Education including activity modification is Grade C (rec 24). Imaging is not for initial management (Grade F, rec 9). Injection can give short-term relief (Grade B, 19a) but is not first line (Grade C, 19b). Return to sport should consider capacity and load tolerance (Grade F, rec 35), using reliable, valid and responsive patient-rated tools (Grade F, rec 36), with no timelines.
- [Pandey 2025 (BESS)](https://doi.org/10.1177/17585732251374282) (British Elbow & Shoulder Society, 24 PICO questions): physiotherapy versus placebo is a strong positive (low to high evidence). Which regime works is a conditional positive on low evidence: below or above pain threshold, dose and supervision are all uncertain. No optimum duration can be recommended. Most symptoms respond within three months. One injection can be considered at 6 to 8 weeks without improvement, and multiple injections are no better than one.
- [AAOS 2025](https://www.aaos.org/globalassets/quality-and-practice-resources/rotator-cuff/rotator-cuff-2025/rotator-cuff-cpg.pdf) (tear-focused): physical therapy and surgery both significantly improve patient-reported outcomes in small to medium full-thickness tears (strong). Tear size, atrophy and fatty infiltration may progress over 5 to 10 years; tear size rose over 5 mm in 19 patients (59%) and over 10 mm in 13 (41%) at 10 years. After repair of small tears, supervision visits add nothing to one instruction session plus a home programme (moderate). Delayed surgery in 14 crossover patients gave a Constant score 10.0 points lower (p=0.03).
- [Lambers Heerspink 2026, Part 1](https://doi.org/10.2340/17453674.2026.45365) (Dutch update): exercise is required in non-surgical care, but "scientific evidence is lacking on the form or protocol of this therapy". It advises gradual loading within sports and work. [Lambers Heerspink 2026, Part 2](https://doi.org/10.2340/17453674.2026.45410) advises non-surgical care first for an isolated non-traumatic supraspinatus tear, considering surgery after 3 to 6 months (low to very low evidence).
- [Diercks 2014](https://doi.org/10.3109/17453674.2014.920991) (older Dutch guideline): exercise beats no treatment (levels 1-2), preferably "low intensity and high frequency, within the pain threshold, and focusing on eccentric training". Later documents do not support its eccentric and pain-threshold emphasis.
- [Vandvik 2019](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l294) makes a strong recommendation against subacromial decompression, and [Lafrance 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.11306) does not recommend it for tendinopathy. [Lafrance 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.11306) recommends active, task-oriented rehabilitation and an early return-to-work plan; [Dyer 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.11307) reports its methods (AGREE II 83%).
- [Eubank 2016](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12874-016-0165-8) (14-expert Delphi): the only staged dose found. All chronic patients should try a home programme first. [Hurley 2026, Part I](https://doi.org/10.1002/arj.70365) (97 surgeons) says most partial or chronic full-thickness tears warrant a rehabilitation trial first. [Hurley 2026, Part III](https://doi.org/10.1002/arj.70367) says return to play should rely on strength, motion, proprioception, pain and psychological readiness rather than a fixed timeline.

### Does exercise work?

- [Page 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012224): 60 trials, 3620 participants; 43 trials at high risk of bias. Manual therapy plus exercise versus placebo at 22 weeks (n=120): pain MD 6.8/100 (-0.70 to 14.30), function 7.1/100 (0.30 to 13.90), success 57% vs 41% (RR 1.39, 0.94 to 2.03). The authors judge none of this clinically important. There were no important differences versus injection or decompression (low quality). Mild adverse events were 31% vs 8% (RR 3.77, 1.49 to 9.54).
- [Steuri 2017](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096515): exercise versus non-exercise control, pain SMD -0.94 (-1.69 to -0.19); specific versus generic -0.65 (-0.99 to -0.32); added manual therapy -0.32 (-0.62 to -0.01); very low quality. [Silveira 2024](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0294014): shoulder-specific exercise versus usual care, pain MD -2.1 (-3.5 to -0.7), adjuncts adding little. [Pieters 2020](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2020.8498): umbrella review with a strong recommendation for exercise first, and moderate evidence of no effect for laser, shockwave, pulsed electromagnetic energy and ultrasound.
- [Liu 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msksp.2026.103556): exercise versus passive physical therapy, short-term pain SMD -0.14 (-1.25 to 0.96), disability -0.06 (-1.57 to 1.45); very low to low certainty. [Tena-Portilla 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/09593985.2026.2717392): 34 articles, 2019 subjects; DASH MD -8.50 versus no intervention, no significant SPADI differences, no approach clearly best; low certainty.
- [Hopewell 2021 (GRASP)](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0140-6736(21)00846-1): 708 adults. Progressive exercise versus best practice advice over 12 months, SPADI MD -0.66 (99% CI -4.52 to 3.20). Injection gave no long-term benefit (-1.11, -4.47 to 2.26). Both arms exercised, so this does not show exercise is useless.
- [Dube 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105027): 123 adults. Strengthening versus education, QuickDASH 1.2 (-4.9 to 7.4) at 24 weeks.
- [Holmgren 2012](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.e787): 97 people on a surgical waiting list. Surgery chosen by 20% vs 63% (OR 7.7, 3.1 to 19.4), with Constant-Murley 15 points better at 3 months (8.5 to 20.6). The control was unloaded. [Petersson 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jse.2024.10.027) reports results maintained at 10 years, with 83 of 97 followed.
- [Wu 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2025.1560597) specific versus non-specific exercise gave modest effects (pain SMD -0.31, CMS 0.59, DASH -0.60), with programmes under 2 months marginally better. [Swinton 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjsem-2022-001389) supplies pre-post benchmarks: pain SMD small 0.5, medium 0.9, large 1.4. [Zhang B 2026](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2025.13445): within-group strength gains (external rotation SMD 0.56, 0.39 to 0.74), none without exercise.

### Load and dose

- [Ingwersen 2017 (RoCTEx)](https://doi.org/10.1177/2325967117723292): high versus low load in 100 patients, DASH adjusted difference -1.37 (-6.72 to 3.99; P=.61). Interaction with corticosteroid favoured high load (P=.028, small subgroup). Compliance 57% vs 61%.
- [Clausen 2021 (SExSI)](https://doi.org/10.1177/03635465211016008): adding a large strengthening dose in 200 patients, SPADI MD 0.6 (-5.5 to 6.6) at 4 months. Exacerbations of at least a week: 49 vs 47.
- [Naunton 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269215520934147): progressive resisted exercise versus placebo or no treatment, 15/100 points (9 to 21), uncertain clinical benefit; non-progressive 4 points (-2 to 9). [Malliaras 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2020.06.013): conflicting, function +20/100 (12 to 28) in one trial of 102 with little pain difference; low to very low certainty. [Pavlova 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-105754): across tendinopathies, external resistance versus body mass beta 0.50 (95% CrI 0.15 to 0.84) in large effect size domains and 0.04 (-0.21 to 0.31) in small; less-than-daily loading gave greater pooled effects. [Naunton 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msksp.2024.103218) shows high-load dumbbell work is feasible (no serious adverse events, 77% adherence). [Liaghat 2025](https://doi.org/10.1515/sjpain-2024-0072): high and low load gave similar pain trajectories, in hypermobile shoulders rather than RCRSP. [Kara 2024](https://doi.org/10.1097/JSM.0000000000001191): blood flow restriction showed no superiority on pain or function (n=28).

### Exercise type

- [Lafrance 2024](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2024.12453): motor control versus nonspecific exercise, disability SMD -0.29 (-0.51 to -0.07) short term and -0.33 (-0.57 to -0.09) medium term; moderate certainty; pain -0.19 (-0.41 to 0.03). Effects may come from progression and tailoring. Eccentric, scapular and intensity comparisons remain uncertain, and no trial compared frequencies or times.
- [Larsson 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-019-2796-5): eccentric versus other exercise, pain MD -12.3 (-17.8 to -6.8), not clinically important per its authors; function SMD -0.10 (-0.79 to 0.58). [Dejaco 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00167-016-4223-x) and [Karabay 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/19417381241236817): no added effect on pain or function.
- [Zhang W 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13018-025-06514-4): concentric ranked best (SUCRA function 99.19%, pain 92.19%) with only 30 (disability network) and 14 (pain network) concentric participants, and high load worst. Treat with caution; see contradictions.
- [Zhong 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2024.1357763): scapular stabilisation versus conventional care, VAS -0.94 (-1.23 to -0.65), SPADI -10.10 (-18.87 to -1.33). [Clifford 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjsem-2020-000760): isometrics not superior to isotonics, limited evidence. [Cooper 2023](https://doi.org/10.3310/TFWS2748): shoulder may gain more from flexibility (0.18, 95% CrI 0.07 to 0.29), and combining concentric and eccentric may be best.
- [Heron 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physio.2016.09.001): open chain, closed chain and range-of-motion exercise did not differ at 6 weeks (n=120). [Littlewood 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269215515593784): one self-managed exercise versus usual physiotherapy, SPADI 3.2 (-6.0 to 12.4), with 26 of 86 patients missing primary outcome data. [Ortega-Castillo 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msksp.2022.102645): progressive exercise at 3 months, activity pain d=0.29, with no progression criterion shown superior.

### Pain during exercise

- [Tran 2025](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2025.13253): 16 trials across 18 studies. No difference between painful and nonpainful exercise at any time point; very low to low certainty; not shoulder-specific. It updates [Smith 2017](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-097383), which found a small short-term benefit (-0.27, -0.54 to -0.05).
- [Cavaggion 2024](https://doi.org/10.2147/OAJSM.S483272): 43 people with chronic RCRSP. No between-group SPADI difference to 6 months (T3 MD 4.15, -2.61 to 10.92). Four to seven episodes of persistent pain or fatigue after home exercises occurred in 14% vs 5%, not significant. [Cavaggion 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2022-070698) was the feasibility study: 88% supervised and 50% unsupervised adherence.
- [Kjaer 2024 (PASE protocol)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-024-07973-6) is the PASE protocol comparing allowed pain (up to 5) with avoided pain (below 3). [Axman 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2026.07.009) reports adherence only: mean pain 2.8 vs 1.6. Clinical results were not found. [Ullern 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-025-08411-7): 67% of 86 trial records mentioned no pain parameter.

### Overhead and pressing

No guideline or trial tests these. What exists is indirect.

- **Risk associations.** [Leong 2019](https://doi.org/10.2340/16501977-2598): work with the shoulder above 90 degrees OR 2.41 (I2 83%), age over 50 OR 3.31, diabetes OR 2.24. [Diener 2024](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-1957-6140): overhead work 2.23 (1.98 to 2.52), repetitive movements 2.17 (1.92 to 2.46), heavy loads 1.57 (1.15 to 2.15), yet its authors say no reliable correlation can be established. These are occupational exposures, not gym training. [Tooth 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/1941738120931764): in overhead athletes, range of motion, cuff weakness and training load are modifiable factors, and previous injury strongly raises risk.
- **Mechanism.** [Park 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-76704-z): 15 studies, 775 participants. Acromiohumeral distance did not differ from controls at neutral (MD 0.28 mm, -0.13 to 0.69) and had no consistent link to pain. [Cavaggion 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/10538127251334430): no change in subacromial space with exercise, and its authors say increasing space should not be a goal of rehabilitation. By contrast, [Taghizadeh Delkhoush 2026](https://doi.org/10.1123/jsr.2025-0060) and [Eraslan 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/15438627.2022.2052068) study space as the outcome; [Motlagh 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2026.2639894) infers higher risk in female powerlifters from bench-position measures.
- **Lifters.** [Aasa 2017](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096037): shoulder among the most common sites, 2.4 to 3.3 injuries per 1000 hours in weightlifting and 1.0 to 4.4 in powerlifting. [Kolber 2010](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181dc4330): up to 36% of documented resistance training injuries involve the shoulder (mostly retrospective surveys). [Pirruccio 2019](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-0927-6818): US emergency presentations rose from about 8,073 to 14,612 per year, with no exposure denominator. [Daher 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/17585732241258743): technique, age, vulnerable positioning and overtraining are named contributors, with no pooled risk. [Kolber 2017](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000001554): symptomatic weight trainers had less rotation range and weaker external rotators and lower trapezius (cause unclear). [Montoya-Minano 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/jfmk11010023): tendinopathy on ultrasound 93.3% vs 41.2%, from only 15 trainers.
- **Technique.** [Noteboom 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2024.1393235) is covered below. [Gundersen 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/14763141.2025.2590028): narrower shoulder-press grips reduced shoulder net joint moments (11 resistance-trained men). [Schutz 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports10020019): cable pulley exercises gave larger maximum shoulder moments than bench press. [Motlagh 2024](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000004973) recommends autoregulation over repetitions to failure, while [Clausen 2021 (SExSI)](https://doi.org/10.1177/03635465211016008) trained to failure with no visible rise in flares.
- **Empty and full can.** [Timmons 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jse.2015.08.046), [Thigpen 2006](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546505281797), [Robert-Lachaine 2015](https://doi.org/10.4085/1062-6050-50.9.06); the last also found added 2.27 kg load did not disrupt rhythm.

## What to avoid or modify

All items are "modify". No source supports a blanket ban on overhead movement, pressing or any named gym exercise.

**Cautions**

- **Eccentric-emphasis rotator cuff strengthening with band or light weight:** The trial appendix shows a full-can raise for supraspinatus and side-lying external rotation for infraspinatus and teres minor, with weeks 9 to 12 becoming concentric/eccentric (3 sets of 10, then 15, repetitions). The article says load came from weights and elastic bands. Participants had pain for more than 6 months, were on an orthopaedic pathway, received a corticosteroid injection first, and had physiotherapist-adjusted load. Do not treat this high-frequency dose as a proven unsupervised minimum. Certainty for eccentric programmes over nonspecific exercise is low to very low. Later evidence does not favour eccentric work: JOSPT 2025 reports one analysis with no significant pain or disability benefit over other exercise and another with a medium-term pain benefit only (SMD -0.62, 95% CI -1.11 to -0.13, low certainty), Larsson 2019 found a pain difference it judged not clinically important, and Dejaco 2017 and Karabay 2025 found no added effect on pain or function. Larsson also reports 6 to 8 weeks as almost as effective as 12.
- **Posterior shoulder stretch (cross-body):** The trial appendix photo shows a cross-body stretch with the back against a wall. It was one part of a multi-exercise programme and was not tested alone. RoCTEx used a shorter dose for its posterior capsule stretch: five repetitions of 20 seconds in the protocol, three holds of about 15 seconds in the published appendix. A meta-analysis of modified posterior stretching reports large effects (activity pain SMD -1.45, 95% CI -2.49 to -0.41) from small trials, with no GRADE rating and no dose in the abstract. Cooper 2023 suggests flexibility work may beat resistance at the shoulder, on low-quality evidence.
- **Arm-supported isometric hold in the direction of pain:** Lewis calls this anecdotal and says conclusive evidence for the shoulder is not available. Individual response should decide whether it continues. A review found limited evidence that isometrics are not superior to isotonic exercise for chronic tendinopathy, and for acute rotator cuff tendinopathy no more effective than ice in the short term. Only 2 of its studies were in the rotator cuff, and 7 of 10 were of poor quality.
- **Short-lever shoulder flexion, or flexion supported on a ball:** Offered by Lewis as gentle loading for the irritable presentation; it should ideally not increase pain. Progress only as tolerated. A meta-analysis favours motor control programmes slightly, but it cannot separate their effect from progression and tailoring, and did not test this specific exercise.
- **Lower-load rotator cuff strengthening (RoCTEx low-load arm):** No significant difference from high load in this trial, with compliance of only 57% to 61%. Six-exercise home programme in the protocol: push-up plus, low row, scaption, side-lying external rotation, posterior capsule stretch, scapulae retraction, with scapular exercises at 20 RM. Pain must not exceed 5/10, or stay elevated beyond 30 minutes, or increase in following days. The trial excluded resting pain over 40 mm VAS, under 90 degrees of active elevation, full-thickness rupture, frozen shoulder and labral lesions.
- **Progressive high-load rotator cuff strengthening (RoCTEx high-load arm):** Not better than low load overall (DASH adjusted difference -1.37, 95% CI -6.72 to 3.99). A small subgroup interaction favoured high load with concomitant corticosteroid. Three patients across the trial were referred for operation for worsening (2 high-load, 1 low-load). At least one episode of increased pain over 1 hour affected 30.2% of high-load and 36.7% of low-load patients, with no significant difference. Same pain rule and exclusions as the low-load arm. Physiotherapy sessions supported progression.
- **Banded external rotation in about 45 degrees of scaption and abduction to about 45 degrees, with an isometric pause (SExSI add-on):** Null trial: adding this to usual care gave no benefit at 4 months (SPADI MD 0.6, 95% CI -5.5 to 6.6), so authors say adding more exercise is not a viable solution. Adherence was about a quarter of prescribed time under tension. Reduce load if a flare lasts more than 24 hours. Exercises: external rotation with elbow supported, abduction to about 45 degrees, external rotation with elbow unsupported.
- **Resisted external rotation, flexion and abduction with bands (GRASP progressive programme):** No better than one advice session with self-guided exercise over 12 months (SPADI MD -0.66, 99% CI -4.52 to 3.20). No serious adverse events; minor flares were not captured. Home programme, not gym lifting.
- **Self-paced progressive shoulder programme to moderate effort:** Uncontrolled cohort of 143 people, so effects cannot be attributed to the programme. Pain advice was to work 'to but not through' pain. The extract did not name each exercise.
- **Assisted range of motion (pulley or assisted abduction, elevation, rotation), then banded and scapular work:** Expert consensus only (level 2 or lower), from a regional panel with no patients. The only staged dose found. Pavlova 2023 reports larger pooled effects with less-than-daily resistance loading, so high frequency is not established, and no adverse-event data come with this dose.

Other doses found, not added as candidates because the exercises were unnamed or tested only in combination: [Page 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012224) reports manual therapy plus exercise over 10 supervised sessions of 30 to 45 minutes across 10 weeks, with home exercises for 22 weeks. [Dejaco 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00167-016-4223-x) compared two eccentric exercises twice a day with six concentric/eccentric exercises once a day, for 12 weeks. [Kara 2024](https://doi.org/10.1097/JSM.0000000000001191) used twice-weekly low-load work for 8 weeks.

## Contradictions and limitations

- **Does exercise beat alternatives?** [Steuri 2017](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096515), [Silveira 2024](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0294014) and [Pieters 2020](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2020.8498) favour exercise. [Page 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012224), [Liu 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msksp.2026.103556) and [Dube 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105027) report little or no important difference. Liu's very wide intervals do not prove equivalence. BESS notes 80% of those completing physiotherapy while awaiting surgery go on to surgery ([Pandey 2025 (BESS)](https://doi.org/10.1177/17585732251374282)).
- **Load.** [Zhang W 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13018-025-06514-4) says high load appeared worst, [Malliaras 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2020.06.013) conflicts, and [Ingwersen 2017 (RoCTEx)](https://doi.org/10.1177/2325967117723292) and [Clausen 2021 (SExSI)](https://doi.org/10.1177/03635465211016008) were null. Zhang's reported values such as 23.87 are implausible as SMDs and its direction conflicts with its stated convention, so it is a weak signal, not proof of harm.
- **Eccentric emphasis.** [Diercks 2014](https://doi.org/10.3109/17453674.2014.920991) favours it. [Desmeules 2025](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2025.13182), [Pandey 2025 (BESS)](https://doi.org/10.1177/17585732251374282), [Lafrance 2024](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2024.12453) and [Larsson 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-019-2796-5) do not support superiority. [Holmgren 2012](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.e787) included it, but against an unloaded control with manual mobilisation in the programme.
- **Pain rules.** [Diercks 2014](https://doi.org/10.3109/17453674.2014.920991) and [Chepeha 2025](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0328728) stay within pain; [Holmgren 2012](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.e787) and [Ingwersen 2017 (RoCTEx)](https://doi.org/10.1177/2325967117723292) allow up to 5/10; [Tran 2025](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2025.13253) and [Cavaggion 2024](https://doi.org/10.2147/OAJSM.S483272) find no difference. In Cavaggion, into-pain applied to one exercise only, so the contrast was limited. PASE clinical results were not found.
- **Supervision.** [Desmeules 2025](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2025.13182) finds it not superior; [Pandey 2025 (BESS)](https://doi.org/10.1177/17585732251374282) calls it uncertain; [AAOS 2025](https://www.aaos.org/globalassets/quality-and-practice-resources/rotator-cuff/rotator-cuff-2025/rotator-cuff-cpg.pdf) agrees only after small-tear repair and cites conflicting studies. [Swinton 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjsem-2022-001389) links it to larger pre-post effects. [Gutierrez-Espinoza 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rehab.2026.102106) reports a Constant-Murley advantage of 25.2 points (17.7 to 29.8) at 12 weeks, but its two programmes also differed in specificity and the effect is unusually large. [Hernandez-Sanchez 2025](https://doi.org/10.23736/S1973-9087.25.08607-1) prints estimates whose CIs exclude zero with non-significant P values, so they should not be quoted as between-group effects.
- **Frequency.** [Holmgren 2012](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.e787) used twice daily and [Eubank 2016](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12874-016-0165-8) four times daily, yet [Pavlova 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-105754) reports larger pooled effects below daily, uncontrolled and across tendinopathies.
- **Modality.** [Cooper 2023](https://doi.org/10.3310/TFWS2748) suggests flexibility may beat resistance at the shoulder, on low-quality evidence. Its proprioception interval (lower bound -1.8) looks like a source typo.
- **Subacromial space.** [Park 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-76704-z) and [Cavaggion 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/10538127251334430) find space does not track symptoms, while [Taghizadeh Delkhoush 2026](https://doi.org/10.1123/jsr.2025-0060), [Noteboom 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2024.1393235), [Motlagh 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2026.2639894) and [Montoya-Minano 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/jfmk11010023) reason from surrogates. This weakens space-based exercise rules on both sides.
- **Surgery.** [Pandey 2025 (BESS)](https://doi.org/10.1177/17585732251374282) says decompression shows no benefit over sham yet that surgery could be considered after 6 months. [Vandvik 2019](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l294) strongly recommends against it. [Lambers Heerspink 2026, Part 2](https://doi.org/10.2340/17453674.2026.45410) notes its 2021 module did the same. [Petersson 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jse.2024.10.027) reports satisfying results in non-responders, from a post-randomisation comparison. [Karjalainen 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013502): repair plus decompression versus exercise gave pain 0.87/10 better (0.43 to 1.30) at one year, probably little or no improvement, in mostly small degenerative tears.
- **Tears.** [AAOS 2025](https://www.aaos.org/globalassets/quality-and-practice-resources/rotator-cuff/rotator-cuff-2025/rotator-cuff-cpg.pdf) reports possible long-term tear progression under physical therapy, so tendinopathy trials do not transfer to known tears. Where a tear is suspected, [Lambers Heerspink 2026, Part 1](https://doi.org/10.2340/17453674.2026.45365) advises ultrasound and [Eubank 2016](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12874-016-0165-8) an ultrasound after 12 weeks of failed home exercise.
- **Doses.** No guideline gives sets or repetitions. Numeric doses come from single trial protocols, several null, or from consensus. Adherence was often poor ([Ingwersen 2017 (RoCTEx)](https://doi.org/10.1177/2325967117723292) 57% to 61%; [Clausen 2021 (SExSI)](https://doi.org/10.1177/03635465211016008) about a quarter of prescribed time under tension). Holmgren's population had long-standing symptoms, an injection first and physiotherapist-adjusted load, so its dose may not transfer to unsupervised use.

## Verification

Independent check: 85 sources checked.

**Removed**

- Red flag wording 'or from systemic illness' and 'or a cardiovascular cause': Lewis 2016 lists cervical, thoracic, abdominal, neural and vascular origins. It does not mention systemic illness or a cardiovascular cause in this context.
- Overhead item qualifiers 'repeated or prolonged' and 'while symptoms are present': Lambers Heerspink 2026 Part 1 gives a work recommendation to limit these movements, without either qualifier.
- Noteboom rationale: 'contradicts the common cue to tuck the elbows': The paper does not discuss this cue; it was the author's inference.
- Holmgren caution: 'using a dumbbell': The appendix text does not name equipment. The article says weights and elastic rubber band.
- Littlewood 2016: 'not powered for equivalence': Not in the abstract read. Replaced with its reported missing data, 26 of 86.
- Motlagh 2024: 'with no direct injury data': Not stated in the abstract read.

**Corrected**

- RoCTEx high-load tempo: about 2 seconds on lowering corrected to about 3 to 4 seconds on each lifting and lowering phase of the rotator cuff exercises (Appendix B).
- RoCTEx adverse events: the unassigned 30% to 37% corrected to 30.2% high-load and 36.7% low-load (Table 1).
- RoCTEx referrals: three patients attributed to the high-load caution corrected to 2 high-load and 1 low-load.
- RoCTEx stretch: the published appendix gives three holds of about 15 seconds, while the protocol gives five of 20 seconds. Both are now recorded.
- JOSPT 2025 eccentric evidence: 'no significant benefit' corrected, because one of its two analyses reports medium-term pain SMD -0.62 (-1.11 to -0.13, low certainty).
- Wu 2025: 'no clear superiority over general exercise' corrected. It reports modest effects favouring specific over non-specific exercise.
- Cavaggion 2024: 14% vs 5% is the share with 4 to 7 episodes of persistent pain or fatigue, not the share with any episode.
- JOSPT 2025 rec 36 concerns patient-rated outcome tools, not capacity and load tolerance, which is rec 35.
- Gundersen 2026: 'healthy' corrected to 'resistance-trained', as the abstract states.
- SExSI exercise name: abduction was to about 45 degrees with slight scaption, not abduction in 45 degrees of scaption.
- Empty-can item: 'lab studies in people without symptoms' corrected. Only Robert-Lachaine states asymptomatic participants in its abstract.
- Pain rule: 'need not be avoided outright' corrected to Tran 2025's 'may not need to be avoided', and Cavaggion's one-exercise contrast added.
- Holmgren weeks 9 to 12: 3 sets of 10, then 15, repetitions added from the appendix.
- Noteboom: the 16 kg bar used in the reported comparisons added.
- GRASP dose: the protocol was opened. It gives no sets or repetitions and uses the modified Borg scale, so it is now cited.
- Access levels: Rees 2021, Wu 2025, Lewis 2016 and the GRASP protocol changed from not_reconfirmed to full_text after being opened.

**Could not be verified**

- Publisher landing pages return HTTP 403 to automated requests, and Thieme (Diener 2024, Pirruccio 2019) returned 404 to them. DOI redirects go to the expected publisher, and all 83 PMIDs, DOIs and PMCIDs agree in PubMed. BESS and AAOS have no PMID and were checked from their PDFs. The Thieme DOIs were confirmed through PubMed metadata only.
- Depth: all sources were checked at abstract or metadata level. Full text was read by keyword only for sources behind avoidOrModify items, doses and red flags, plus Rees 2021 by section. The other evidence-summary claims were checked against abstracts, so details not in an abstract were not confirmed.
- Holmgren appendix photos were not re-examined; only its text was read. The cross-body wall stretch and catalog match therefore rest on the earlier pass.
- Page 2016 dose details (10 sessions, 22 weeks) were not reopened; the Cochrane full text was not retrieved in this pass. The abstract numbers were confirmed.
- Zhang W 2025: no numeric SUCRA for high-load training was readable, as ranking figures are images.
- Thigpen 2006: symptom status is not stated in the abstract and the full text is paywalled.
- PASE clinical results were not searched for again in this pass.
