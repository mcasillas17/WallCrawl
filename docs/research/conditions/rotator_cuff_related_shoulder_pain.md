# Rotator cuff-related shoulder pain

**Overall evidence grade for exercise guidance: low.** Guidelines broadly endorse exercise, but evidence for any specific programme, dose or load is weak. This is general information for working around a condition, not diagnosis or treatment.

## Is the diagnosis contested?

Yes. RCRSP is an umbrella label covering subacromial pain or impingement, rotator cuff tendinopathy, and partial-thickness and atraumatic full-thickness tears. Lewis (2016) reports that a definitive structural diagnosis has been contested and lists the cause and source of symptoms as unresolved. Guidelines still use narrower terms.

## What the evidence shows

- **Cochrane 2016:** Manual therapy plus exercise gave a small benefit over placebo in one trial of 120 people, not judged clinically important. Mostly temporary adverse events were more common (31% vs 8%).
- **Naunton 2020:** Progressive resisted exercise had uncertain benefit over placebo or no treatment (15 points on a 100-point scale, 95% CI 9 to 21). Non-progressive exercise showed no significant benefit. Certainty was low.
- **GRASP 2021:** There was no SPADI difference over 12 months between up to six sessions of progressive exercise and one advice session (-0.66, 99% CI -4.52 to 3.20). The advice arm also did self-guided exercise.
- **Lafrance 2024:** Motor control programmes were probably slightly better than nonspecific exercise for disability, though the cause may be progression and tailoring. Eccentric, scapula-focused and intensity comparisons remain uncertain. No trial compared frequencies or durations.
- **Zhang 2025 and Wu 2025:** Zhang ranked concentric training first for disability and said high-load training appeared to be the worst, using small arms and no certainty rating. Wu found modest effects and no clear superiority over general exercise, also with no GRADE rating.
- **Holmgren 2012:** In people with long-standing symptoms on an orthopaedic pathway, a specific programme improved the Constant-Murley score by 15 points more than unspecific exercise at 3 months (95% CI 8.5 to 20.6). Fewer chose surgery (20% vs 63%).

## Avoid or modify

All four items are "modify". No source opened supports avoiding a movement outright.

| Movement | Why | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Load causing pain above 5/10, or pain not back to baseline by the next session | One trial's reduce-load rule; thresholds vary across trials and are mainly pragmatic | Expert opinion |
| Fast, high-load, high-repetition resisted work when the shoulder is easily aggravated | Clinical observation; load management advised | Expert opinion |
| Assuming heavier or higher-volume training is better | Intensity evidence is uncertain; high-load training appeared worst in one network analysis; harm not shown | Systematic review |
| Activities that reliably provoke the pain | Part of guideline-style education and GRASP advice; effect not isolated | Guideline (secondary summary) |

## Candidate exercises

| Exercise | Catalog | Dose | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eccentric-emphasis rotator cuff strengthening | new | 3 x 15, twice daily for 8 weeks, then daily for 4 weeks (trial dose) | RCT |
| Posterior shoulder stretch (cross-body) | `cross-body-shoulder-stretch` | 3 holds of 30 s or more, twice daily (trial dose: 30 to 60 s in the article, 30 to 45 s in its appendix) | RCT |
| Arm-supported isometric hold | new | None found | Expert opinion |
| Short-lever or ball-supported flexion | new | None found | Expert opinion |

The trial dose came after a corticosteroid injection, with load adjusted by a physiotherapist. It is not a proven unsupervised minimum.

## Red flags

Stop and get medical assessment. The referral wording below is for clinicians and needs plain-language review before it appears in the app.

- **Same-day emergency referral:** a suspected infected joint, or a traumatic dislocation that has not been put back in place.
- **Urgent referral:** suspected tumour or malignancy (local two-week cancer pathway), a suspected acute cuff tear after trauma, or new inflammatory arthritis affecting several joints.
- **Medical assessment:** symptoms that may come from the neck, chest or abdomen, nerves or blood vessels, or from a cardiovascular or systemic cause.

## Limitations

- The JOSPT 2025 full text returned HTTP 403. Only its abstract was read, and its grades come from a secondary summary (Physiotutors), so they need checking against the original.
- NICE CKS could not be opened and is not used.
- No source opened supports a blanket ban on overhead movement, upright rows or any named gym exercise, so none is listed.
- The Holmgren appendix names the exercises. Its photo shows a cross-body posterior stretch; it has no band row.
- The only numeric dose comes from one single-centre trial, and its population may not match app users.
- Trials under-report dosing. Other shoulder catalog exercises, such as `band-pull-apart`, `scapular-push-up` and `doorway-chest-stretch`, are not listed here. They were not reviewed as candidates, although the Holmgren appendix includes a push-up plus against a table.
- Traumatic presentations, tears being considered for surgery, instability, frozen shoulder and inflammatory arthritis fall outside this evidence.

## Verification

An independent check opened all 13 sources.

- **Removed:** the band row (`banded-row`). The Holmgren appendix has no row; its nearest exercise is a standing shoulder retraction with a low-anchored band, so the catalog match is not confirmed.
- **Label corrected:** the 5/10 pain rule moved from RCT to expert opinion. It was a protocol rule the trial did not test separately, and Ullern 2025 reports that such rules rest mainly on clinical expertise.
- **Text corrected:** the appendix confirms the cross-body stretch position and the rotator cuff exercises. Details of Zhang, Lewis, Wu and several citations were also fixed.
- **Could not be fully verified:** the JOSPT 2025 full text (403), so its grades rest on the Physiotutors summary; Zhang's numeric SUCRA for high-load training; and Lafrance and Naunton beyond their abstracts.
