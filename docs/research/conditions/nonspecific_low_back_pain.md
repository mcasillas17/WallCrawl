# Nonspecific low back pain

General exercise information for a private offline workout app. This is not treatment.

**Evidence grade: moderate, for the general claim only.** Exercise probably reduces pain in chronic nonspecific low back pain compared with no treatment, usual care or placebo. Guidance on any individual exercise, restriction or dose is low to very low certainty.

**Diagnosis contested: no.** The label is a diagnosis of exclusion covering a mixed population. It presumes serious disease has been ruled out, and the app cannot do that.

## Key findings

- Hayden 2021 (Cochrane): moderate certainty for pain, -15.2 on a 0-100 scale, which meets the review's clinical threshold. Disability improved by -6.8, which falls short of its threshold.
- George 2021 (JOSPT guideline): for chronic pain, trunk strength and endurance, multimodal, trunk activation, aerobic, aquatic and general exercise get grade A. Movement control or trunk mobility exercise gets grade B. No type is clearly superior, and no standard dose is prescribed.
- Saragiotto 2016 (Cochrane): motor control exercise is not clearly better than other exercise.
- Owen 2020: Pilates, stabilisation, resistance and aerobic training ranked highest, all on low-quality evidence. Stretching did not differ significantly from control.
- Acute pain: exercise may have no clinically relevant short-term effect, and the evidence is very uncertain (IJzelenberg 2024; Karlsson 2020).

## Red flags

**Stop and seek emergency help immediately (possible cauda equina syndrome):**

- New or worsening trouble urinating or emptying the bladder, with back or leg pain
- New or worsening loss of bowel control or sensation, with back or leg pain
- New or worsening altered feeling around the buttocks, genitals or between the legs
- New or worsening sexual sensation or function changes, with back or leg pain
- New or worsening severe pain in both legs, or rapidly progressive leg weakness

**Stop exercising and get medical assessment:**

- Possible fracture (prompt): significant trauma, or severe localised pain with osteoporosis, long-term corticosteroid use or previous spinal fracture
- Possible malignancy (prompt): past cancer, unexplained weight loss or night pain
- Possible infection (urgent): fever, immunosuppression, injection-drug use, recent infection or recent spinal procedure. Absence of fever does not exclude infection.

The "prompt" wording is expert opinion, since the opened summaries set no timeframe. Sources: somerset_ces, iatrox_vadod, pogo_finucane_summary.

## Limitations

- **Doses are thin.** Remaining doses are programme-level figures from single trial arms, with no reps, holds or sets for the individual exercise.
- **Effects of single exercises are unknown.** Named exercises were tested inside programmes, often with other treatments.
- **Harms are poorly reported.** Reported harms were mostly minor, most commonly increased low back pain and muscle soreness.
- **Some evidence is dated or disputed.** Hayden's searches ended in April 2018 and Dahm's in May 2009. Owen 2020 carried an expression of concern that was later rescinded.
- **Some primary sources could not be read.** NICE pages, Cochrane Library pages, the IFOMPT full text and the WHO full guideline were unavailable. NICE is represented only by a 2016 secondary report, and a reported July 2026 NICE update was not verified.
- **Red flags rely partly on secondary summaries.** The Finucane abstract notes that most red flags lack strong diagnostic accuracy evidence.
- **Braces are recorded outside the movement list.** WHO advises against lumbar braces, belts and supports for most people.
- **Dead bug is left out.** It and similar candidates appeared only in search results that were not opened.
- **The app cannot screen for serious pathology.**

## Verification

An independent check opened all 21 sources.

**Items removed:**

- avoidOrModify: A movement that sharply reproduces or lastingly worsens the person's own symptoms: The Cochrane summary reports increased low back pain and muscle soreness as harms but never calls them temporary, so 'temporary increases' and the conclusion that short-lived soreness is not a stop signal are unsupported. iatroX does not address symptom-guided modification.
- candidateExercises: Bird dog: Dose unsupported. Hlaing 2021 introduces alternate arm and leg lifting only in week 4 of the strengthening arm, so it was not done for 4 weeks. Kanli 2024 and Asik 2025 do support the exercise itself, so it can be re-added with a dose those trials report.
- candidateExercises: Glute bridge: Dose unsupported. Hlaing 2021 introduces bridging in week 2 of the strengthening arm, so it was not done for 4 weeks. Kanli 2024 and Asik 2025 do support the exercise itself, so it can be re-added with a dose those trials report.
- candidateExercises: Abdominal drawing-in: Dose unsupported. Hlaing 2021 places the drawing-in stage in weeks 1-2, then moves to co-contraction, so the 4-week dose does not describe this exercise. Kanli 2024 does list abdominal hollowing, but with no dose.
- candidateExercises: Repeated prone extension: The guideline does not report an acute trial. Shah and Kage enrolled patients with pain under 3 months and appears in its chronic section, and manual therapy outperformed repeated prone extension after 7 sessions. That does not support the dose description or the guideline strength label.

**Labels and citations corrected:**

- Lumbar flexion while lifting: evidenceStrength systematic_review to expert_opinion. Saraceni 2020 supports no blanket ban, but the modify rule itself is opinion.
- Heavy spinal loading during a flare: evidenceStrength biomechanical to expert_opinion. The McGill abstract does not address flares, load limits or outcomes.
- Source dahm2022: PubMed and Europe PMC list this DOI as Cochrane 2010;(6):CD007612, PMID 20556780. Citation year and pmid corrected; the id is unchanged.
- Source pogo_finucane_summary: citation corrected, since the page attributes only part of its content to Finucane 2020.

**Sources removed as unused:** hayden2021_summary, who2023_record, hlaing2021

**Could not be fully verified:**

- The July 2026 NICE update noted in limitations was not checked and remains unverified.
- Kanli 2024 says 'prone plank' and does not state forearm support, so its match to catalog:plank is assumed. Asik 2025 names forearm plank but is not cited on that item.
- Red flag on bowel sensation: the Somerset page says 'not feeling you are pooing', while the item says sensation of needing to pass stool. Kept because the meaning is close and the safety action is the same.
- Candidate items citing george2021 rest on exercise categories only. The guideline does not name plank, side plank, curl-up or cat-cow.
