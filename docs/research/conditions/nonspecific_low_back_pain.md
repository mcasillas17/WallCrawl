# Nonspecific low back pain

General exercise information for a private offline workout app. This is not treatment.

**Evidence grade: moderate, for one claim only.** Exercise probably reduces short-term pain in chronic nonspecific low back pain compared with no treatment, usual care or placebo. Dose, single exercises and acute pain are low to very low certainty.

**Diagnosis contested: no.** The label is a diagnosis of exclusion covering a mixed population. It presumes serious disease has been ruled out, and the app cannot do that.

## Scope and search log

This review covers adults with nonspecific low back pain: chronic, acute and subacute, with prevention evidence where it bears on dosing. Radicular presentations have their own record and appear here only where a guideline combines them. It merges three independent searches run for guidelines, systematic reviews and dose analyses, and resistance, deadlift and biomechanics evidence. PMIDs for papers the searchers returned were checked against PubMed for first author, journal, year, DOI and PMCID; that check opened no paper.

Access levels: `full_text` means full text was available and read at least in the part noted in the JSON, often by keyword-targeted excerpts. `abstract_only` means the abstract. `registry_or_summary` means an official summary, protocol, landing page or news item.

Not covered: AAOS guidance, powerlifting injury epidemiology, and the Wilke and Callaghan load papers. A failed PubMed query and unmatched title phrases explain the last two. The search also passed over a retracted 2026 implementation review (PMID 41625965), which was not used.

## Evidence summary

### Chronic pain: overall effect

- [Hayden 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009790.pub2) (Cochrane, 249 trials): against no treatment, usual care or placebo, pain MD -15.2/100 (95% CI -18.3 to -12.2, moderate certainty, meets the 15-point threshold) and function MD -6.8/100 (-8.3 to -5.3, moderate certainty, below the 10-point threshold). Against other conservative care, pain MD -9.1 (-12.6 to -5.6, low certainty) and function MD -4.1 (-6.0 to -2.2). No difference from manual therapy (MD 1.0, -3.1 to 5.1).
- [Verville 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10926-023-10124-4) (commissioned for WHO, 13 low or unclear risk-of-bias RCTs, 1,362 participants): pain SMD -0.33 (-0.58 to -0.08) and function SMD -0.31 (-0.57 to -0.05), moderate certainty, fading by intermediate term (pain SMD -0.08, -0.29 to 0.13).
- [Belavy 2026](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjmed-2025-001908) (551 studies, 71,126 patients): short-term pain MD -15.59 as reported (-17.51 to -10.05), with no treatment clinically significant long term. GRADE low 1.4%, very low 98.6%.
- [Comachio 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jshs.2025.101038) (umbrella, 70 reviews): small, mostly short-term benefit, low to moderate certainty. [Chou 2017](https://doi.org/10.7326/M16-2459) and [Grooten 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-022-05722-x) agree. [Searle 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269215515570379) pooled SMD -0.32 (-0.44 to -0.19). The older [Hayden 2005 Cochrane](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000335.pub2) is superseded.

### Guidelines

- [George 2021](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2021.0304) (JOSPT). The letters are derived from the guideline's own wording map (should = A, may = B, can = C), because the extracted text dropped them. Chronic, should/A: trunk strengthening and endurance, multimodal, specific trunk activation, aerobic, aquatic, general exercise. Chronic, may/B: movement control or trunk mobility. Older adults, should/A: general exercise. Acute, can/C: exercise including trunk activation. It states "no clear evidence of the superiority of one form of exercise" and "No studies reported harm".
- [NICE NG59](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59/chapter/Recommendations) (original read): consider a group exercise programme; manual therapy only within a package that includes exercise; keep normal activities. Recommendations 1.2.13 and 1.2.14 were removed in July 2026.
- [WHO 2023 summary](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mca-documents/ageing/lbp/summary_who_guideline_lower-back-pain_brochure_v7.pdf): structured exercise may be offered (low certainty) with education, needling and others; a tailored package over a single intervention. [Briggs 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41256-025-00426-w): 24 recommendations, all conditional, 367 RCTs; [WHO 2023 news item](https://www.who.int/news/item/07-12-2023-who-releases-guidelines-on-chronic-low-back-pain) is the earlier news item.
- [Qaseem 2017](https://doi.org/10.7326/M16-2367): chronic, exercise first-line (strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence). Acute and subacute: most improve regardless; exercise not named.
- [KNGF guideline (Apeldoorn 2024)](https://doi.org/10.23736/S1973-9087.24.08352-7): strong recommendation for exercise in higher-risk profiles, conditional independent instruction at low risk. Acute and subacute: no clinically relevant effect vs no exercise (low certainty). No form appears more effective than another.
- [Stochkendahl 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-017-5099-2): recent onset; stay active, and if treatment is needed, education, supervised exercise and manual therapy. [Wang XQ 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/02692155241229398): subacute core or motor control allowed; exercise not listed for acute. [Skelly 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/pm/pnag110): reported by [Managed Healthcare Executive 2026](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/new-acute-low-back-pain-guideline-steers-clinicians-away-from-routine-imaging-and-opioids) as assuring patients that staying active will not cause harm, with added therapy if not eased after one to two weeks.
- [VA/DoD synopsis (Macedo 2024)](https://doi.org/10.1097/PHM.0000000000002356) with [AAFP 2023 summary](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2023/0400/practice-guidelines-low-back-pain): many structured programmes help, "Nearly every activity is beneficial". [iatroX VA/DoD summary](https://iatrox.com/guidelines/low-back-pain-and-sciatica) remains a secondary source for red flags only.
- [Gao 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41413-026-00572-y): strong for aquatic, tai chi, Baduanjin or qigong, McKenzie, cognitive functional therapy, pain neuroscience education; weak for core, Pilates and yoga. [Aldera 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm15020528): no standalone exercise recommendation found.
- Comparisons: [Zhou 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-024-07468-0) counts therapeutic exercise in 13 of 14 guidelines for chronic and 9 of 13 for acute pain. [Corp 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejp.1679) and [Nicol 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm12041685) agree on the broad direction. [Oliveira 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2665-9913(26)00077-9) was not readable beyond its abstract.

### Acute and subacute pain

- [IJzelenberg 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009365.pub2) (Cochrane; also published as [IJzelenberg 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2024.02.732)): against sham, pain MD -0.80 (-5.79 to 4.19), function MD 2.00 (-2.20 to 6.20), very low certainty. No clinically relevant effect against no treatment.
- [Karlsson 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-020-01412-8): little or no important difference, very low to moderate certainty. [Macedo 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012085): motor control showed no benefit over other treatments, and three small trials allow no firm conclusions. [Almeida 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009711.pub2): short-term pain MD -7.3 (-12.0 to -2.56) vs minimal intervention, low certainty; authors conclude McKenzie is not effective.
- [Gianola 2022](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-103596) is the outlier: immediate-term exercise SMD -1.40 (-2.41 to -0.40), with low risk of bias in 19.6% of trials.
- Staying active: [Dahm 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007612.pub2) and [Hagen 2002](https://doi.org/10.1097/00007632-200208150-00010), detailed in the table below.

### By modality in chronic pain

- **Pilates:** [Owen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2019-100886) pain SMD -1.86 (-2.54 to -1.19) vs true control, low quality; [Owen 2020 notice](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2019-100886eoc1) records a later-rescinded expression of concern. [Fernandez-Rodriguez 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.10671) SUCRA 93% for pain. [Yamato 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010265.pub2) pain MD -14.05 (-18.91 to -9.19) vs minimal intervention, low quality, and no conclusive superiority to other exercise.
- **Resistance:** [Owen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2019-100886) function SMD -1.14 (-1.71 to -0.56). [Tataryn 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-021-00306-w) posterior chain vs general exercise: pain SMD -0.61 (-1.21 to 0.00), disability -0.53 (-0.97 to -0.09), best at 12-16 weeks, adverse events RD -0.02 (-0.10 to 0.05). [Ranzani 2025](https://doi.org/10.33393/aop.2025.3533) loaded vs unloaded: pain at 7+ weeks MD -0.52/10 (-0.92 to -0.08), non-significant without one Kell trial. [Rodriguez-Dominguez 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/09638288.2025.2566275) is larger but small in pool.
- **Deadlift:** [Michaelson 2016](https://doi.org/10.2340/16501977-2091) VAS 43 to 27 and motor control 47 to 30 at 24 months, RMDQ 7.2 to 3.8 vs 7.1 to 3.6 (Group x Time p = 0.98). [Aasa 2015](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2015.5021) favoured motor control on PSFS (4.2 vs 2.5 points, P<.001), not pain (P = .505). [Berglund 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spinee.2017.07.178) found no group-level alignment change. [Stock 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports10110168) found no force deficit in trained adults with mild-to-moderate acute pain.
- **Motor control:** [Saragiotto 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012004) long-term pain MD -12.97 (-18.51 to -7.42) vs minimal intervention, not clinically better than other exercise.
- **Yoga:** [Wieland 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010671.pub3) function -1.69 RMDQ (-2.73 to -0.65), pain -4.53/100 (-6.61 to -2.46), clinically unimportant; clinical improvement RR 2.33 (1.46 to 3.71).
- **Walking:** [Vanti 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/09638288.2017.1410730) similar to other exercise. Prevention: [Pocovi 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(24)00755-4) HR 0.72 (0.60 to 0.85), median 208 vs 112 days.
- **Hips:** [Ceballos-Laita 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjpt.2023.100502) short-term gains at very low certainty, null later. [Rodriguez-Lopez 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports14100456) adding hip work was not significant between groups.
- **Network and umbrella estimates:** [Hayden 2021 NMA](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphys.2021.09.004) (217 RCTs) gave -15 to -19 for pain and -10 to -12 for function across the top types vs minimal treatment, and still advises the exercise people enjoy. [Li 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2023.1155225) tai chi SMD -2.11 (-3.62 to -0.61).
- **Prevention:** [Steffens 2016](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.7431) exercise plus education RR 0.55 (0.41 to 0.74), education alone 1.03 (0.83 to 1.27).

### Biomechanics, for context only

[Rezaei 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bj.2026.101012) gives order-of-magnitude L4-L5 values (symmetric 15-30 kg lift about 3-5 kN compression). [Armstrong 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jelekin.2024.102867) links lower moments to leg drive, close load and shorter lift time. [McGill 2012](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e31823a4063) reports a 16-kg kettlebell swing at about 3,200 N, with authors noting a few find swings irritating. [Cherni 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports14040151) secondary peak figures should not be cited without the primary paper. These do not set limits.

## What to avoid or modify

| Movement | Action | Basis | Evidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prolonged bed rest or stopping ordinary activity because of back pain | Avoid | Advice to stay active gave small improvements over advice to rest in bed for acute low back pain (2 trials, N = 401: pain SMD 0.22, 95% CI 0.02 to 0.41; function SMD 0.29, 0.09 to 0.49; moderate quality). For sciatica there was little or no difference, so this is weaker with leg symptoms. Advice to stay active alone has little benefit, but there is no evidence of harm from it and potential harm from prolonged bed rest. [NICE](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59/chapter/Recommendations) recommends encouraging normal activities, and the [George guideline](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2021.0304) retains text against education promoting extended bed rest. The [Dahm](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007612.pub2) search ended in May 2009. | systematic review | [Dahm 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007612.pub2), [Hagen 2002](https://doi.org/10.1097/00007632-200208150-00010), [NICE NG59](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59/chapter/Recommendations), [George 2021](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2021.0304) |
| Lumbar flexion (rounding the lower back) while lifting, only where it provokes the person's symptoms | Modify | Do not apply this as a blanket ban. Low-quality evidence found greater lumbar flexion during lifting is not a risk factor for onset or persistence (peak flexion difference 1.5 degrees, -0.7 to 3.7, longitudinal). An umbrella review of 41 reviews found no consensus on causality for bending or lifting. Against that, occupational lifting/carrying (OR 1.7, 1.4 to 2.2) and non-neutral postures (OR 1.5, 1.2 to 1.9) are associated with chronic pain, which is association from highest-versus-lowest work exposure and not evidence on gym loading. In a modelling study stoop lifting had lower compression but higher shear than squat lifting, so neither style is simply safe. The modify rule itself rests on individual symptom response (expert opinion). | expert opinion | [Saraceni 2020](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2020.9218), [Swain 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2019.08.006), [Jahn 2023](https://doi.org/10.5271/sjweh.4114), [von Arx 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2021.769117), [Nolan 2020](https://doi.org/10.1515/sjpain-2019-0089) |
| Loaded spinal flexion or lifting in a person with known osteoporosis, long-term corticosteroid use or previous spinal fracture | Modify | A secondary summary of the [IFOMPT framework](https://www.pogophysio.com.au/?p=8582) describes spinal fracture as often following a fall or lifting in flexion, with these as risk factors. Applying that to exercise selection is an inference, not a tested rule. Such users should get clinician clearance. | expert opinion | [POGO Physio summary](https://www.pogophysio.com.au/?p=8582) |
| Heavy spinal loading during a flare, such as heavily loaded hinges or squats, or heavy deadlifting with high current pain or low back-extensor endurance | Modify | Not a prohibition. Supervised heavy deadlifting was neither better nor worse than low-load motor control on pain and disability at 2, 12 or 24 months. But within the deadlift arm, higher pain, more disability and poorer Biering-Sorensen endurance predicted less benefit, and the authors advise sufficient back-extensor capacity and a low enough pain level first. That trial progressed training if pain had not increased. In one loaded-training trial, a transient flare-up in each group was managed by temporarily reducing load or repetitions. The prediction models are small and not validated, and no source gives a safe load limit. Fatigue protocols increased trunk range of motion, and higher load increased spinal flexion, in studies cited by a narrative review. Extending the deadlift findings to hinges and squats is an inference. | observational | [Michaelson 2016](https://doi.org/10.2340/16501977-2091), [Berglund 2015](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000000837), [Ranzani 2025](https://doi.org/10.33393/aop.2025.3533), [Cherni 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports14040151) |
| Using stretching as the entire exercise programme | Modify | Stretching is not contraindicated, but it did not differ from true control for pain or function in one network meta-analysis, was the one type not beating control for pain in another, and showed no effect on disability in older adults (MD -0.3, -9.53 to 8.93). Specific trunk activation outperformed stretching in small trials in a movement control impairment subgroup. Contrary evidence exists: flexibility exercise had a moderate-to-important function effect in [Hayden 2021 NMA](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphys.2021.09.004) and hamstring stretching reduced pain in [Gou 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/20503121241252251) (SMD -0.72, I2 89%). Combine it with strengthening or general exercise. | systematic review | [Owen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2019-100886), [George 2021](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2021.0304), [Fernandez-Rodriguez 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.10671), [Zhang 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13018-023-03988-y), [Hayden 2021 NMA](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphys.2021.09.004), [Gou 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/20503121241252251) |

## Red flags

**Stop and seek emergency help immediately (possible cauda equina syndrome):**

- With back or leg pain: new or worsening trouble starting or maintaining urination, or not emptying the bladder. [Somerset NHS](https://www.somersetft.nhs.uk/trauma-and-orthopaedics/trauma-and-orthopaedics/spinal-surgery-service/cauda-equina-syndrome/)
- With back or leg pain: new or worsening loss of bowel control or of the sensation of needing to pass stool. [Somerset NHS](https://www.somersetft.nhs.uk/trauma-and-orthopaedics/trauma-and-orthopaedics/spinal-surgery-service/cauda-equina-syndrome/)
- New or worsening altered feeling around the buttocks, genitals or between the legs. [Somerset NHS](https://www.somersetft.nhs.uk/trauma-and-orthopaedics/trauma-and-orthopaedics/spinal-surgery-service/cauda-equina-syndrome/), [iatroX VA/DoD summary](https://iatrox.com/guidelines/low-back-pain-and-sciatica), [Alshanqity 2025](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.100428)
- New or worsening changes in sexual sensation or function, with back or leg pain. [Somerset NHS](https://www.somersetft.nhs.uk/trauma-and-orthopaedics/trauma-and-orthopaedics/spinal-surgery-service/cauda-equina-syndrome/)
- New or worsening severe pain in both legs, or rapidly progressive leg weakness. [Somerset NHS](https://www.somersetft.nhs.uk/trauma-and-orthopaedics/trauma-and-orthopaedics/spinal-surgery-service/cauda-equina-syndrome/), [iatroX VA/DoD summary](https://iatrox.com/guidelines/low-back-pain-and-sciatica), [Alshanqity 2025](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.100428)

**Stop exercising and get medical assessment:**

- Back pain after significant trauma, or severe localised pain with osteoporosis, long-term corticosteroid use or previous spinal fracture. Stop exercising and get prompt medical assessment for possible fracture. The urgency wording is expert opinion, since the opened summaries set no timeframe. [POGO Physio summary](https://www.pogophysio.com.au/?p=8582), [iatroX VA/DoD summary](https://iatrox.com/guidelines/low-back-pain-and-sciatica)
- Back pain with a past history of cancer, unexplained weight loss or night pain. Stop exercising and get prompt medical assessment for possible malignancy. The urgency wording is expert opinion, since the opened summaries set no timeframe. [POGO Physio summary](https://www.pogophysio.com.au/?p=8582), [iatroX VA/DoD summary](https://iatrox.com/guidelines/low-back-pain-and-sciatica)
- Back pain with fever, immunosuppression, injection-drug use, recent infection or a recent spinal procedure. Absence of fever does not exclude infection. Stop exercising and seek urgent medical assessment for possible spinal infection. [POGO Physio summary](https://www.pogophysio.com.au/?p=8582), [iatroX VA/DoD summary](https://iatrox.com/guidelines/low-back-pain-and-sciatica)

[Alshanqity 2025](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.100428) describes one deadlift-associated L5-S1 extrusion with these signs, after a year of pain and resumed unsupervised training. It supports urgent referral over substituting exercise for such symptoms. It does not show how often this happens. Guidelines that exclude these cases ([KNGF guideline (Apeldoorn 2024)](https://doi.org/10.23736/S1973-9087.24.08352-7)) and warn against routine imaging without them ([NICE NG59](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59/chapter/Recommendations), [Managed Healthcare Executive 2026](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/new-acute-low-back-pain-guideline-steers-clinicians-away-from-routine-imaging-and-opioids)) do not add new signs.

## Contradictions and limitations

### Contradictions in the evidence

1. **Stretching.** [Owen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2019-100886) (no difference from true control, p>0.095) and [Fernandez-Rodriguez 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.10671) (only type not beating control for pain) against [Hayden 2021 NMA](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphys.2021.09.004) (flexibility important for function), [Hayden 2005 strategies](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-142-9-200505030-00014) (stretching and strengthening largest gains) and [Gou 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/20503121241252251) (hamstring stretching SMD -0.72, I2 89%). [Quentin 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18168430) also favours stretching, on pre-post effects.
2. **McKenzie.** [Hayden 2021 NMA](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphys.2021.09.004) found moderate to important effects, and [Hennemann 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/10669817.2024.2408084) found short-term benefit in people with a directional preference (lead author is a McKenzie Institute instructor). [Owen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2019-100886) and [Fernandez-Rodriguez 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.10671) did not, and [Almeida 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009711.pub2) concluded it is not effective for (sub)acute pain. [Halliday 2016](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2016.6379) found no difference from motor control. [Gao 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41413-026-00572-y) still grades it strong.
3. **Dose frequency and length.** [Arora 2026](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2026-111988) (3-4/week, 25-30 min, 8-13 weeks, limited benefit at higher exposure) against [Wang L 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2026.1836061) (6-7/week, 15-20 min, 16+ weeks), [Fernandez-Rodriguez 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.10671) (1-2/week, 3-9 weeks), [Zhang 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13018-023-03988-y) (60+ min suggested in older adults, while under-60 had a larger VAS estimate) and [Kell 2011](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181d09df7) (4 days/week best). [Liang 2024](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2024.12153) reports a U-shape. [Hayden 2005 strategies](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-142-9-200505030-00014) found no clear dose effect.
4. **Acute pain.** [IJzelenberg 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009365.pub2), [Karlsson 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-020-01412-8) and [KNGF guideline (Apeldoorn 2024)](https://doi.org/10.23736/S1973-9087.24.08352-7) found no clinically relevant benefit; [Gianola 2022](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-103596) found SMD -1.40 (-2.41 to -0.40) at immediate term against inert therapy. [Qaseem 2017](https://doi.org/10.7326/M16-2367) and [Wang XQ 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/02692155241229398) do not name exercise for acute pain, while [George 2021](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2021.0304) gives it can/C and [Stochkendahl 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-017-5099-2) suggests supervised exercise if treatment is needed. [Zhou 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-024-07468-0) counts 9 of 13 guidelines recommending it.
5. **Aerobic exercise.** [George 2021](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2021.0304) grades it should/A and [Verville 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10926-023-10124-4) found a pain signal (very low certainty), but [Searle 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269215515570379) calls cardiorespiratory programmes ineffective and [Kell 2009](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181918a6e) saw no significant improvement. No completed Cochrane review surfaced, only [de Zoete 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD015503).
6. **Core and motor control.** [George 2021](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2021.0304) gives should/A to specific trunk activation; [Gao 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41413-026-00572-y) only weakly recommends core stabilization; [Saragiotto 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012004) and [KNGF guideline (Apeldoorn 2024)](https://doi.org/10.23736/S1973-9087.24.08352-7) find it no better than other exercise. [Macedo 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012085) is null for acute pain. [Wang XQ 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/02692155241229398) allows it for subacute pain.
7. **Bracing.** Effective protocols used bracing ([Michaelson 2016](https://doi.org/10.2340/16501977-2091), [Park 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare14162550)), while [Lunkes 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphys.2026.03.007) found relaxed cueing slightly better in Pilates.
8. **Pilates ranking.** Network analyses rank it top ([Owen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2019-100886), [Fernandez-Rodriguez 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.10671), [Liang 2024](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2024.12153)), but [Yamato 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010265.pub2) found no conclusive superiority over other exercise (short-term disability MD -3.29, -6.82 to 0.24) and [Gao 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41413-026-00572-y) grades it weak. [Tottoli 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/02692155241277041) found its advantage over home exercise not clinically relevant.
9. **Yoga size.** [Li 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2023.1155225) gives SMD -1.76 for pain, far above [Wieland 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010671.pub3) (MD -4.53/100, clinically unimportant). [AAFP 2023 summary](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2023/0400/practice-guidelines-low-back-pain) reports that yoga does not improve outcomes more than physical therapy or strength training.
10. **Mind-body and aquatic.** [Gao 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41413-026-00572-y) strongly recommends aquatic exercise, tai chi and qigong, yet [Verville 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10926-023-10124-4) found no low or unclear risk-of-bias trial for them. [Li 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2023.1155225) ranks tai chi highly with a wide interval.
11. **Resistance size.** [Rodriguez-Dominguez 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/09638288.2025.2566275) (pain SMD -1.15, disability -2.76, GRADE moderate) against [Ranzani 2025](https://doi.org/10.33393/aop.2025.3533) (small, fragile), [Verville 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10926-023-10124-4) (strength training immediate pain MD -0.39, -1.16 to 0.38) and [Tomschi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/10538127261448967) (no added benefit from one session a week).
12. **Prevention.** [Comachio 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jshs.2025.101038) reports short-term RR 0.67 but long-term RR 1.04 (0.73 to 1.49), and no added benefit from high leisure activity. The 0.67 short-term figure matches [Shiri 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwx337), so the two may not be independent.
13. **Certainty of the headline claim.** [Hayden 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009790.pub2) and [Verville 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10926-023-10124-4) rate pain effects moderate certainty. [Belavy 2026](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjmed-2025-001908), with 551 studies, rates comparisons low (1.4%) to very low (98.6%). [Innocenti 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2023.09.001) shows outcome-measurement bias may slightly overestimate function effects.
14. **Deadlift set and rep reporting.** [Michaelson 2016](https://doi.org/10.2340/16501977-2091) gives no exact sets or reps; [Tataryn 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-021-00306-w) tabulates 5+ x 1-10 for the same trial population. The primary [Aasa 2015](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2015.5021) report was abstract only, so this is unresolved. [Michaelson 2016](https://doi.org/10.2340/16501977-2091), [Aasa 2015](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2015.5021), [Berglund 2015](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000000837) and [Berglund 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spinee.2017.07.178) share a trial population and count as one trial.
15. **Non-exercise conflicts, recorded only.** Acupuncture or needling: [NICE NG59](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59/chapter/Recommendations) and [Stochkendahl 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-017-5099-2) against, [WHO 2023 summary](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mca-documents/ageing/lbp/summary_who_guideline_lower-back-pain_brochure_v7.pdf) and [Qaseem 2017](https://doi.org/10.7326/M16-2367) for. NSAIDs: [Stochkendahl 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-017-5099-2) against, [Qaseem 2017](https://doi.org/10.7326/M16-2367) and [WHO 2023 summary](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mca-documents/ageing/lbp/summary_who_guideline_lower-back-pain_brochure_v7.pdf) for. [Aldera 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm15020528) keeps the psychological-package wording that [NICE NG59](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59/chapter/Recommendations) removed in July 2026.

### Limitations

- **The moderate grade is narrow.** It covers short-term pain versus no treatment, usual care or placebo in chronic pain ([Hayden 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009790.pub2), searches to April 2018). Function fell short of the review threshold, benefits fade ([Verville 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10926-023-10124-4): intermediate pain SMD -0.08, -0.29 to 0.13) and no treatment reached clinical significance long term ([Belavy 2026](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjmed-2025-001908)).
- **No established dose.** Every dose in this file describes what a trial did or what a model predicted. [Arora 2026](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2026-111988) states the evidence does not support a single optimal dose, and 84% of its trials were at high risk of bias. No guideline read gives a numeric prescription.
- **Exercises were tested inside programmes.** No trial here isolates the effect of plank, bird dog, dead bug, bridge or cat-cow. Exercise-level doses come mainly from [Park 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare14162550) (pilot in men) and [Kshirsagar 2026](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.114958) (non-randomised, young male runners), so transfer to other people is untested.
- **Home delivery is a real gap for this app.** Programme supervision beat home-only exercise in [Hayden 2005 strategies](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-142-9-200505030-00014) and [Quentin 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18168430), and [Nicol 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm12041685) reports insufficient evidence that home programmes differ from no care. Most deadlift evidence is supervised.
- **Harms are under-reported.** Adverse events appeared in 28.5% of trials in [Arora 2026](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2026-111988) and under 31% of reviews in [Comachio 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jshs.2025.101038). [IJzelenberg 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009365.pub2) could reach no conclusion on safety in acute pain. In [Hayden 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009790.pub2), the median was 0.14 events per participant vs 0.12, mostly minor. [Verville 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10926-023-10124-4) reported mixed-exercise harms OR 4.24 (0.69 to 25.95, 2 RCTs, very low certainty).
- **Biomechanics are not outcomes.** Load figures in [Rezaei 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bj.2026.101012), [McGill 2012](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e31823a4063) and [von Arx 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2021.769117), disc changes in [Yanagisawa 2021](https://doi.org/10.2463/mrms.mp.2020-0052) and flexion changes in [Shoji 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2025.1682991) come from healthy or trained people and set no safe limit. [McGowen 2024](https://doi.org/10.26603/001c.90707) used body weight only, so its title should not be generalized to loaded lifts. The landmark Wilke and Callaghan load papers and powerlifting injury epidemiology were not retrieved.
- **Implausible effects were not used.** [Pourahmadi 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/pm/pny208) (disability SMD -8.03) and the large effects in [Tan 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2026.1725132) and [Rodriguez-Dominguez 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/09638288.2025.2566275) did not inform doses or claims.
- **Access limits.** Many records are abstract only, and long reviews were read in part (see each reference). [WHO 2023 summary](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mca-documents/ageing/lbp/summary_who_guideline_lower-back-pain_brochure_v7.pdf) is a summary, since the full WHO guideline could not be retrieved, and all WHO recommendations are conditional ([Briggs 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41256-025-00426-w)). The original VA/DoD guideline was not read, so [VA/DoD synopsis (Macedo 2024)](https://doi.org/10.1097/PHM.0000000000002356) and [AAFP 2023 summary](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2023/0400/practice-guidelines-low-back-pain) stand in and no grades are reported. [Skelly 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/pm/pnag110) recommendations are known only from [Managed Healthcare Executive 2026](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/new-acute-low-back-pain-guideline-steers-clinicians-away-from-routine-imaging-and-opioids). [Oliveira 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2665-9913(26)00077-9) content is unread. Nothing can be said about AAOS, because no search targeted it.
- **Red flags are still partly secondary.** Non-emergency flags rest on [POGO Physio summary](https://www.pogophysio.com.au/?p=8582) and [iatroX VA/DoD summary](https://iatrox.com/guidelines/low-back-pain-and-sciatica), and their urgency wording is partly expert opinion. [Finucane 2020](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2020.9971) notes most red flags lack strong diagnostic accuracy evidence. [Alshanqity 2025](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.100428) is one case and cannot show causation or incidence.
- **Owen 2020.** [Owen 2020 notice](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2019-100886eoc1) records an expression of concern on [Owen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2019-100886), rescinded by a journal editorial note dated 27 July 2020.
- **Screening.** The movement control impairment subgroup is a clinician classification and cannot be self-assigned. The app cannot screen for serious pathology, and this is general information, not treatment.
