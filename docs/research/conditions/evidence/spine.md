# Spine evidence extraction — research draft

Accessed 2026-11-01/02 during this session. Seven assigned conditions only. This is a targeted, non-exhaustive investigation, not a systematic review or clinical approval. Publication years below are the source's actual dates, not search-engine relative dates. Source IDs are local to this report. All proposed app implications remain unresolved until clinical/content review and execution-level catalog comparison. No personal regimen is supplied.

Access: **FT** means underlying full article/guideline inspected; **abstract** means PubMed/publisher abstract only; **partial** means selected text available but full text not successfully inspected. For several PMC browser challenges, Europe PMC's fullTextXML endpoint succeeded; exact alternate URLs are recorded. A trial's inclusion/exclusion criteria limit applicability; they do not become universal contraindications. A beneficial intervention does not prove alternatives harmful. Study dose is extraction, never an automatically approved app dose.

## Chronic primary low-back pain

### Retrieved evidence

**LBP-G1 — WHO, 2023 final guideline.** [Official publication](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240081789); [recommendations](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK599213/); ISBN 9789240081789. **FT relevant exercise recommendation.** Adults including older adults, chronic primary LBP in primary/community care; specific causes require separate assessment. Structured exercise may be offered as part of care (conditional; low certainty). Individualization, preferences, capacity and access matter; a single preferred exercise modality or universal load/ROM ceiling is not established. Evidence includes temporary symptom exacerbation; harms are incompletely captured. This is a treatment recommendation, not evidence that every gym execution is appropriate.

**LBP-S1 — Verville et al., 2023, WHO exercise systematic review.** DOI [10.1007/s10926-023-10124-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10926-023-10124-4), PMID [37991647](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37991647/), [PMC10684665](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10684665/). **FT.** Search updated through May 2022; primary analysis 13 low/unclear-risk RCTs, 1362 adults; two trials included adults ≥60. Aerobic, strengthening, mixed, Pilates, flexibility, yoga and motor-control programs versus no intervention/usual care/sham. Benefits vary by modality, comparator and time; certainty for individual modalities low/very low. Harms sparse, mainly temporary pain increases. Supports an exercise category with individual selection; not an exact sequence or exclusion list.

**LBP-S2 — Hayden et al., 2021 Cochrane review.** DOI [10.1002/14651858.CD009790.pub2](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009790.pub2), PMID [34580864](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34580864/), PMC8477273. **Abstract and publisher conclusions inspected; not all underlying trials.** 249 RCTs, 24,486 participants, chronic nonspecific LBP >12 weeks. Earliest follow-up: exercise versus no treatment/usual care/placebo reduced pain 15.2/100 (95% CI 12.2–18.3), functional limitation 6.8/100 (5.3–8.3); functional difference below review's clinical-importance threshold. Heterogeneity and trial bias matter. Adverse reporting insufficient to establish comprehensive safety; reported events mostly minor muscle pain. No evidence here for a blanket spinal flexion, rotation, squat or lifting ban.

**LBP-P1 — Aasa et al., 2015 RCT.** DOI [10.2519/jospt.2015.5021](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2015.5021), PMID [25641309](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25641309/). **Abstract.** 70 adults with recurrent mechanical/nociceptive-dominant LBP; low-load motor control versus high-load lifting, both education; 12 sessions/8 weeks. Motor control improved patient-specific activity more (4.2 versus 2.5 points); no significant between-group pain difference. Does not establish lifting as universally unsafe or equivalent for every chronic-primary-LBP phenotype. Detailed adverse/progression data taken from its same-cohort follow-up below, not invented from abstract.

**LBP-P2 — Michaelson et al., 2016 same-cohort RCT follow-up.** DOI [10.2340/16501977-2091](https://doi.org/10.2340/16501977-2091), [journal full text PDF](https://medicaljournalssweden.se/jrm/article/download/15412/19219/56936). **FT.** 35/group; mechanical LBP >3 months; 8-week intervention, 2-,12-,24-month outcomes. No significant between-group pain/disability/QoL differences; no untreated control. Deadlift progression was supervised and contingent on pain before/during/after; no 1RM testing. Two high-load participants reported adverse effects, one withdrew; none reported in low-load arm. Selected population and close technique/progression supervision limit unsupervised generalization. Supports individualized progression and a lower-load alternative; not a rule that all patients must avoid deadlifts or remain neutral in every daily movement.

### Draft implications and gaps

- Ordinary training: preserve activity, tailor tolerable starting demand and progression. Do not encode diagnosis-wide bans on flexion, extension, rotation, axial loading, squats or deadlifts; retrieved studies do not justify them. LBP-P2's neutral deadlift technique is protocol-specific, not proof of harm from all spinal motion.
- Supportive candidates: general progressive strengthening, motor-control work, aerobic exercise; select exact executions only after catalog comparison. No novel identity required solely because an exercise is described as “core.” Stretching remains mobility, not strength-set credit.
- Unresolved: universally safe load/ROM/pain threshold, acute flare algorithms, safe unsupervised high-load dose, subgroup effects, and older-adult transfer. Specific pathology, postoperative restrictions and radicular symptoms cannot inherit this record.
- Currentness search found a 2025 Cochrane overview (DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD014691.pub2; PMC11945228) and a 2026 JAMA clinical review; they were located but not fully extracted. They do not replace direct execution-level evidence. No claim of exhaustive current coverage.

## Lumbar radicular pain

### Retrieved evidence

**LR-G1 — Apeldoorn et al., KNGF guideline, 2024 final.** DOI [10.23736/S1973-9087.24.08352-7](https://doi.org/10.23736/S1973-9087.24.08352-7), PMID [38407016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38407016/), [PMC11112513](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11112513/); **FT via [Europe PMC XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11112513/fullTextXML)**. Adults LBP/lumbosacral radicular syndrome without red flags. Activity/exercise progression depends pain/irritability: avoid increasing pain in high irritability; brief increase may be acceptable in moderate irritability. This is individualized guidance, not a fixed joint angle cap. Immediate physician contact advised for saddle numbness, bowel/bladder changes or increasing leg weakness. Severe neurological cases and recent surgery fall outside guideline scope; that scope limit is not itself a trial-proven contraindication. No universal flexion/extension or lifting exclusion established.

**LR-P1 — Fritz et al., 2021 (online 2020) pragmatic RCT.** DOI [10.7326/M20-4187](https://doi.org/10.7326/M20-4187), PMID [33017565](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33017565/), [PMC7856080](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7856080/). **FT.** 220 adults 18–60, recent sciatica <90 days. Education versus education plus 4-week PT including individualized directional exercise/manual therapy (recommended 6–8 visits; home exercise every 4–5 hours). Disability advantage at 6 months −5.4/100 (95% CI −9.4 to −1.3); at one year −4.8 (−8.9 to −0.7); no health-care-use/work-loss difference. Thirty-seven PT recipients reported 133 effects, commonly back pain/stiffness; 13 effects severe in intensity, seven persisted >24 h. Movements chosen to centralize symptoms; extension often used, never automatically for all. Cannot isolate exercise from manual therapy or establish an unsupervised gym prescription.

**LR-P2 — Luijsterburg et al., 2008 RCT.** DOI [10.1007/s00586-007-0569-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-007-0569-6), PMID [18172697](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18172697/), [PMC2295266](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2295266/). **Abstract/available summary; FT not yet extracted.** Acute sciatica, GP care with versus without added PT, 12-month follow-up. Added PT improved global perceived recovery at 12 months, without significant relevant differences in leg pain, disability, fear or general health; not more cost effective. Included as contrary/qualified evidence: PT benefits depend on outcome and care package, and do not prove particular ordinary exercises harmful. Dose/adverse details unresolved here.

**LR-S1 — Barbari et al., 2024 systematic review.** DOI [10.7759/cureus.57278](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.57278), PMID [38559546](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38559546/), [PMC10981532](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10981532/); **FT via [XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10981532/fullTextXML)**. Two resistance-training RCTs; abstract reports 196 analyzed participants, body 212 recruited. Mixed low-back-related leg pain, including referred pain, not a homogeneous confirmed radiculopathy cohort. One versus two sets showed no clear difference; individual functional restoration versus advice yielded limited/mixed superiority. No serious adverse events reported; optimal dose/long-term superiority uncertain. Cannot turn these mixed-population findings into radiculopathy-specific load ceilings or assume neural “flossing” is equivalent to strength training.

**LR-G2 — Aldera et al., 2026 final Saudi adapted guideline.** DOI [10.3390/jcm15020528](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm15020528), PMID [41598467](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41598467/), [PMC12842004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12842004/); **selected FT sections via [XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12842004/fullTextXML)**. Adults nonspecific LBP/sciatica in Saudi health-system context. Psychological care suggested as part of an exercise-containing package; low/very-low certainty predominates. The inspected sections do not establish execution-level bans. Not used to invent new workout rules; full module-by-module extraction remains a gap.

### Draft implications and gaps

- Ordinary training: high-irritability stage needs clinician-defined tolerable activity/progression; worsening neurological symptoms must stop automated escalation and trigger assessment. Persistent or progressive deficit requires a clinical pathway, not substitution to “fill” a workout. LR-G1 supports this clinical boundary; LR-P1 supports assessed directional response, not diagnosis-only extension assignment.
- Supportive candidates: clinician-selected directional movement, graded general conditioning/strengthening. Directional preference/centralization must be supplied by assessment; unknown is unknown. No fixed flexion or extension prohibition, nerve-tension dosage or heavy-lifting ceiling is established.
- Restriction-only draft is defensible while symptom stage/directional response are unknown. Actual release requires a usable, clinically reviewed rule and compatible catalog execution. No direct comparative primary study retrieved establishes that all squats, deadlifts or stretches worsen lumbar radicular pain.
- Search gap: 2025 chronic lumbar radicular self-management scoping review located (ScienceDirect S2468781225001122), full text not inspected. No dosing inference drawn.

## Lumbar spinal stenosis

### Retrieved evidence

**LSS-G1 — Bussières et al., 2021 final clinical practice guideline.** DOI [10.1016/j.jpain.2021.03.147](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpain.2021.03.147), [author-institution PDF](https://depot-e.uqtr.ca/id/eprint/9766/1/BUSSIERES_A_167_ED.pdf). **Relevant final recommendation text inspected.** Adults LSS causing neurogenic claudication. Conditional/weak recommendation for multimodal nonpharmacologic care: education/advice, behavioral change, home exercise, manual therapy/rehabilitation (moderate-quality evidence). Postoperative rehabilitation is a separate setting/timing. Does not prescribe one universal flexion dose or ban all extension/standing. NASS official completed-guideline page inspected: LSS guideline remains listed under **2011**, not 2025 (the nearby 2025 header belongs to antithrombotics).

**LSS-S1 — Comer et al., 2024 (online 2023) systematic review/component analysis.** DOI [10.1177/02692155231201048](https://doi.org/10.1177/02692155231201048), PMID [37715644](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37715644/), [PMC10829420](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10829420/); **FT via [XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10829420/fullTextXML)**. Thirteen trials, 1440 participants with neurogenic claudication; 23 exercise interventions, heterogeneous comparators. Supervision/flexion common; cycling, strengthening, stretching and psychological components more frequent in successful packages. Flexion itself appeared in both successful and unsuccessful programs. Component co-occurrence is not randomized proof of independent benefit or safety; no reliable supervision/dose conclusion. Supports considering tolerable fitness/strengthening within a multimodal plan, not prohibiting all extension or inferring every stretch is beneficial.

**LSS-P1 — Schneider et al., 2019 RCT.** DOI [10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2018.6828](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2018.6828), [journal FT](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2720073). **FT.** 259 adults ≥60, imaging-confirmed LSS and neurogenic claudication; symptoms relieved by sitting/flexion among diagnostic signs. Six-week medical care, group exercise or manual therapy/individualized exercise; outcomes 2/6 months. Individualized package improved symptom/function scores more at 2 months; no between-group responder differences at 6 months. All groups improved walking. No serious treatment-related events; transient muscle soreness 54% individualized, 31% group exercise, 6% medical; all anticipated events resolved ≤48h. Excluded cauda equina, prior stenosis surgery/fusion and severe competing walking conditions; exclusions limit generalization. Symptom relief supports a tolerability-based posture adaptation, not proof extension damages tissue.

**LSS-P2 — Comer et al., 2013 RCT.** DOI [10.1371/journal.pone.0072878](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0072878), PMID [24098633](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24098633/), [journal FT](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0072878). **FT available text.** Adults ≥50 with walking-limited neurogenic claudication; six-week home flexion/aerobic exercise plus advice versus advice/education. No significant symptom-severity benefit at 8 weeks (adjusted difference 0.06; 95% CI −0.19 to 0.31); no systematic sustained improvement; 12-month outcomes measured. No adverse events reported. Mean three contacts; post hoc severe-symptom subgroup not confirmatory. Important contrary evidence against claiming an app-delivered generic flexion program will reproduce supervised rehabilitation benefits.

**LSS-P3 — Whitman et al., 2006 RCT.** DOI [10.1097/01.brs.0000241136.98159.8c](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.brs.0000241136.98159.8c), PMID [17047542](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17047542/). **Abstract; full PDF located but not fully extracted.** 58 people LSS, six-week manual therapy/body-weight-supported walking/exercise versus flexion/walking/subtherapeutic ultrasound. Recovery better with manual/exercise package (NNT 2.6 at six weeks); 62% versus 41% recovered at one year. Multi-component comparison cannot identify which exercise determines benefit, dose, or contraindications. Detailed harms unresolved.

### Draft implications and gaps

- Ordinary training: for documented extension/standing-provoked claudication, shorten or modify that exposure and consider symptom-relieving supported/flexion-tolerant conditioning; mark this a symptom-based clinical inference (LSS-P1), not a global extension ban. Walking is not automatically excluded; it is an outcome and intervention in these trials.
- Supportive candidates: cycling, individually tolerable trunk/lower-limb strengthening, conditioning. Exact equipment/position and stage remain attributes. No diagnosis-wide numerical flexion angle, standing-duration cap, lifting limit or progression increment is established.
- Neurological deterioration/cauda-equina assessment cannot be replaced by a workout. Coexisting osteoporosis, radiculopathy, balance limitations and postoperative orders retain independent precedence.
- Currentness search found 2026 network meta-analysis PMC13145387 and a 2026 small feasibility RCT DOI 10.3390/app16104695; not fully extracted, no added rule based on them. No newer final exercise-specific guideline verified.

## Nonspecific neck pain

### Retrieved evidence

**NN-G1 — El-Allawy et al., 2025 final German S3 guideline summary.** DOI [10.3238/arztebl.m2025.0119](https://doi.org/10.3238/arztebl.m2025.0119), PMID [40665902](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40665902/), [FT PMC12620902](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12620902/); [official guideline](https://www.degam.de/leitlinie-s3-053-007), published August 2025, valid to February 2030. **FT summary.** Adults without identified structural cause; exercise recommended for chronic >12-week pain, activation/self-management central. Specific exercise modality not established because interventions heterogeneous. Immobilization discouraged; neurological deficit/trauma or structural warning signs require evaluation. No exercise dose, overhead lifting cap or cervical ROM prohibition established. Summary explicitly says these consensus-evaluated S3 guidelines are not separately journal peer reviewed; preserve distinction from peer-reviewed primary studies.

**NN-S1 — Mueller et al., 2023 systematic review/meta-analysis.** DOI [10.2519/jospt.2023.11820](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2023.11820), PMID [37339388](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37339388/). **Abstract and publisher results text.** 68 trials chronic nonspecific neck pain; resistance, motor-control and mindfulness-based exercise versus controls/other exercises. Benefit for pain/disability, very-low to moderate certainty; substantial heterogeneity (many I² values >90%). No resistance-exercise dose-response relationship demonstrated. Modality rankings do not prove alternatives dangerous or provide safe loads for individual users. Full protocol and harms extraction remains incomplete.

**NN-P1 — Ylinen et al., 2003 RCT.** DOI [10.1001/jama.289.19.2509](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.289.19.2509), PMID [12759322](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12759322/), [journal FT](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/196580). **FT.** 180 female office workers with chronic nonspecific neck pain. Twelve-month isometric strength/dynamic endurance programs versus stretching/aerobic advice; supervised introductory rehabilitation then home practice encouraged three times weekly. Both active programs reduced pain/disability more; included upper-body dumbbell presses, rows and shrugs as part of package. Excluded major structural/radicular/traumatic/postoperative disease. Systematic adverse-event reporting not identified. Selected supervised population and mixed package prevent assuming every press/shrug is safe for every neck disorder; equally, findings undermine an evidence-free diagnosis-wide ban on all upper-body resistance work.

### Draft implications and gaps

- Ordinary training: activity and individualized progression compatible with assessed nonspecific neck pain; do not apply cervical-radicular, myelopathic, trauma or instability restrictions indiscriminately. No universal overhead press, shrug, neck extension/rotation or strength-training prohibition supported by retrieved evidence.
- Supportive candidates: neck motor-control/isometric endurance and scapular/upper-body strengthening, with exact execution/dose reviewed independently. A neck bridge is not interchangeable with low-load cervical isometrics.
- No validated numerical pain cutoff, load ceiling, ROM threshold or automatic progression rule extracted. Detailed adverse surveillance is a major gap.
- Newer 2025–2026 network analyses were located (Spine Journal S1529943025003444; PMC13401843; PMC13112673) but not used to infer personal “optimal” doses. Their rankings/study-level dose curves do not establish app starting loads.

## Cervical radicular pain

### Retrieved evidence

**CR-G1 — Dutch NVvN cervical radicular syndrome guideline, final July 24, 2024.** [Exercise module](https://richtlijnendatabase.nl/richtlijn/cervicaal_radiculair_syndroom/conservatieve_therapie_bij_rl_crs/fysiotherapie_bij_rl_crs/oefentherapie_bij_rl_crs.html). **FT module.** Consider targeted exercise with a suitably experienced therapist; low-certainty pain/disability evidence, very-low/absent evidence for several outcomes/harms. Initial reactive phase prioritizes advice to avoid aggravation; subacute active approach respects individual capacity; later self-management. This clinical stage advice does not yield a fixed extension/rotation or lifting ban. Evidence synthesis finds little/no additional disability benefit over wait-and-see or other exercise. Unknown stage should not become an invented “approved” neck-strengthening routine.

**CR-P1 — Kuijper et al., 2009 RCT.** DOI [10.1136/bmj.b3883](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.b3883), [BMJ full article](https://www.bmj.com/content/339/bmj.b3883). **Substantive full article text available in search result; direct open subsequently 403.** 205 recent-onset (<1 month) cervical radiculopathy patients; collar/rest, PT/home exercises, or wait-and-see; six-week treatment, six-month follow-up. Hands-off graded mobilizing/stabilizing/strengthening PT twice weekly. Arm/neck pain reduction over six weeks favored collar or PT (~12–17 mm/100 difference); little/no persistent between-group difference by six months. Adverse-event outcome absent per CR-G1's synthesis. Collar/rest arm's short-term efficacy does not justify app-imposed immobilization, especially when applying a different neck-pain diagnosis or recovery stage.

**CR-P2 — Dedering et al., 2018 RCT.** DOI [10.1016/j.apmr.2018.06.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2018.06.008), PMID [30473018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30473018/). **Abstract; protocol details additionally inspected in CR-G1 and CR-S1, not primary FT.** 144 confirmed CR patients; three-month neck-specific training versus prescribed general physical activity; both behavioral approach, outcomes 3/6/12/24 months. No significant group or group×time effects; both improved. Guideline describes progression from gentle isometrics to low-load endurance, response-tailored, three sessions/week; comparator aerobic/muscular activity ~30 min three times/week. Primary detailed harms unavailable. Supports several possible active pathways, not superiority of one specific execution or a blanket general-activity ban.

**CR-S1 — Mallard et al., 2022 updated OPTIMa review.** DOI [10.2340/jrm.v54.2799](https://doi.org/10.2340/jrm.v54.2799), PMID [35797062](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35797062/), [FT PMC9422871](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9422871/). **FT relevant synthesis.** Four acceptable RCTs and one low-quality RCT, adults recent/any-duration radiculopathy; multimodal rehabilitation compared with traction, general activity, injection or surgery-containing care. Benefits generally small/trivial and low certainty; safety evidence lacking. Review's short-term pairwise signal favoring multimodal rehabilitation is compatible with the original trial's nonsignificant overall group/time model; do not present as decisive superiority.

**CR-S2 — Núñez de Arenas-Arroyo et al., 2025 component network meta-analysis.** DOI [10.1177/02692155251365193](https://doi.org/10.1177/02692155251365193), [publisher abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/02692155251365193). **Abstract.** Search to July 1, 2025; 36 RCTs, 25 interventions, eight active components. Neurodynamic/articular/traction-containing combinations and strengthening appeared promising for pain, moderate confidence for selected combinations. Component network assumptions, heterogeneous packages and short-term pain focus do not establish exercise contraindications or permit self-directed traction/manipulation/dry needling. Detailed adverse-event and dose extraction unavailable.

### Draft implications and gaps

- Ordinary training: when nerve-root irritability is active, alter demand/position according to assessed symptom response and capacity; this is clinical-guidance inference (CR-G1), not proof overhead lifting or all cervical extension is intrinsically harmful. No numeric cervical ROM/load ceiling established.
- Supportive candidates: clinician-guided low-load neck control/endurance; general physical activity can be a candidate, not automatically contraindicated. Neurodynamic tensioning/sliding are clinically distinct and cannot be generated from generic stretches.
- Restriction-only/research draft until diagnosis, neurological status, stage and clinically reviewed thresholds are known. Progressive deficit or possible myelopathy requires assessment, not automated progression. Specific urgent criteria need a separately approved clinical pathway rather than being reverse-engineered from trial exclusions.
- No evidence retrieved to authorize postoperative exercise; prior surgery is an attribute requiring its own orders. NASS 2010 is older; 2024 Dutch and 2025 synthesis used for currentness.

## Whiplash-associated disorder

### Retrieved evidence

**WAD-G1 — SIRA, final third edition 2014 acute WAD guideline.** [Official landing page, updated Dec 19 2024](https://www.sira.nsw.gov.au/resources-library/treatment-advice-centre/acute-whiplash/whiplash-guidelines); [final PDF](https://www.sira.nsw.gov.au/resources-library/motor-accident-resources/publications/for-professionals/whiplash-resources/SIRA08104-Whiplash-Guidelines-1117-396479.pdf). **FT relevant sections/appendix.** Adults first 12 weeks; advises activity and neck ROM/low-load isometric/postural endurance/strengthening exercise. Appendix cautions exercises should not cause severe pain and higher strengthening examples start later in recovery with treating-professional guidance. Example holds are source-specific, not generic five-second progression. Online fourth-edition document explicitly titled **Draft**; not adopted here as current final guidance. No diagnosis-wide lifelong lifting or movement prohibition.

**WAD-P1 — Ludvigsson et al., 2015 RCT (online 2014).** DOI [10.1097/AJP.0000000000000123](https://doi.org/10.1097/AJP.0000000000000123), PMID [24918474](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24918474/), [PMC4352069](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4352069/); **FT via [XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4352069/fullTextXML)**. 216 adults chronic WAD grades II/III. Twelve weeks neck-specific exercise (NSE), NSE plus behavioral approach, or prescribed general activity; two supervised visits/week plus home exercise. NSE progressed gentle isometrics to resistance/endurance, symptom/capacity tailored. NSE arms had greater disability improvement at 3/6 months; no significant difference between NSE arms. One arm avoided pain aggravation; behavioral arm deemphasized temporary increases. Adverse-event section not found. Thus no evidence for one universal zero-pain rule, mandatory grade-III exclusion, or unrestricted pain-pushing; clinician monitoring is material.

**WAD-P2 — Michaleff et al., 2014 PROMISE RCT.** DOI [10.1016/S0140-6736(14)60457-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(14)60457-8), [full article PDF mirror](https://www.lsft.fi/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Comprehensive-physiotherapy-exercise-programme-or-advice-for-chronic-whiplash-PROMISE-a-pragmatic-randomised-controlled-trial.pdf). **FT.** 172 adults 18–65, WAD I/II, 3 months–5 years; comprehensive 20-session PT exercise versus one advice session/phone support. At 14 weeks pain difference 0.0/10 (95% CI −0.7 to 0.7); no advantage at 6/12 months. No serious adverse events recorded. Nerve-root compromise/grade III excluded, limiting scope; not proof grade III exercise is unsafe (WAD-P1 studied it). Contrary evidence prevents promising that more sessions or complexity necessarily improves outcomes.

**WAD-P3 — Malfliet et al., 2025 RCT.** DOI [10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.26674](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.26674), PMID [40794407](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40794407/), [PMC12344539](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12344539/). **Abstract and relevant results/full PDF excerpts.** 120 adults 18–65 chronic WAD; pain-science/stress-management/cognition-targeted exercise versus usual physiotherapy. Prespecified six-month disability endpoint negative (difference 2.38/50; 95% CI −0.12 to 4.89); secondary immediate/12-month differences favored intervention. Other pain/QoL differences absent. Full harms/dose not extracted; cannot use this to mandate a universal pain-response progression model.

**WAD-S1 — Correia et al., 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis.** DOI [10.3390/muscles4040049](https://doi.org/10.3390/muscles4040049), PMID [41283437](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41283437/), PMC12641875. **Abstract only; PMC challenge/MDPI rate-limit prevented FT.** Thirteen reports, 2427 participants 18–63, neck-specific exercise ± behavioral approach; quantitative pooling found no clear between-approach pain/disability advantage at follow-up despite some individual positive reports. Need check overlapping cohorts before treating participant total as independent unique patients. Does not establish acute WAD advice or a prohibition against ordinary exercise; harms/dose unresolved.

### Draft implications and gaps

- Ordinary training: acute trauma/uncleared fracture or instability is not a generic workout state. Once assessed, recovery-stage and symptom-response modifications can allow activity; later strengthening cannot be scheduled solely from an app's elapsed time. WAD-G1 supports severe-pain avoidance in acute examples; chronic trial arms use different pain-response approaches.
- Supportive candidates: gentle cervical ROM, low-load cervical isometrics, neck endurance/control, staged scapular/general conditioning. Exact grade, stage, neurologic status and execution matter; never substitute neck bridges or externally loaded cervical exercises for gentle isometrics.
- No universal numeric ROM, external-load ceiling, recurrence threshold or automated dose established. Restriction-only draft while stage/clearance unknown; no fabricated “clinician approved” status.

## Adult degenerative scoliosis

### Retrieved evidence

**ADS-S1 — Schoutens et al., 2020 (online 2019) systematic review.** DOI [10.1093/pm/pnz253](https://doi.org/10.1093/pm/pnz253), PMID [31617915](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31617915/). **Abstract; institutional FT PDF located but direct fetch 403.** Symptomatic degenerative scoliosis ≥50, nonsurgical interventions, follow-up ≥6 weeks; six studies. Injection evidence low; yoga/bracing very low; no specific PT evidence found in review period. Cannot advise for/against these approaches from available evidence. This is uncertainty, not a contraindication against exercise. No condition-specific ordinary-training load/ROM ban supported.

**ADS-P1 — Bayram et al., 2024 prospective uncontrolled cohort.** DOI [10.26574/maedica.2024.19.1.23](https://doi.org/10.26574/maedica.2024.19.1.23), PMID [38736938](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38736938/), [FT PMC11079735](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11079735/). **FT.** Forty nonsurgical thoracolumbar ADS participants enrolled, 30 women completed. Combined modalities (heat/TENS/ultrasound) plus stretching, strengthening, stabilization, posture/balance/coordination, 15 sessions/3 weeks, 60 min/day five days/week in the PT unit. Methods p. 25 says one patient continued a home program; Discussion p. 27 reports patients continuing one hour/day at least three days/week. Both descriptions are retained as an unresolved participation discrepancy. The Discussion schedule is reported home prescription, not measured adherence; actual home participation/adherence was not established. Pain/disability/QoL improved at 1/3 months. No control, ten dropouts, multiple cointerventions; no causal exercise-specific effect or long-term curve effect established. Detailed adverse-event surveillance not identified; health problems among attrition reasons cannot be assigned to treatment. Not a proof that every listed exercise is safe for all curves.

**ADS-P2 — Fishman et al., 2014 case series.** DOI [10.7453/gahmj.2013.064](https://doi.org/10.7453/gahmj.2013.064), PMID [25568820](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25568820/), [PMC4268609](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4268609/). **Abstract and available methods/results snippets; FT browser challenged.** Twenty-five mixed idiopathic/degenerative scoliosis cases, curves 6–120°, side plank with convex side down, daily practice, average follow-up 6.8 months. Curve reductions reported among adherers; no randomized/control group, mixed age/etiology and adherence selection. No defensible universal one-sided plank prescription, side assignment or curve-correction promise. Actual degenerative subgroup/adverse details require full extraction before considering a therapeutic mapping.

**ADS-P3 — Monticone et al., 2016 RCT, deliberately indirect.** DOI [10.1007/s00586-016-4528-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-016-4528-y), PMID [27015689](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27015689/). **Abstract.** 130 adults with **idiopathic**, lower-progression-risk scoliosis, main curve <35°; 20-week active self-correction/task exercises/CBT versus general mobilization/stretching/strengthening. Favorable disability outcome described, but this is not a degenerative-scoliosis RCT. Do not transfer its curve threshold, dose, side-specific correction or outcomes into ADS as direct evidence. Full harms unavailable.

**ADS-R1 — Zhao et al., 2026 narrative review (not guideline).** DOI [10.3389/fsurg.2026.1807406](https://doi.org/10.3389/fsurg.2026.1807406), PMID [42518838](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42518838/), [publisher text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/surgery/articles/10.3389/fsurg.2026.1807406/full). **Relevant text inspected.** Discusses individualized rehabilitation by curve pattern, balance, stenosis phenotype/comorbidity; high-quality protocol trials still needed. This is background/expert synthesis, not direct proof of extensor strengthening or cycling for every ADS patient and not a current final exercise CPG.

### Draft implications and gaps

- No automatic scoliosis-wide ban on rotation, side bending, axial load, extension or asymmetric exercise is defensible from these retrieved studies. Likewise, no evidence-based number can be assigned to a safe load/ROM limit.
- Ordinary-training modifications must follow actual coexisting stenosis/radicular symptoms, osteoporosis/fracture risk, balance, surgery and clinician orders. Those conditions retain precedence; curve side alone cannot determine a universal “safe” side.
- Supportive exercise candidates remain research-only individualized trunk/hip strength, conditioning and balance; no execution-level therapeutic mapping approved. ADS may remain restriction-only/unresolved pending specific clinical planning rules. A primary exercise study is recorded even if no exercise is added.
- Current final ADS-specific exercise guideline not verified after targeted searches. Do not substitute adolescent idiopathic scoliosis guidelines or the adult idiopathic RCT. Long-term deformity progression, neurologic adverse outcomes and strength-training restrictions remain major gaps.

## Cross-condition disposition and search limits

Every condition has a studied evidence trail above; none is app-supported by this scratch report. No source establishes a blanket gym exclusion list that can safely ignore symptom stage, coexisting conditions, surgeon orders or individual joint limits. No trial exclusion was promoted to a universal contraindication. No inferred restriction has an invented numerical threshold. Pain-response rules from different populations are not interchangeable.

Searches included official WHO, KNGF/peer-reviewed publication, DEGAM/AWMF, Dutch Richtlijnendatabase, SIRA and NASS sources; PubMed/PMC and journal/repository primary articles; systematic reviews and contrary RCTs. Newer papers were actively sought to November 2026 context. Coverage is targeted and English-access dominated; no database-complete, duplicate-screened systematic search performed. Some sources remain abstract-only/partial, detailed primary intervention supplements were not all retrieved, and older primary trials may not represent app users with multimorbidity. These limitations are explicit release gaps, not reasons to fabricate rules.

The seven structured dossiers are linked from the [condition inventory](../summary.md); their nine candidate variations are in the [exercise request register](../exercise-requests.json). Catalog metadata was inspected; no exact execution match or artwork provenance was accepted. Execution gaps and retained-PNG requirements remain in those records. Research does not change app behavior.
