# Piriformis syndrome: evidence extraction

Draft research for workout generation. The doses below describe studies; they are not a personal exercise prescription or released app defaults. The selected diagnosis is not interchangeable with every cause of buttock pain or deep gluteal syndrome.

## Population and scope

- **population:** Adults with clinically assessed piriformis syndrome; study-specific populations retained per source.
- **stage:** Conservative care candidates; severity and symptom duration insufficiently characterized.
- **operativeStatus:** No postoperative protocol extracted; do not infer operative clearance.
- **exclusions:** Do not apply to unassessed sciatica-like symptoms or other diagnoses by label alone. Trial exclusions are applicability limits, not universal contraindications.
- **uncertainty:** A condition label does not select an individual stretch, dose or range. No clinical reviewer is identified.

## A controlled clinical trial investigating the effects of stretching and compression exercises on electromyography of calf muscles in chronic LBP patients with a deep gluteal syndrome

[Source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-023-00802-4) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-023-00802-4) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38200475/)

**Access:** full-text, 2026-11-01. Publisher HTML and 11-page PDF inspected: https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13102-023-00802-4.pdf . Initial figure retrieval failed; coordinator subsequently retrieved the linked Figure2 JPEG and visually confirmed the wall-supported crossed-ankle pose. No numeric angle or clinical suitability inferred from the photograph.
**Publication:** final journal article

- **design:** Assessor-blind randomized three-group trial; 45 participants, 15/group; retrospectively registered.
- **population:** Adults 18–60 with low-back pain, positive FAIR and piriformis tenderness. Other lumbar/hip pathologies and recent hip/knee injuries excluded; operative status not reported.
- **comparator:** Foam-roller compression or no added exercise.
- **coInterventions:** All groups: heat 15 min and TENS 15 min.
- **outcomes:** No between-group pain or calf sEMG benefit. Disability favored stretching versus compression: MD −12.62, 95% CI −20.41 to −4.38, p=0.009.
- **followUp:** Pre/post ten treatment sessions; later follow-up not reported.
- **harms:** not reported; no loss to follow-up is not a harms assessment.
- **uncertainty:** Small groups; participants unblinded; sex imbalance; broad DGS label. Narrative conclusion broader than its explicit pairwise result; do not assert superiority versus control.
- **limitations:** Indirect for piriformis-only diagnosis; multimodal supervised care does not establish a self-directed app routine or universal contraindications.

**Protocol extraction:**

- **execution:** Supine, unaffected foot on wall; affected ankle above opposite kneecap; arms alongside, palms down; slight stretch.
- **dose:** 3 sets × 2 minutes; 2 minutes rest between sets.
- **frequency:** 3 sessions/week, 10 sessions total.
- **supervision:** Experienced physiotherapist monitored execution.
- **progression:** not reported
- **stoppingRules:** not reported

**Source locations:** Methods > Participants (PDF pp. 2–3); Methods > Interventions > Stretching exercise; Figure 2 (PDF pp. 3–4); Results > Secondary outcomes > Pain and Disability; Table 3 (PDF pp. 7–8); Limitation of study (PDF p. 10)

**Integrity check:** Publisher/PubMed record inspection and DOI + correction/retraction web search; later evidence search. Not a registry-wide clearance. ## Evaluation of the Efficacy of Ultrasound-Guided Dry Needling Therapy and Exercise in Piriformis Muscle Syndrome

[Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10508642/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.43804) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37731410/)

**Access:** full-text, 2026-11-01. PMC open timed out; indexed PMC full-text sections and complete publisher seven-page PDF inspected: https://assets.cureus.com/uploads/original_article/pdf/178201/20230820-28244-6wx7st.pdf . Exercise handout not attached.
**Publication:** final journal article

- **design:** Prospective observational, nonrandomized; 44 adults, 22/group; allocation by accepting dry needling.
- **population:** Adults 18–70 with clinically assessed unilateral piriformis muscle syndrome after alternative hip/lumbar/sacroiliac causes excluded; pain persisted after oral treatment. Stage and operative status not reported.
- **comparator:** Ultrasound-guided dry needling weekly × 3.
- **coInterventions:** Exercise arm: stretching, bilateral bridging, flexed hip/knee feet-together leg raises, posture/activity advice; medication use during follow-up not reported.
- **outcomes:** Within-group improvement in VAS, ODI, LEFS and DN4; no significant between-group follow-up differences. No untreated comparator or isolated component test.
- **followUp:** 1 and 3 months.
- **harms:** No adverse effects stated for needling arm; exercise-specific harms not reported.
- **uncertainty:** Confounding, sex imbalance, no untreated control. Baseline ODI direction conflicts between narrative and Table 2; do not resolve by guessing.
- **limitations:** Bundled program cannot establish independent benefit of a stretch or bridge; exclusion criteria are not universal exercise prohibitions.

**Protocol extraction:**

- **execution:** Static self-stretch position unspecified; bilateral bridging; side leg raises with hip/knee flexion 45 degrees, feet together.
- **dose:** Stretch hold 30 seconds; wording: 10 repetitions/sets. Bridge/leg-raise dose not reported.
- **frequency:** Stretch twice daily; exercise program 3 weeks.
- **supervision:** Home program taught by therapist with image handout; ongoing supervision not reported.
- **progression:** not reported
- **stoppingRules:** not reported

**Source locations:** Materials and methods (PDF p. 2); Materials and methods > Exercise protocol (PDF p. 2); Results > Table 2 (PDF p. 4); Discussion > adverse effects and limitations (PDF pp. 5–6)
