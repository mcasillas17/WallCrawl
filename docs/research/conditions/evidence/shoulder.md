# Shoulder source extraction — Task 2 research draft

Access date: 2026-11-01 (local; session crossed into 2026-11-02 UTC). Scope: the six approved shoulder inventory diagnoses only. This is a targeted evidence review, not an exhaustive systematic review, a regimen, or clinical approval. No app restrictions are released by this report. Canonical JSON awaits controller schema.

Search method: web searches combining each diagnosis with current clinical practice guideline, systematic review, rehabilitation, exercise, and randomized trial; primary PubMed/PMC, publisher, professional-society sources checked. Where PMC browser access failed, Europe PMC full-text XML was retrieved for the same PMCID. Dates below are publication dates, not search-engine relative-date labels. Source summaries deliberately distinguish author conclusions from proposed app inferences. Trial exclusion is an applicability limit, never evidence of contraindication.

## 1. Rotator cuff tendinopathy

### RCT-G: Desmeules et al., 2025 final clinical practice guideline

- Citation/access: *JOSPT* 55:235–274; DOI [10.2519/jospt.2025.13182](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2025.13182); PMID [40165544](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40165544/). Final full publisher text inspected; society [PDF](https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/Rotator_Cuff_CPG.pdf) located.
- Population: adults with suspected/confirmed tendinopathy, with/without calcification and partial-thickness tears. This scope does not establish safety for full-thickness tears or postoperative repair.
- Findings: initial active rehabilitation can include motor control and resistance training at varied loads; individualized education/activity modification; return to sport considers capacity/load tolerance. No single best frequency, load, exercise mode or duration established. Supervision is not consistently superior to a home program. Adverse-event incidence is not a safety clearance for arbitrary gym exercises.
- Inference: support a clinician-tailored active-exercise candidate pathway; individual adjustment of provoking load/range is reasonable. This guideline does not establish a diagnosis-wide overhead, bench-press, internal-rotation or stretching ban, nor a universal kilogram/degree/pain cutoff.

### RCT-S: Lafrance et al., 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis

- Citation/access: [10.2519/jospt.2024.12453](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2024.12453), *The Efficacy of Exercise Therapy for Rotator Cuff–Related Shoulder Pain According to the FITT Principle*. Publisher abstract/results inspected; final [PDF](https://asht.org/sites/asht/files/images/Research/Research%20Updates/2025/lafrance-et-al-2024-the-efficacy-of-exercise-therapy-for-rotator-cuff-related-shoulder-pain-according-to-the-fitt.pdf) located.
- 22 RCTs/1281 adults with RCRSP. Moderate-certainty motor-control versus nonspecific exercise evidence: small disability differences short term (SMD −0.29) and medium term (−0.33); short-term pain difference uncertain. Other modes and intensity low/very-low certainty; no comparisons of different frequency or time. Program tailoring/progression may explain differences. Harms inadequately resolved in inspected material.
- Inference: no evidence-based requirement that every user receive one specific mode or high-load progression; no proof that general exercises are harmful.

### RCT-P1: Ingwersen et al., RoCTEx, 2017 RCT

- Citation/access: [10.1177/2325967117723292](https://doi.org/10.1177/2325967117723292); PMID [28875153](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28875153/); [PMC5576542](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5576542/). Final full-text XML and exercise appendices inspected.
- 100 adults 18–65, symptoms ≥3 months, ultrasound tendinopathy. Full-thickness tear, active elevation <90°, resting VAS >40/100 excluded: not contraindications established by this experiment.
- Both arms: scapular/cuff/mobility exercises, home 3/week, six therapist contacts. High-load cuff work progressed 15RM→6RM over 12 weeks versus 20–25RM. Exact studied candidates: standing dumbbell scaption, side-lying dumbbell external rotation; also supine scapular protraction and band shoulder extension. Program pain guidance capped at 5/10, recovery within 30 minutes, no worsening following days; modify range/load, isometric option when needed. This is a trial protocol, not a validated universal damage threshold.
- DASH between-group difference −1.37 (95% CI −6.72 to 3.99), no high-load superiority. Three referred for surgery with worsening; no significant group difference in increased-pain/strength-loss episodes. Cuff loading can be a candidate; a trial supports neither mandatory heavy training nor blanket avoidance.

### RCT-P2: Hopewell et al., GRASP, 2021 RCT

- Citation/access: [10.1016/S0140-6736(21)00846-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(21)00846-1); PMID [34265255](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34265255/); [PMC8343092](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8343092/). Final full-text XML inspected. Detailed intervention report [10.3310/hta25480](https://doi.org/10.3310/hta25480), [intervention chapter](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK572851/) located but browser challenge blocked that chapter.
- 708 adults, episode ≤6 months, cuff-related disorder; significant trauma, instability/frozen shoulder and surgery candidates excluded. Up to six progressive-physiotherapy sessions over 16 weeks versus one advice session, both with home exercise, factorial injection comparison.
- SPADI over 12 months: progressive versus advice adjusted difference −0.66 (99% CI −4.52 to 3.20); no serious adverse events reported. Both exercise-supported arms improved. Advice is not an inactive comparator.
- Inference: supervised progression not necessarily superior to well-taught self-management. Does not validate a generic autonomous workout, all tear subtypes, or exact substitutions.

### RCT-S2: Lazzarini et al., 2026 network meta-analysis

- Citation/access: [10.1136/bjsports-2025-110024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2025-110024), final publisher abstract/results/discussion inspected.
- 89 RCTs/5532 adults, search through March 2024. Shoulder exercise versus no treatment may improve pain/function, but very-low-certainty evidence. Most adjuncts not clearly superior to shoulder exercise; rankings rely on few small studies and indirect comparisons. Long-term and adverse-event evidence limited.
- Inference: strengthens uncertainty around precise exercise superiority; do not turn a ranking into a mandatory exercise or ban alternatives.

### Draft disposition and gaps

Therapeutic candidates exist; implementation remains draft/requires clinical review and exact execution matching. RoCTEx scaption and side-lying external rotation are sufficiently described to request exact variants, but no catalog ID has been claimed. No universal overhead restriction is supported. Pain-response monitoring is protocol-informed; numeric thresholds/progression need explicit review rather than automatic copying. No therapeutic stretching dose is established across all tendinopathy presentations.

## 2. Full-thickness rotator cuff tear

### FTT-G: AAOS Management of Rotator Cuff Injuries, 2025 final guideline

- Citation/access: published/adopted 2025-08-18; [final guideline](https://www.aaos.org/rccpg2025), full PDF inspected. No DOI/PMID for this document verified.
- Strong recommendations: physical therapy and surgery both improve outcomes in symptomatic small/medium full-thickness tears; with nonoperative treatment, tear size, atrophy and fatty infiltration can progress over 5–10 years. Healed repairs may outperform PT/unhealed repairs.
- Postoperative early/delayed mobilization findings concern selected small/medium repairs and are not permission for unrestricted resistance exercise. Large/massive/unrepairable tears have a different evidence base.
- Inference: require known operative status, tear extent/clinical plan before applying generic progression. Broadly prohibiting all strengthening is contradicted by nonoperative studies, while symptom improvement cannot certify tendon healing. This review does not establish a safe universal weight/ROM cap or automatic return-to-lifting date.

### FTT-P1: Kuhn/MOON et al., 2013 prospective cohort

- Citation/access: [10.1016/j.jse.2013.01.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jse.2013.01.026); PMID [23540577](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23540577/); [PMC3748251](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3748251/). Full accepted manuscript inspected; final publication identified.
- Adults with MRI-confirmed symptomatic atraumatic full-thickness tears; injury-related symptoms, prior surgery, GH arthritis and adhesive capsulitis excluded. 452 enrolled; follow-up report included 422. No randomized comparator.
- Daily ROM/flexibility plus cuff/scapular strengthening 3/week, reassessment at 6 and 12 weeks. Approximately 75% avoided surgery at 2 years; surgery election defined failure and is influenced by preferences. Specific adverse-event surveillance insufficiently detailed in inspected text.
- Inference: selected atraumatic tears can improve during tailored exercise. Does not generalize to acute traumatic tears, postoperative healing, combined pathology, or prove all ordinary exercises safe. Exact therapeutic exercise variant/dose requires supplementary-protocol review before mapping.

### FTT-P2: Kuhn/MOON et al., 2024 ten-year follow-up

- Citation/access: [10.2106/JBJS.23.00978](https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.23.00978); PMID [38980920](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38980920/). Citation and abstract-level author/institution record inspected; full report not retrieved. Same atraumatic cohort, not independent replication.
- Outcome scores in nonoperated participants improved and did not decline over follow-up. Surgery predictors changed over time. Structural healing and per-exercise safety cannot be inferred from this abstract; detailed dose/adverse-event extraction unavailable here.
- Inference: durability of reported function is compatible with nonoperative care for selected patients but cannot replace the conflicting randomized structural/long-term data below.

### FTT-P3: Moosmayer et al., 2024 randomized 15-year follow-up

- Citation/access: [10.2106/JBJS.24.00065](https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.24.00065); PMID [39197154](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39197154/); [PMC11594065](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11594065/). Final full-text XML inspected.
- 103 small/medium full-thickness tears ≤3 cm; includes gradual onset and acute-on-chronic/minor-trauma presentations, not every acute traumatic tear. Repair versus tailored PT with optional secondary repair. PT 40 minutes twice weekly first 12 weeks, tapering next 6–12 weeks; not a standalone exercise comparison.
- At 15 years 83 assessed, 15/51 PT patients crossed to surgery. Repair favored Constant by 11.8 points and ASES by 13.9. Among 26 unrepaired tears, mean AP size increased 16.2→31.6 mm. Retears occurred after surgery; structural follow-up and additional treatments reported.
- Inference: retain long-term progression uncertainty even if pain improves. Does not show a particular gym exercise caused tear enlargement or establish a safe load cutoff. Its postoperative schedule is a trial regimen, not a universally applicable repair protocol.

### Draft disposition and gaps

Restricted/manual-review candidate, with nonoperative therapeutic possibilities. Acute traumatic, repaired, massive/unrepairable and chronic small/medium atraumatic status must remain separate attributes. No autonomously prescribed strengthening or stretching regimen justified for undifferentiated full-thickness tear. Hold generic upper-body progression pending appropriate clinical plan is an app-scope safeguard inferred from heterogeneous populations, not an RCT-proven contraindication. Numeric restrictions and exact substitutions unresolved. A blanket shoulder-exercise exclusion would overstate evidence.

## 3. Adhesive capsulitis

### AC-G1: Lee et al., 2025 primary frozen shoulder guideline

- Citation/access: [10.5535/arm.250057](https://doi.org/10.5535/arm.250057); PMID [40602400](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40602400/); [PMC12231400](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12231400/). Final full-text/Europe PMC XML inspected.
- Primary frozen shoulder, not postoperative or all secondary stiffness. ROM/manual therapy may help; self-stretching evidence insufficient (no eligible isolated self-stretching controlled study), expert-consensus education/adherence advice. Strengthening very-low-certainty, clinician discretion: two small heterogeneous RCTs, pooled pain MD −1.02, I² 97%; ROM differences not established.
- Inference: supports individualized mobility/possible strengthening candidates, not mandatory stretch dose or generic five-second progression. No evidence that strength credit belongs to stretching. Stage/irritability and clinical assessment needed; generic shoulder limits cannot be assumed safe.

### AC-G2: Kelley et al., 2013 guideline

- Citation/access: [10.2519/jospt.2013.0302](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2013.0302); PMID [23636125](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23636125/); final [PDF mirror](https://www.pt.or.th/PTCouncil/download/cpg/M_05.pdf) inspected after initial mirror text-search timeouts. Older guideline retained for irritability framework, not called the newest.
- Recommends matching stretching intensity to tissue irritability rather than fixed progression. High-irritability mobility is pain-free passive/assisted ROM; moderate-irritability stretching is gentle/moderate without sustained post-treatment symptom aggravation. Low-irritability work can progress into resistance. These are clinical classifications, not an app diagnostic algorithm; no table-derived numeric dose imported here.
- Inference: avoid forcing painful end-range in a clinically identified highly irritable presentation is consistent with this framework. Do not convert limited ROM into a requirement to force symmetry.

### AC-P1: Diercks & Stevens, 2004 prospective successive-cohort study

- Citation/access: [10.1016/j.jse.2004.03.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jse.2004.03.002); PMID [15383804](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15383804/); final [full PDF](https://upload.orthobullets.com/journalclub/free_pdf/15383804.pdf) inspected.
- 77 idiopathic frozen shoulders with >50% GH restriction ≥3 months. Despite wording about random groups, methods/discussion show successive temporal cohorts: 45 supportive education/pain-limited pendulum and active movement versus 32 intensive passive stretching/mobilization and active exercise beyond pain threshold. Not a randomized trial; confounding/time effects possible.
- At 24 months Constant ≥80: 89% versus 63%, favoring pain-limited group. No quantified exercise-injury rate; author's proposed inflammatory mechanism is a hypothesis, not demonstrated harm mechanism.
- Inference: evidence against assuming aggressive painful stretching improves this population; cautious pain-limited mobility candidate. It does not prove every painful repetition causes damage, nor generalize to all stages/secondary/postoperative capsulitis. Pendulum/active-movement frequency and hold time not established as a transferable prescription.

### AC-P2: Rawat et al., 2017 RCT

- Citation/access: [10.1016/j.jht.2016.10.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jht.2016.10.007); PMID [27884497](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27884497/). Published final abstract inspected; full text/detailed exercise dose unavailable.
- 42 patients: TENS/joint mobilization versus same plus rotator cuff strengthening, 12 sessions/4 weeks. SPADI favored added strengthening (34.66 versus 54.29 at end); pain/function/ROM reported improved. Abstract pain figures appear internally unclear, so not reused numerically. Stage, exact variants, loads and adverse events not adequately available.
- Inference: strengthening candidate only, not a mapped default exercise or forced painful end-range rule. Other components prevent attributing all change to an isolated exercise.

### AC-P3: UK FROST, Rangan et al., 2020 RCT

- Citation/access: published final trial [author repository](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/161540/), [PMC7750869 trial report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7750869/); abstract/report results inspected. DOI [10.1016/S0140-6736(20)31965-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(20)31965-6).
- 503 secondary-care primary frozen-shoulder patients; early structured PT with graduated home exercise and steroid versus manipulation under anesthesia or arthroscopic release (with subsequent PT). No clinically important superiority at 12 months; surgical adverse events occurred. This tests whole treatment packages, not stretch dose or ordinary gym safety.
- Inference: conservative-care option is credible; no permission for autonomous generic shoulder loading or claim that every patient fully recovers without follow-up.

### Draft disposition and gaps

Pain-limited mobility candidates and possible reviewed cuff strengthening; exact variants/doses unresolved. Irritability-driven range/load modification is supported more than fixed-degree restrictions. Avoid automatic escalation of stretch duration/range in painful flare; this is a cautious synthesis of AC-G1/G2 and AC-P1. Stage calendar alone is inadequate. No universal overhead ban, no five-second stretch progression, and no strength-set credit for mobility.

## 4. Glenohumeral osteoarthritis

### GHOA-G: Michener et al., APTA 2023 final guideline

- Citation/access: [10.1093/ptj/pzad041](https://doi.org/10.1093/ptj/pzad041); [PMC10256270](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10256270/). Final full-text XML inspected (publisher browser retrieval failed).
- Adult native GHOA and anatomic TSA; excludes revision/reverse arthroplasty and primary rheumatoid arthritis. For nonoperative PT, no high/moderate-quality comparative studies: recommendations are best-practice opinion, not proof of specific exercise superiority. ROM/strength selected for individual impairment/irritability; excessive intensity can increase pain.
- Inference: clinician-tailored tolerable ROM/strengthening is plausible; no validated universal loading/degree cap, exercise ban or fixed progression. Post-TSA evidence cannot become a native-OA rule. Unknown surgical status matters. Matching intensity to irritability supports symptom-response modification but not a quantified damage threshold.

### GHOA-P1: Guo et al., 2016 prospective cohort

- Citation/access: [10.3928/01477447-20160606-02](https://doi.org/10.3928/01477447-20160606-02); PMID [27286050](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27286050/). Published final abstract inspected; full text not available in this review.
- 129 Chinese patients >65 with unilateral shoulder OA, multimodal conservative treatment; ROM/strengthening details identified in GHOA-G, not independently extracted from unavailable methods. No untreated comparator. Pain/function/quality-of-life generally improved at 3 years, with changing trajectories at intermediate assessments. Exercise dose and adverse-event surveillance unavailable.
- Inference: candidate conservative approach, low certainty; cannot isolate exercise benefit, import exact exercises or infer all loads safe. Do not extrapolate this older cohort to every stage/age/surgery type.

### GHOA-P2: Su et al., 2025 prospective pragmatic cohort

- Citation/access: [10.1016/j.jseint.2024.08.189](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jseint.2024.08.189); PMID [40182250](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40182250/); [PMC11962565](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11962565/). Final full-text XML inspected; publication collection March 2025.
- 62 primary GHOA patients chose treatment modalities; six-week PT emphasizing motion/strength offered, analgesics/injections possible. Prior fracture/dislocation/surgery/full-thickness tear excluded. 14/62 underwent TSA; 31% met clinically important ASES change; no ASES difference with versus without PT (P=.524). Only 53% completed 12-month ASES. Nonrandomized, self-selection/confounding; no detailed exercise injury attribution.
- Inference: conflicts with optimistic interpretation of Guo; PT benefit uncertain. Authors speculate loading could exacerbate symptoms, but this is not a causal trial of strengthening harm. No universal strengthening ban justified.

### Draft disposition and gaps

Manual-review/insufficient exercise-specific support. A symptom-modified mobility/strength program might be suitable after review, but no exact candidate is mapped on this evidence alone. Preserve independent joint ROM limits. Do not imply that a low-pain workout prevents OA progression, or that unstudied pressing is proven harmful. Native OA, arthroplasty, cuff arthropathy and inflammatory arthritis are different applicability states.

## 5. Shoulder instability

### INST-G1: BESS/BOA 2019 atraumatic pathway

- Citation/access: [10.1177/1758573218815002](https://doi.org/10.1177/1758573218815002); PMID [30719099](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30719099/); [PMC6348586](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6348586/). Final published pathway/full indexed text inspected.
- Atraumatic instability includes laxity, motor-control abnormalities, possible microtrauma/structural components. Specialist physiotherapy first-line; complex/persistent displacement requires specialist assessment. Evidence limited, largely small cohorts/consensus.
- Inference: no generic “stretch a tight shoulder” pathway; subtype and direction matter. This source does not prove every overhead position is unsafe or justify applying anterior-dislocation restrictions to posterior/MDI presentations.

### INST-G2: BESS 2026 traumatic anterior-dislocation rehabilitation guideline

- Citation/access: [10.1177/17585732261439731](https://doi.org/10.1177/17585732261439731); PMID [41959665](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41959665/). Final published abstract/citation inspected; full protocol not obtained.
- Evidence-informed national survey/Delphi/evidence synthesis/workshop; separate operative/nonoperative phases: protection, early movement/strength, progressive strength, return to sport/high-level function. Abstract time windows are descriptive, not app clearance dates. Outcomes/harms not independently tested by the consensus itself.
- Inference: acute/postoperative traumatic anterior instability needs its own phase/clinical criteria. No exact ROM, sling or loading rule serialized from this abstract.

### INST-S1: Cochrane, 2026 MDI exercise review

- Citation/access: Karasuyama et al., [10.1002/14651858.CD015450.pub2](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD015450.pub2); [Cochrane review summary](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD015450_what-are-benefits-and-harms-exercise-therapy-multidirectional-instability-shoulder-where-joint-moves). Final 2026-02-18 summary inspected; search through May 2025.
- No eligible randomized exercise-versus-placebo/no-treatment/usual-care trials, so absolute benefit/harms uncertain. This does not erase active-program comparison Warby; comparator eligibility differs. No quantitative safety claims supported.
- Inference: rehabilitation is a candidate supported by pathway/relative-program evidence, with uncertainty about treatment-specific benefit and harms versus control.

### INST-P1: Warby et al., 2018 RCT (online 2017)

- Citation/access: [10.1177/0363546517734508](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546517734508); PMID [29048942](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29048942/). Published final abstract inspected; full detailed program not available. [2016 protocol](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/6/9/e013083), DOI 10.1136/bmjopen-2016-013083, identified but publisher retrieval 403.
- 41 nontraumatic, nonstructural MDI participants; 12 weekly therapist sessions, Watson versus Rockwood exercise programs. At 24 weeks WOSI difference 12.6 (95% CI 3.4–21.9), MISS 15.4 (5.9–24.8), pain −2.0, favor Watson. Dislocation incidence assessed; abstract does not provide numerical adverse outcomes.
- Inference: relative program benefit in this specific subtype; not proof of optimal isolated cuff/scapular exercise, prophylactic stretching benefit, or safety in acute traumatic structural instability. No exact candidate variant/dose mapped from abstract.

### INST-P2: ARTISAN, Kearney et al., 2024 RCT

- Citation/access: [10.1136/bmj-2023-076925](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-076925); PMID [38233068](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38233068/); [PMC10792684](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10792684/). Final full-text XML inspected; published correction [10.1136/bmj.q662](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.q662) identified, content not separately extracted.
- 482 adults (mean 45) first-time radiologically confirmed traumatic anterior dislocation managed nonoperatively; neurovascular complications, bilateral injury, surgical candidates excluded. Both arms initial advice, progressive exercises/materials; extra PT up to four months versus option to self-refer.
- Six-month Oxford instability score difference 1.5 (95% CI −0.3 to 3.5); no worthwhile extra-PT benefit. Complications similar; ten redislocations overall. 73% provided primary outcome.
- Inference: extra visits not universally needed after well-taught program in selected first-time dislocations. It does not test immediate unrestricted gym return, all recurrent instability, young collision athletes alone, or no rehabilitation.

### Draft disposition and gaps

Undifferentiated instability is not ready for autonomous exercise mapping. Capture traumatic/atraumatic, direction, recurrent status, surgery and clinician plan. Controlled cuff/scapular motor-control rehabilitation is a candidate class, not an exact exercise identity. Avoid selecting deliberately unstable/apprehension-provoking ranges and aggressive laxity-increasing stretching pending review is a conservative planning inference, not a proven universal contraindication. Numeric ROM caps, “90/90” bans, hanging/pressing exclusions and return dates unresolved. Anterior and posterior precautions cannot be pooled blindly.

## 6. Acromioclavicular osteoarthritis

### ACOA-G: BESS/BOA shoulder primary/community care guideline, 2021

- Citation/access: [10.1177/1758573220984471](https://doi.org/10.1177/1758573220984471); PMID [33747136](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33747136/); [PMC7905504](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7905504/). Final published pathway summary inspected; AC-specific detailed exercise protocol not established. Welch review below identifies its conservative-treatment/injection pathway.
- Inference: conservative treatment is a clinical option; no dosage-specific strength/stretch recommendation or pressing ban inferred from a primary-care flowchart. No newer AC-specific exercise guideline verified in targeted search; broad shoulder/occupational guidelines exist but were not used to manufacture an AC regimen.

### ACOA-S1: Farrell et al., 2019 systematic review

- Citation/access: [10.1177/1758573219840673](https://doi.org/10.1177/1758573219840673); PMID [32269599](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32269599/); [PMC7094063](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7094063/). Final abstract/indexed methods/results inspected; full PMC browser/XML retrieval failed.
- Isolated clinically/radiologically diagnosed AC OA ≥3 months, age ≥16; searches to October 2018. Ten surgical studies described prior conservative treatment; none directly evaluated nonpharmacologic effectiveness. Activity modification in eight and PT in four is documentation of prior care, not causal effectiveness/contraindication evidence. Exercise doses and harms unresolved.
- Inference: failure-of-conservative-care windows cannot determine app exercise progression or justify specific bans.

### ACOA-S2: Welch et al., 2023 online/2024 issue systematic review

- Citation/access: [10.1177/17585732231157090](https://doi.org/10.1177/17585732231157090); PMID [38655415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38655415/); [PMC11034467](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11034467/). Final indexed full sections inspected via web; direct PMC/XML access failed.
- 48 studies; 1902 primary AC OA shoulders, physiotherapy-only one study; heterogeneous concomitant pathology, injections and surgeries. No meta-analysis. One surgery-versus-PT study included impingement, limiting isolated-OA inference. Physiotherapy-only military cohort short follow-up; not robust isolated exercise evidence.
- Inference: improvements in treatment packages do not establish a scapular-strengthening/stretch dose or prove bench presses, dips or cross-body adduction harmful in all AC OA. Distal clavicle osteolysis/traumatic separation evidence cannot automatically stand in for primary OA.

### ACOA-P1: Harris et al., 2012 prospective cohort

- Citation/access: [10.2519/jospt.2012.3866](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2012.3866); PMID [22030329](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22030329/); final [full PDF with appendices](https://storage.googleapis.com/edcompass/material/AC%20joint%20pain.pdf) inspected.
- Injection-confirmed nonacute primary AC joint pain, 15 enrolled/13 completed; mean 41, 85% male, military setting; four normal radiographs. Thus not uniformly imaging-confirmed primary OA. Manual therapy twice weekly/3 weeks, assisted ROM allowed; no strengthening until four-week assessment, then home resistance-band/ROM program 3/week. No control.
- SPADI improved 25.9 points at four weeks, 29.8 at six months; no additional care/increased medication/activity reduction among completers. Formal adverse-event count not found. Multiple joints treated and later exercises confound component effects.
- Inference: assisted ROM/band strengthening are exploratory candidates only. Delaying strength in this protocol does not prove strength contraindicated before week four. Exact appendix variants would need execution matching; not mapped.

### ACOA-P2: ACAM randomized-study protocol, 2020 (date corrected at serialization)

- Citation/access: [10.1136/bmjopen-2019-034439](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2019-034439); final published [protocol](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2019-034439) inspected, not an outcome paper. NCT03951480.
- Planned 136 adults 40–70 with radiographic symptomatic AC OA ≥3 months; manual therapy versus corticosteroid injection, activity-related pain at three months. No outcome results retrieved. This is not an exercise efficacy trial.
- Inference: supplies research context only; cannot claim benefit, noninferiority, safety or a workout restriction.

### Contrary scope evidence: Soler et al., 2021 scoping review

- Citation/access: [10.1007/s00167-020-06377-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00167-020-06377-8); PMID [33386878](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33386878/). Published final abstract inspected.
- 19 studies/861 shoulders, primary OA mixed with post-traumatic OA/distal-clavicle osteolysis; four conservative-treatment reports, no direct conservative-versus-surgical comparisons, very-low methodological quality. Both groups of studies improved; title must not be read as randomized equivalence.
- Inference: broader mixed-diagnosis optimism does not override isolated-primary-OA uncertainty.

### Draft disposition and gaps

Insufficient evidence/restriction-review only; no exact therapeutic exercise approved for app use. Individual modification of symptom-provoking compression/cross-body/pressing load is plausible but not a trial-proven diagnosis-wide prohibition. No evidence-derived universal weight, ROM or pain threshold found. An explicit gap is safer than importing rotator cuff or distal-clavicle osteolysis rules.
