**[Effect of High-Intensity Strength Training on Knee Pain and Knee Joint Compressive Forces Among Adults With Knee Osteoarthritis: The START Randomized Clinical Trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7887656/)** — DOI `10.1001/jama.2021.0411`, PMID `33591346`; final journal article; access `full-text`.

PMC full HTML inspected including Methods, Tables and harms. Supplement not retrieved.

Design/population: Assessor-blinded single-center RCT, n=377; 320 completed. Ambulatory adults >=50; BMI 20–45; medial tibiofemoral KL 2–3 OA. Severe patellofemoral/lateral-predominant OA excluded; nonsurgical exercise study.
Comparator/cointerventions: High-intensity vs low-intensity strength vs attention workshops. Usual medication continued; ten-exercise upper/lower/core program and behavioral support.

- dose: High: 3 sets, 75%1RM 8 reps weeks 1–2; 80%8 weeks 3–4; 85%6 weeks 5–6; 90%4 weeks 7–8. Low: 3x 15, 30–40%1RM.
- frequency: Three supervised sessions/week, 18 months; 5 min warmup, 40 min training, 15 min cooldown.
- supervision: Staff interventionists; four technique sessions precede 1RM testing.
- progression: Week 9 taper/retest 1RM; repeat 9-week block.
- stoppingRules: not reported as a universal numeric pain-stop criterion
