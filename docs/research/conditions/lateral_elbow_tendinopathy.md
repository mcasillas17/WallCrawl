# Lateral elbow tendinopathy

**Overall evidence grade for exercise guidance: low.** This is general information for a workout app. It is not diagnosis or treatment.

## Summary

- Resisted wrist extensor exercise has the best support. The JOSPT guideline gives isometric, concentric and/or eccentric work a grade B recommendation for subacute or chronic cases (Lucado 2022).
- The effects are small and the certainty is low to very low. Exercise did better than passive treatments, and better than wait-and-see on only some measures (Karanasios 2021).
- The best exercise type, load and dose are unknown. No study in the guideline's review described how load was set or progressed.
- Longer-term benefit is less clear. Physiotherapy beat wait-and-see in the short term with no difference at 52 weeks (Bisset 2006, abstract, not used as a list source).

## Is the diagnosis contested?

Not contested as a clinical diagnosis. The mechanism is less settled, and it can be mimicked by cervical radiculopathy, radial tunnel syndrome, posterior interosseous syndrome and joint conditions. A self-reported label may be wrong.

## Movements to modify

Nothing is listed as a strict "avoid".

| Movement | Evidence | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Wrist extensor loading that aggravates symptoms | Guideline | Progress from isometric to isotonic to eccentric as tolerated |
| Repeated wrist or elbow flexion and extension | Observational | Risk factor above 2 hours a day |
| Repeated forearm twisting or screwing motions | Observational | Risk factor only |
| Lifting with the palm facing down | Expert opinion | Applying this to gym lifts is an extrapolation |
| Very heavy loads in the hands | Observational | Tools over 20 kg at work, not a gym threshold |

## Candidate exercises

| Exercise | Catalog | Dose | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wrist extension with a light dumbbell | `wrist-extension` | 3 x 15 for 6-12 weeks | Guideline |
| Isometric wrist extension hold | New | 3 x 15 suggested; daily for 8 weeks in the trial; hold time unknown | RCT |
| Eccentric wrist extension with a rubber bar | New | 3 x 15 daily for 7 weeks | Systematic review |
| Shoulder and scapular stabilizer training | New | Not established | Guideline, grade C |

- **Hold time:** the isometric trial's hold time was not in the sources opened, so it is left empty.
- **Isometric trial result:** it improved disability and worst pain, but not global improvement or pain-free grip strength. Its authors question it as a sole treatment (Vuvan 2020).
- **Eccentric versus other strengthening:** the pain benefit was small, with no clear difference in strength or function (Yoon 2021).
- **Scapular training:** it applies only when impairments are identified, which an app cannot do. The guideline passage reviewed names no specific exercises. The trials conflict: Mostafaee 2020 favored adding it, while Day 2021 found no added value in a multimodal program.
- **Catalog:** `wrist-curl` was not matched because it loads the wrist flexors, not the extensors.

## Red flags

Stop elbow-loading exercise and seek urgent medical assessment for:

- Atypical mass or swelling
- Unremitting or increasing pain
- Night pain
- Feeling systemically unwell, malaise or weight loss

Get prompt assessment for progressive weakness when extending the fingers or wrist, which can point to posterior interosseous nerve compression.

Get a professional evaluation if symptoms may come from the neck or a nerve, or do not fit the usual pattern.

## Limitations

- **Dose:** the 3 x 15 dose comes from best available evidence and expert opinion, and trial protocols varied widely.
- **Currency:** the guideline covers literature published before November 2021, and no later systematic review was read.
- **Sources not opened:** the NICE CKS page and the BESS tennis elbow pathway could not be opened, so neither is cited. No Cochrane review was opened.
- **Partial reads:** Karanasios 2021, Vuvan 2020 and Bisset 2006 were read as abstracts only. The Tyler 2010 protocol was read only as tabulated in Yoon 2021.
- **Red flags:** they come from one local NHS pathway and one case-based article, not a validated screening tool. Some patient-facing wording is this report's own.
- **Extrapolation:** applying occupational risk factors to gym movements has not been tested.

## Verification

An independent check opened 8 sources: the 7 listed in the JSON, including NHS Tayside, plus Bisset 2006, which only the summary above uses. All exist and their details match. The `wrist-extension` catalog entry exists and is the same movement.

**Removed:**

- **Hard or sustained gripping that provokes pain:** no cited source supports it. The guideline mentions gripping only as a provoking test in a trial's inclusion criteria, and NHS Tayside says only "Modify loading". NHS Tayside was then unused and was removed.
- **Wrist extensor stretch:** no source recommends it as a candidate or gives a dose. The trials the guideline describes tested stretching only combined with strengthening, so no source isolates a benefit from stretching.

**Labels corrected:** none.

**Other changes:** the Karanasios 2021 and Vuvan 2020 URLs in the JSON were search queries and now use DOI links. The scapular training caution now notes the conflicting trials.

**Could not be fully verified:**

- The publisher copy of Lucado 2022 returned 403. The full text was read from the orthopt.org PDF, which carries the JOSPT download stamp.
- "Stop elbow-loading exercise" is not in the NHS Highland pathway. Its clinician-facing action is an urgent call to on-call orthopaedics plus a referral.
- Cha 2014 names progressive weakness of the digit extensors and extensor carpi radialis brevis. "Wrist" is an anatomical reading of that muscle, not the article's word.
- The Tyler 2010 protocol was checked only as tabulated in Yoon 2021. Vuvan 2020 hold time, sets and reps were not checked in its full text.
