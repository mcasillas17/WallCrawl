# Carpal tunnel syndrome: exercise evidence

**Evidence grade for exercise guidance: very low.** This supports workout planning and general mobility information. It is not treatment. The structured version is `carpal_tunnel_syndrome.json`, which holds the full source list.

## Bottom line

- The best review (Cochrane 2012, 16 studies, 741 participants) rates exercise and mobilisation evidence as limited and very low quality, and could not pool the results. Only two studies measured adverse effects.
- Nerve and tendon gliding may help as an add-on to usual care. Evidence for them alone is lacking (Kim 2015; Ballestero-Perez 2017; Medina McKeon 2008; Akalin 2002).
- Reported guideline support is Grade C for a combined orthotic and stretching programme in mild to moderate CTS. The 2019 guideline summary makes no recommendation on neurodynamic mobilisation because the evidence conflicts.
- A neutral-positioned night orthosis has stronger support (Grade B), but it is outside the app's exercise scope.

## Diagnosis

The condition is accepted, but how to diagnose and grade it is debated. A secondary summary reports no true reference standard, normal electrodiagnostic results in some symptomatic people, and severity grading of limited validity. Padua 2016 also reports limited evidence on the best assessment approach.

## Movements to modify

No movement is listed as a blanket "avoid". A secondary summary of the 2026 guideline favours individual modification over avoiding hand use altogether.

| Movement | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Sustained or loaded near end-range wrist extension or flexion (flat-palm push-ups, planks and front-rack are inferred examples) | Canal pressure rises away from neutral, more in CTS | Biomechanical |
| Forceful, high-repetition hand work | Repetition HR 1.87; force HR 1.84, from workplace cohorts | Observational |
| Hard or sustained pinching and fingertip loading | Small loads raise pressure; pinching nearly doubles it | Biomechanical |
| Hand-transmitted vibration | Mixed findings across reviews; NHS advises cutting back | Observational |
| Strong nerve tensioning (prefer sliding) | Cadaver strain 6.8% versus 0.8%; no clinical harm shown | Biomechanical |

| Finger bend at the middle joints | new | 3 x 10, 5 s hold | Expert opinion (CSP) |
| Light palm-down wrist extension | `catalog:wrist-extension` | 3 x 10, very light load | Expert opinion (CSP) |
| Rubber ball squeeze | new | 3 x 10, 5 s hold | Expert opinion (CSP) |

CSP says to use its exercises for at least 6-8 weeks but does not state sessions per day or week. It also lists a wrist lift with no dose, so that is omitted.

The catalog has `wrist-extension` as the only true match. `wrist-curl` is barbell loaded flexion, so it was not used. Hangs and grip-heavy lifts are not candidates.

**Where sources disagree:** CSP describes bending the wrist to a right angle and recommends ball squeezing. Pressure studies show the highest pressures at 90 degrees and increases with fingertip loading; the same effect from squeezing is inferred. CSP also says to increase the wrist extension weight gradually, without a limit. Both right-angle bends and squeezing carry a caution, and a smaller range or softer effort is our untested inference.

## Red flags

- **Sudden, rapidly worsening symptoms, especially after a fracture, bleeding or high-pressure injury:** stop and seek emergency care the same day.
- **Hand or thumb weakness, or wasting at the base of the thumb:** stop these exercises and get prompt assessment.
- **Constant numbness or progressive sensory loss:** get prompt assessment.
- **Several nerve areas affected, or neck pain, walking changes or brisk reflexes:** stop the programme and get medical evaluation.
- **Symptoms worsening, persisting or not responding to home treatment:** see a GP or clinician.

## Limitations

- The JOSPT 2019 and 2026 guidelines, the AAOS 2024 guideline and NICE CKS could not be read directly. Guideline statements come from secondary summaries (Guideline Central, Physiotutors, iatroX) and should be checked against the originals before release.
- Several papers were read only as abstracts; the source list marks which.
- Gliding doses come from small trials where gliding was combined with other treatment, so they are not proven standalone doses.
- CSP doses are patient-information advice with no linked trial data found.
- Pressure studies are mostly in healthy volunteers. Risk data are occupational and may not transfer to training.
- No study found measured push-ups, planks, hangs or barbell grip in people with CTS, so those examples are inference.
- Red flag wording draws heavily on one educational summary of AAOS and on a small case series for acute CTS.
- The Cochrane review dates from 2012 by its DOI record, so it may miss later trials.
