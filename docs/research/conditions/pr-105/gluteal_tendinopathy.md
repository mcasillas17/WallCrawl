# Gluteal tendinopathy (greater trochanteric pain syndrome)

General exercise information for planning around a condition. This is not treatment.

**Overall evidence grade: low.** Structured data is in `gluteal_tendinopathy.json`.

## Scope and search log

This review covers exercise, load advice and safety for people with lateral hip pain from gluteal tendinopathy or greater trochanteric pain syndrome (GTPS). Injection, shockwave and surgery are included where they bear on exercise choice or test it as a comparator.

Three independent searchers ran 44 searches across PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, Cochrane and the web. A later check confirmed PubMed metadata and reopened the LEAP and Nava protocol tables. The result is 71 sources.

Gaps in the search:

- The Cochrane Library returned HTTP 403, and a guessed NICE CKS address did too.
- AAOS, WHO and the libraries of national guideline bodies were not checked directly.
- One PubMed biomechanics query failed with HTTP 429 and was not retried.
- Most listings were screened by title, often only the first page.

## Evidence summary

### Education plus exercise

[Mellor 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.k1662) (LEAP) is the main trial: 204 people, 82% women, mean age 54.8, with MRI-confirmed tendon changes.

| Global success | Education plus exercise | Corticosteroid injection | Wait and see |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 weeks | 51/66 (77%) | 38/65 (58%) | 20/68 (29%) |
| 52 weeks | 51/65 (78%) | 36/63 (57%) | 31/60 (52%) |

- **8 weeks:** the risk difference was 49.1% against wait and see (95% CI 34.6 to 63.5, NNT 2.0) and 19.9% against injection (4.7 to 35.0, NNT 5.0). Pain differences were -2.2 (-2.89 to -1.54) and -1.04 (-1.72 to -0.37).
- **52 weeks:** the risk difference was 20.4% against injection (4.9 to 35.9) and 26.8% against wait and see (11.3 to 42.3). Pain did not differ from injection (-0.26, -1.06 to 0.55). Injection did not beat wait and see on global success (6.4%, -10.7 to 23.6).
- **Harms and cost:** no serious adverse events were reported, and weekly adherence stayed above 80%. Non-serious counts are in a web appendix that was not opened. [Wilson 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphys.2022.11.007) found the programme cost-effective against both comparators.

### Which part works

- **Exercise against sham.** [Ganderton 2018](https://doi.org/10.1089/jwh.2017.6729) (94 postmenopausal women) found no between-group difference when both arms received education. [Mcmillan 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/03635465211061142) (132 women) found the same: VISA-G MD 2.15 (P=.49) at 12 weeks and -3.08 (P=.32) at 52 weeks. In its BMI under 25 stratum, hormone therapy did better than placebo (-20.72, -31.22 to -10.22, at 12 weeks), with a baseline imbalance in that stratum.
- **Exercise type.** [Nava 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsams.2025.04.012) (60 women) found motor control no better than general exercise: pain MD -0.06 (-1.41 to 1.29) at 8 weeks and -0.75 (-2.35 to 0.83) at 60 weeks. It was not an equivalence trial. [Clifford 2019](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjsem-2019-000558) found no isometric advantage, with a VISA-G difference of -0.1 (-13.8 to 13.5) at 12 weeks in 23 completers. [Clifford 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjsem-2020-000760) agrees across tendinopathies, with only one gluteal study.
- **Mechanism.** [Mellor 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.11261) found benefit mediated by patient-specific function, pain constancy and pain self-efficacy, not by hip movement or torque. [Plinsinga 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejp.1199) found strength did not differ by severity, though severe cases had more catastrophising and depression, lower self-efficacy and greater BMI. [Plinsinga 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msksp.2019.07.009) does find lower abductor strength against healthy controls, with very low certainty.
- **Lower contact.** [Almousa 2023](https://doi.org/10.12688/hrbopenres.13796.2) and [French 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msksp.2026.103607) tested 6 sessions rather than 14, meeting feasibility thresholds without testing efficacy. [Morin Melas 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2024-090688) is an ongoing trial of 3-5 sessions. In [Hogsholt 2026](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2026-120837), 7 supervised sessions gave a HAGOS pain change of 14.5 (9.0 to 20.0), but only 20/55 reported global success.

### Injection and shockwave

- **Corticosteroid:** [Nissen 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10067-018-4309-6) (46 people) found it no better than saline at 1, 3 or 6 months. [Rompe 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546509334374) and [NICE HTG248](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/htg248/chapter/1-Recommendations) show it ahead at 1 month (75% success, against 7% home training and 13% shockwave) and behind at 15 months (48%, against 80% and 74%). [Grimaldi 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/rap/rkae022) and [Grimaldi 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msksp.2025.103253) argue against it as first-line care. [Brennan 2017](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2017.6994) found dry needling noninferior at 6 weeks.
- **Shockwave:** [Rhim 2024](https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.RVW.24.00091) found a small benefit at 2-4 months (SMD -0.431, -0.82 to -0.039), with 7 of 8 RCTs at high risk of bias. [Harding 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/msc.1892) found no significant effect against control. [Wheeler 2022](https://doi.org/10.1097/JSM.0000000000000880) found no benefit over minimal dose in 120 chronic patients. [Heaver 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/11207000211060396) favoured focused shockwave over injection at 12 months (VAS 37.1 against 55.0).
- **Structure:** [Carney 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-023-01150-y) found no clear MRI change years after treatment in 20 people, in arms that received platelet-rich plasma or corticosteroid.

### Diagnosis and presentation

Partly contested. The condition is accepted, but GTPS is a non-specific umbrella term covering bursitis, external snapping hip and gluteus medius/minimus tendinopathy or tears. There is no consensus on the most useful clinical tests. Tendon changes on MRI were found in 88% of asymptomatic women in one small study, so imaging correlates poorly with symptoms. Trials used varying eligibility criteria. [Kinsella 2024](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2023.11890) (6 studies, 272 people, certainty very low to moderate) found that in people with lateral hip pain, positive gluteal tendon palpation followed by positive resisted abduction raised the probability of GTPS from 59% to 96%, and both negative lowered it to 14%. [ISHA 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jhps/hnac050) names gluteal tendinopathy with or without bursitis as the primary pain source, and the NHS Lothian fact sheet calls MRI findings a poor predictor of symptoms. [Ganderton 2017](https://doi.org/10.1089/jwh.2016.5889) is the source of the 88% figure, from 17 asymptomatic women scanned.

[Grimaldi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjpt.2026.101583) gives the LEAP baseline function scores (0 unable, 10 pre-condition ability). The lowest were court or field sports 2.5, jumping 2.7, martial arts 3.0 and running 3.2. Walking was the activity most often nominated (68%). Lifting and bending scored comparatively well at 6.3, which is an indirect signal only.

Gait and muscle studies describe differences but cannot show cause. These include [Allison 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinbiomech.2016.10.003), [Allison gait 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2018.02.011), [Allison EMG 2018](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.12942) and [Ganderton EMG 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jelekin.2017.01.004).

## What to avoid or modify

Almost all of this is expert reasoning. It was bundled into effective education programmes, but no restriction was tested alone. [Grimaldi 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-015-0336-5) and [Grimaldi 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/rap/rkae022) set out the compression model, and [Grimaldi 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/rap/rkae022) states that high tensile and compressive load together are most detrimental.

[Derksen 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/jor.70107) is about walking after repair and does not apply to non-surgical tendinopathy.

**Cautions**

- **Standing isometric hip abduction in slight abduction:** Use gentle effort (Borg 11-12) and avoid adduction. The XML table offsets its labels from its values, so assigning this row to this position is a reading of the layout, and it should be checked against the published PDF.
- **Supine isometric hip abduction hold:** Use gentle effort (Borg 11-12) and stay out of adduction. Restored after an earlier check removed a mismatched dose. The XML table offsets its labels from its values, so assigning this row to this position is a reading of the layout, and it should be checked against the published PDF. The earlier entry's belt or band is not in the protocol text, so none is listed.
- **Side-lying isometric hip abduction hold, painful side up:** Keep pillows between the knees and do not let the top hip drop into adduction. Tested in a 30-person pilot trial with no untreated control, where it showed no advantage over isotonic work. Bilateral symptoms prevent side-lying without compressing one hip, so use the supine hold instead. Clifford 2019 started without external load, then added elastic bands around both ankles, keeping pain at or below 5/10.
- **Standing weight-bearing isometric gluteal contraction, moving the free leg in and out:** Hold a wall or chair for support. Stop adding load if pain increases overnight or the next day. Pilot-trial evidence only. Clifford 2019 started without external load, then added elastic bands around both ankles, keeping pain at or below 5/10.
- **Side-lying hip abduction:** Keep the painful side up, pillows between the knees at the start, and the knee straight. Lift to about 30 degrees and lower with control. The catalog entry does not carry these setup cues. Pilot-trial evidence only. Clifford 2019 started without external load, then added elastic bands around both ankles, keeping pain at or below 5/10.
- **Standing hip abduction slide:** Hold a chair or table, keep the foot on the floor and the working knee straight. Requires a surface that allows sliding. Pilot-trial evidence only. Clifford 2019 started without external load, then added elastic bands around both ankles, keeping pain at or below 5/10.
- **Double-leg glute bridge:** The dose is the first week of a supervised, progressive multi-part programme, and this exercise was not tested alone.
- **Offset bridge, biased to one leg:** A later progression at 'somewhat hard' effort (Borg 13-15). It is less demanding than the catalog's full single-leg bridge and is not the same movement.
- **Bodyweight double-leg squat:** Do not let the pelvis shift over one hip. Part of a multi-part programme and not tested alone.
- **Offset squat, biased to one leg:** A later progression at 'somewhat hard' effort (Borg 13-15). Keep the pelvis level and avoid drifting into hip adduction.
- **Bodyweight side-stepping:** Push into abduction with the stance leg and keep the pelvis and trunk aligned. The catalog's banded-lateral-walk adds band resistance, so it was not treated as the same movement.
- **Band side slide:** A later progression at 'somewhat hard' to 'hard' effort. Keep weight on the stationary leg and slide the other foot on a slippery surface. Requires a band.
- **Single-leg-biased bridge:** The progression from the offset bridge. Keep the pelvis level and avoid drifting into hip adduction. Part of a multi-part programme and not tested alone.
- **Single-leg-biased squat:** The progression from the offset squat. Compression cannot be fully avoided in single-leg squatting. Keep the pelvis level and avoid drifting into hip adduction. Part of a multi-part programme and not tested alone.
- **Banded side walking:** The motor control arm of a 60-woman trial, which was not superior to general exercise. A narrative review advises checking technique and adjusting resistance or stride for gluteal tendinopathy, with no band-walk study behind that advice. Requires a band.
- **Single-leg stance hold:** The table lists an elastic band as material. It conflicts with ISHA's advice to minimise single-leg time in daily activities, and single-leg stance for 30 s is also used as a pain provocation test, so keep it for later stages. The arm was not superior to general exercise.
- **Hip hike on a step:** The table prints 'Hold 15s' and '8-12' in one cell without saying how they combine, so use one or the other until the source is checked. Lists an elastic band and step. Hip hitch variations reach over 40% MVIC across gluteus medius, though EMG predicts muscle force poorly. The arm was not superior to general exercise.
- **Wall squat hold with a ball:** Thighs parallel to the ground. Dose is read from the table in Cordeiro 2024, not from the original trial, whose full text was not opened. Rompe 2009 used sequential assignment rather than randomisation, and its programme also included stretches this record advises against. Requires a ball.

### Programme structure and progression

- **LEAP ([LEAP protocol 2016](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-016-1043-6), [Mellor 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.k1662)):** 14 sessions over 8 weeks, weekly for 2 weeks and then twice weekly. The home programme had 4-6 exercises, no longer than 15-20 min, daily. Borg targets were warm-up 11-12, functional retraining 13-15 and heavy slow strengthening 14-17. Supervised sliding-platform work is left out because it needs specialist equipment: warm-up 5 each way, then 5-10 each way at somewhat hard to very hard effort, twice weekly.
- **LEAP-Ireland ([Almousa 2023](https://doi.org/10.12688/hrbopenres.13796.2)):** 6 days a week. Weeks 3-8 used three hard to very hard days, three light days and one rest day, which is the most explicit weekly structure found.
- **Clifford ([Clifford 2019](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjsem-2019-000558)):** daily for 12 weeks with 8 physiotherapy sessions, 6 min time under tension. Isometric holds were 30 s x 6 with 60 s rest. Isotonic work was 3 x 10 at 3 s up and 3 s down.
- **Nava ([Nava protocol 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0269230)):** 50-60 min twice weekly for 8 weeks, 3 sets of 8-12. The text says week 1 is unloaded, while the table lists bands. Load rose when modified Borg was 3 or lower.
- **Ganderton and McMillan (via [Cordeiro 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-53283-x) only):** 10-15 min, twice daily, 2-4 sets of 5-15 reps, for 12 weeks.
- **Rompe (via [Cordeiro 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-53283-x) only):** twice daily, 7 days a week. Beyond the wall squat, it included gluteal hip extension holds of 5 s, 3 x 10, a straight leg raise 3 x 10, and the stretches advised against above.
- **Selection:** [Moore 2020](https://doi.org/10.26603/ijspt20200856) and [Ebert 2017](https://doi.org/10.1123/jsr.2016-0088) rank exercises by EMG, but [Collings 2025](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003733) found poor agreement with force (rho 0.29-0.51). [Gonzalez-de-la-Flor 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/jfmk10030294) reports 30-50% MVIC in the stance limb during lateral band walks.

The list has no stretches. [ISHA 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jhps/hnac050) allows pain-free stretching that avoids lateral compression after injection, but gives no specific stretch or dose.

## Red flags

Stop the plan and seek medical assessment for the items below. Action wording was written for this app, because no source gives a condition-specific list, and [York pathway](https://hnyppr.org.uk/web/york/w/greater-trochanteric-pain-syndrome-pathway) lists its red flags as "None provided".

- **Hip or groin pain that worsens with weight bearing after a fall or a rapid increase in activity, especially with known osteoporosis.** Stop the plan and seek prompt medical assessment for possible hip fracture ([Grimaldi and Fearon 2015](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2015.5829), [Wilson and Furukawa 2014](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2014/0101/p27.html))
- **Hip or groin pain linked to repetitive weight-bearing exercise, particularly in endurance athletes, smokers, steroid users or people with female athlete triad.** Stop impact and loading work and seek medical assessment for possible stress fracture, since an early X-ray can be normal ([Wilson and Furukawa 2014](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2014/0101/p27.html))
- **Sudden hip pain without injury that makes weight bearing difficult, especially with diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, recent joint surgery, a prosthesis or age over 80.** Seek urgent medical assessment for possible joint infection ([Wilson and Furukawa 2014](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2014/0101/p27.html))
- **Deep, unrelenting pain, possibly worse at night, particularly with a history of cancer or unexplained weight loss.** Stop the plan and seek prompt medical assessment ([Grimaldi and Fearon 2015](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2015.5829))
- **Pain on walking with risk factors for osteonecrosis, including corticosteroid use, heavy alcohol use, smoking, lupus, sickle cell disease or HIV infection.** Seek medical assessment before continuing loading work ([Wilson and Furukawa 2014](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2014/0101/p27.html), [Grimaldi and Fearon 2015](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2015.5829))
- **Heat, redness or swelling in several areas, morning stiffness over 1 hour, or symmetrical joint signs.** Seek medical assessment for possible inflammatory disease ([Grimaldi and Fearon 2015](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2015.5829))
- **Pain spreading below the knee, or lateral hip pain with low back pain.** Ask a clinician to check the back and leg, because the pain may not come from the gluteal tendons ([ISHA 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jhps/hnac050), [Grimaldi and Fearon 2015](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2015.5829))
- **Groin pain with morning stiffness, lost hip motion, locking, giving way, or difficulty putting on shoes and socks.** Ask a clinician to check for hip joint disease ([ISHA 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jhps/hnac050), [Grimaldi and Fearon 2015](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2015.5829))
- **Gluteal tendon repair surgery in the past 16 weeks.** Do not use this plan. Follow the surgical team's rehabilitation. ISHA's consensus limits hip flexion to 90 degrees, external rotation to 20 degrees and adduction to neutral in weeks 1-6, contraindicates active against-gravity abduction until week 8 and active long-lever hip flexion until week 12, and excludes jumping, hopping, cutting and pivoting in weeks 12-16. ([ISHA 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jhps/hnac050), [Ebert 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00402-021-03952-7))
- **A known gluteal tendon tear or rupture on imaging.** Ask a clinician before loading. Spencer 2026 found no non-surgical treatment studies for symptomatic tears. One later uncontrolled cohort reported no adverse events and no worse outcome in its rupture subgroup, which is too weak to establish safety. ([Spencer 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-026-09519-0), [Hogsholt 2026](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2026-120837))

### Numbers not to quote

- **[Bremer 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/02692155251327298):** the focused shockwave long-term SMD of 5.77 (4.84 to 6.71) is implausibly large.
- **[Challoumas 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-023-00616-1):** the printed long-term estimate does not contain its own point estimate.
- **[Cordeiro 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-53283-x):** the table gives inconsistent Rompe arm sizes, and participant totals differ within the paper.
- **[ISHA 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jhps/hnac050):** the panel makeup differs between abstract and methods.
- **[Clifford 2019](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjsem-2019-000558):** adherence denominators and one week 4 figure are inconsistent.

### Limitations

- **Evidence grade:** it stays low because the positive evidence rests heavily on one unblinded trial with patient-rated outcomes, certainty ratings are low in most reviews, and trial results are fragile.
- **Generalising:** participants were mostly women, many postmenopausal. LEAP excluded back, sciatic or groin pain above 2/10 and total hip replacement.
- **Compression:** no study found measures tendon compression during gym exercises.
- **Doses:** table layouts in the LEAP protocol are offset in XML, and one Nava cell is ambiguous. "BD" is defined only as "bi-daily". These should be checked against the published PDFs before they ship.
- **Adverse events:** detail is thin. [Bremer 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/02692155251327298) does not report them, and most abstracts gave no counts. [Cordeiro 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-53283-x) calls them uncommon and mostly short-term pain increases.
- **Reading depth:** access level is listed per reference. Many full-text sources were read by targeted extraction. The sources behind the red flags were reopened in the latest check.
- **Downstream:** this update did not change `CONSOLIDATION.md`, whose counts now differ from this record.

## Verification
