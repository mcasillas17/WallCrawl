# Lumbar radiculopathy (sciatica): literature review

**Overall evidence grade for exercise guidance: low.** This is general information for planning exercise around a condition. It is not diagnosis or treatment.

## Bottom line

Staying active is the best-supported advice, because bed rest adds nothing. No exercise type is established as better than the others, and structured exercise adds little beyond advice in the long term. Early physical therapy produced a modest benefit in one 220-person trial, while supervised neural mobilisation shows large pooled effects that are probably inflated. No trial tested self-directed neural mobilisation alone. No study tested gym lifts in people with active sciatica, so all lifting guidance below is inference.

## 1. Scope and search log

**Question.** What can a person with lumbar radiculopathy do in a self-guided exercise app, and what should they modify, with particular attention to self-directed neural mobilisation?

**Method.** Three independent searchers covered guidelines, then efficacy and reviews, then trials, doses, harms and biomechanics. Results were merged and deduplicated by DOI or PMID. This record holds 102 sources. An independent citation check then reopened them; see the end of section 6.

**Not retrieved.** WHO guideline full text (reCAPTCHA), Danish SST quick guide (HTTP 429), AAPM guideline full text (HTTP 403), KCE Report 287 (HTTP 404), the KNGF guideline itself, and the Cochrane Library directly (HTTP 403). ## 2. Evidence summary

### Is the diagnosis contested?

Partly. The condition is accepted, but its definition is not standard. One review found definitions varied widely across 23 prevalence studies, with prevalence from 1.2% to 43% ([Konstantinou 2008](https://doi.org/10.1097/BRS.0b013e318183a4a2)). Most physical tests perform poorly in isolation ([van der Windt 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007431.pub2)), and a modelling study used two reference standards with differing agreement ([Stynes 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0191852)). A person's self-reported "sciatica" may not be true nerve-root pain. [Khorami 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm10112482) found guidelines consistent on straight leg raise, crossed straight leg raise, pain mapping, gait assessment and sign-symptom congruence, and against routine imaging.

### Natural history

[van Wambeke 2020 (KCE summary)](https://doi.org/10.23736/S1973-9087.19.05983-5) reports that radicular pain usually recovers spontaneously, yet pain and impairment persist at one year in about one third of cases. In [Vroomen 1999](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199902113400602), 87% had improved at 12 weeks in both groups. Spontaneous regression on imaging was estimated at 96% for sequestration, 70% for extrusion, 41% for protrusion and 13% for bulging ([Chiu 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269215514540919)). Uncontrolled improvement therefore cannot be credited to exercise, which also applies to the often-cited cohort in [Saal 1989](https://doi.org/10.1097/00007632-198904000-00018) (90% good or excellent, but only 64 of 347 identified patients, with no control) and the case series in [Hahne 2011](https://doi.org/10.3109/09638288.2010.533814).

Reviews of guidelines add context. [Khorami 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm10112482) (23 guidelines, AGREE II 17% to 92%) rates physical activity a should-do and exercise or physical therapies a could-do (its table lists 7 guidelines for each, while its text cites 8 and 6). Bed rest, manipulation or mobilisation, and traction were inconsistent across guidelines, and neural mobilisation does not appear. [Zaina 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2023.02.022) found 4 high-quality guidelines recommending education, multimodal education with exercise and spinal manipulation, and intensive interdisciplinary rehabilitation for persistent pain, with a search ending in 2019.

A [Neuropathic pain consensus 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2021.756940) gives motor control, aquatic stabilising and isometric exercise Grade A with 100% consensus of 21 voters. That rests on short-term leg pain benefits, so the grade looks generous.

For contrast, Cochrane reports in chronic non-specific low back pain, not radiculopathy, that exercise changes pain by MD -15.2 (-18.3 to -12.2) with moderate certainty ([Hayden 2021 (Cochrane)](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009790.pub2)). ### Staying active and bed rest

For sciatica, Cochrane found moderate-quality evidence of little or no difference between advice to rest in bed and advice to stay active: pain SMD -0.03 (-0.24 to 0.18), function SMD 0.19 (-0.02 to 0.41) ([Dahm 2010 (Cochrane)](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007612.pub2)). In [Vroomen 1999](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199902113400602) (183 people), 70% improved with bed rest versus 65% with watchful waiting at 2 weeks (adjusted OR 1.2, 0.6 to 2.3). Bed rest did not beat continuing activities in [Hofstee 2002](https://doi.org/10.3171/spi.2002.96.1.0045). Among prospective cohorts, activity at least 4 times a week was associated with fewer new cases of radicular pain (RR 0.88, 0.78 to 0.99), as observational data ([Shiri 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejp.885)).

The trials are therapist-delivered or mixed. The nearest thing to self-direction is the home programme attached to supervised sessions, as in [Ferreira protocol 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbmt.2016.02.012) and the trials tabulated in [Peacock 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/10669817.2022.2065599). [Correa 2022 protocol](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0263152) is the largest planned trial found (210 people); its results were not found.

### Mechanism and nerve biomechanics

Sliders move the nerve more than tensioners in [Coppieters 2015](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2015.5743) (17.0 mm vs 3.2 mm, 15 healthy people), but only slightly in [Ellis 2012](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2012.3854) (3.2 mm vs 2.6 mm), and not meaningfully in [Ellis 2017](https://doi.org/10.1179/2042618615Y.0000000020) (slider 6.4 to 6.9 mm, tensioner 6.0 to 6.4 mm). Slump versus upright did not change excursion in [Ellis 2017](https://doi.org/10.1179/2042618615Y.0000000020) (P=0.26). Movement sequence barely changed end-position strain in [Boyd 2013](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2013.4413). In a study of people with disc herniation, tibial nerve excursion at 20 and 40 degrees was smaller than in healthy people, so healthy data may not transfer ([Toribe 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmpt.2025.11.001)). Cadaver dye spread rose by a mean 1.1 mm after repeated straight leg raise, which is rationale only ([Gilbert 2015](https://doi.org/10.1179/2042618615Y.0000000009)).

### Surgery, as context for expectations

Discectomy improved leg pain faster than non-surgical care: immediate -12.1 (-23.6 to -0.5), short -11.7 (-18.6 to -4.7), medium -6.5 (-11.0 to -2.1), 12 months -2.3 (-4.5 to -0.2), with no difference at 24 months, on very low to low certainty ([Liu 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2022-070730)). For sciatica lasting 4 to 12 months, leg pain at 6 months was 2.8 with surgery versus 5.2 nonoperatively, and 34% crossed over ([Bailey 2020](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1912658)). Persistent sciatica may not settle with exercise alone. Non-surgical arms in these trials do not isolate exercise.

### Harms

Harms are poorly measured. In [Fritz 2021](https://doi.org/10.7326/M20-4187), 37 of 100 people reported 133 side effects, mostly increased back pain (24) and stiffness (19). Thirteen were rated severe, and 7 lasted beyond 24 hours. [Hahne 2011](https://doi.org/10.3109/09638288.2010.533814) found minor adverse events in 8% and one adhesive capsulitis. [Lin 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/life13122255) says none of its 20 trials reported adverse events, which likely reflects non-collection, since [Danazumi 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/10669817.2023.2192975) did not measure them and [Peacock 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/10669817.2022.2065599) did not treat them as a formal outcome. [Boyd 2017](https://doi.org/10.1080/10669817.2016.1180772) saw no symptom provocation in a single session of self-performed sliders, in people with diabetes. [Albert centralisation 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-011-2018-9) saw no worsening or new neurological deficit during repeated movement testing. [Shacklock 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/09593985.2022.2037797) reported no adverse responses, among 20 patients.

### Medication and injections, briefly

Outside the scope of an exercise app, and guidelines disagree. NICE says not to offer gabapentinoids, other antiepileptics, oral corticosteroids or benzodiazepines for sciatica ([NICE NG59](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59/chapter/Recommendations)), while [Pangarkar 2019 (VA/DoD)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-019-05086-4) finds evidence on gabapentinoids insufficient. [Price 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/pmrj.13142) found very little agreement across 11 guidelines. NASS recommends transforaminal injection for short-term relief at Grade A ([Kreiner 2014 (NASS)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spinee.2013.08.003)), and VA/DoD recommends against epidural steroids for long-term reduction ([Pangarkar 2019 (VA/DoD)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-019-05086-4)). The app should not advise on these.

Spinal load data sit behind several of these. Disc pressure in one healthy person was 0.1 MPa prone, 0.5 standing, 1.1 standing flexed and 0.46 sitting ([Wilke 1999](https://doi.org/10.1097/00007632-199904150-00005)). [Sato 1999](https://doi.org/10.1097/00007632-199912010-00008) found load higher sitting (996 N) than standing (800 N), and [Rohlmann 2001](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140130120943) found sitting slightly lower. Herniations were larger on standing weight-bearing MRI in 37 patients, with higher nerve root compression grades for paracentral ones ([Nordberg 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-020-07132-w)), with no link to exercise. Novices had higher L4/L5 moments late in a flat-floor squat, and that difference became non-significant with heels elevated, at light loads ([Sayers 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2020.1738675)). Sumo-style deadlifts showed 10% lower moment than conventional in elite lifters ([Cholewicki 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1758295/)). Controlling early morning flexion reduced pain by a point estimate of 33% in chronic non-specific low back pain, which applies to sciatica only by inference ([Snook 1998](https://doi.org/10.1097/00007632-199812010-00015)). None of these sets a safe limit.

## 6. Contradictions and limitations

### Contradictions

- **Does exercise help?** [Kreiner 2014 (NASS)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spinee.2013.08.003) finds insufficient evidence, [Dove 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-022-07356-y) finds null pooled effects, and [Lewis 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spinee.2013.08.049) finds exercise inferior to injection or surgery. Yet [Stochkendahl 2018 (Danish)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-017-5099-2) recommends supervised exercise, [Neuropathic pain consensus 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2021.756940) gives Grade A, and [Fritz 2021](https://doi.org/10.7326/M20-4187) is positive. The difference partly reflects comparators, populations and old physiotherapy content.
- **Direction-guided exercise.** [Albert 2012](https://doi.org/10.1097/BRS.0b013e31821ace7f) and [Fritz 2021](https://doi.org/10.7326/M20-4187) are positive, while [Kilpikoski 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/02692155231196393) found one advice session matched seven visits at 24 months. Common teaching treats peripheralisation as a bad sign, but [Albert centralisation 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-011-2018-9) found peripheralisers improved about as much as centralisers, while the no-effect group improved least (RMQ 3.0 vs 9.5 and 12.0). The peripheralisation group was small, and the abstract and body disagree on a time point (3 months vs 8 weeks).
- **Neural mobilisation.** Large pooled effects in [Lin 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/life13122255) sit beside null trials: [Ferreira 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphys.2016.08.007), [Sousa Filho 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbmt.2022.01.007), [Plaza-Manzano 2020](https://doi.org/10.1097/PHM.0000000000001295), [Nahid 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/hsr2.70872). [George 2021 (JOSPT)](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2021.0304) therefore limits its recommendation to short-term adjunctive use, and evidence behind it found neural mobilisation superior to minimal intervention but not to comparators.
- **Sliders vs tensioners.** The earlier verification removed a restriction on tensioners because [Lin 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/life13122255) does not advise one. The new excursion studies do not change that. Lin's subgroups are not head-to-head, and excursion differences vary from about 5-fold to none.
- **Slump position.** [Shaheen 2025](https://doi.org/10.23736/S1973-9087.24.08541-1) favours slump, but [Ellis 2017](https://doi.org/10.1179/2042618615Y.0000000020) found no change in excursion and [Pourahmadi 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/pm/pny208) suggests non-radicular pain may benefit most.
- **Traction.** [NICE NG59](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59/chapter/Recommendations) and [George 2021 (JOSPT)](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2021.0304) advise against. [Kreiner 2014 (NASS)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spinee.2013.08.003) and [Pangarkar 2019 (VA/DoD)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-019-05086-4) find evidence insufficient. [Wegner 2013 (Cochrane)](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003010.pub5) and [Thackeray 2016](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2016.6238) are null. [Vanti 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/ptj/pzaa231) is positive only in low-quality supine trials, and [Delitto 2012](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2012.42.4.A1) noted a possible nerve-root subgroup.
- **Sitting vs standing.** [Wilke 1999](https://doi.org/10.1097/00007632-199904150-00005) and [Rohlmann 2001](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140130120943) show lower sitting load, [Sato 1999](https://doi.org/10.1097/00007632-199912010-00008) the opposite.
- **Lifting technique.** [Stoop-to-squat modelling 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2021.769117) found lower compression stooping, though shear was generally higher. [Neutral spine review 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports14040151) argues strict neutral may be oversimplified, especially for trained lifters. [Mundt 1993, lifting](https://doi.org/10.1097/00007632-199304000-00012) links bent-back lifting to herniation onset, and [Mundt 1993, sports](https://doi.org/10.1177/036354659302100617) finds no link with weight equipment. [Deadlift narrative review 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34875981/) found only two studies of deadlift spinal loads. These address onset in healthy people, not recovery.
- **Flexion.** [Shacklock 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/09593985.2022.2037797) used flexion in side-lying and forward bending with benefit, and [VanGelder 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24175134/) avoids flexion in phase I.
- **Referral timing.** 6 to 8 weeks in [Luites 2022 (Dutch occupational)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10926-021-09993-4), 12 weeks in [Stochkendahl 2018 (Danish)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-017-5099-2), before six months in [Kreiner 2014 (NASS)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spinee.2013.08.003).
- **Discectomy label.** [Khorami 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm10112482) reports could-do in its table and should-do in its abstract.

### Limitations

- **Self-direction.** No trial tested self-directed neural mobilisation alone. Home components were always attached to supervised care.
- **Gym lifts.** No study tested them in symptomatic lifters. Evidence comes from healthy, cadaveric, porcine and case data. This absence is not evidence that the lifts are safe.
- **Negative findings.** Neural mobilisation was absent from [NICE NG59](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59/chapter/Recommendations), [Kreiner 2014 (NASS)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spinee.2013.08.003), [Pangarkar 2019 (VA/DoD)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-019-05086-4), [Aldera 2026 (Saudi)](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm15020528), [Luites 2022 (Dutch occupational)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10926-021-09993-4), [Khorami 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm10112482) and [Briggs 2025 (WHO commentary)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41256-025-00426-w). That means they are silent, not that they advise against it. The Cochrane title filter found no exercise review for sciatica, which does not prove none exists.
- **Doses.** Most are from single small trials. Doses for bird dog, glute bridge, pelvic tilt and core bracing were given to all arms of one trial, so their effect is untested. Cat-cow comes from a co-intervention given to both groups of one trial. Dead bug is not named in any source.
- **Population.** Much guidance covers low back pain with leg pain, not confirmed radiculopathy.
- **Reading depth.** Summarising fetches or keyword extraction were used for parts of [Dove 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-022-07356-y), [Liu 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2022-070730), [Lin 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/life13122255), [Baptista 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/02692155231215216), [VanGelder 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24175134/), [STOPS protocol 2011](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2474-12-104), [Delitto 2012](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2012.42.4.A1), [Kreiner 2014 (NASS)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spinee.2013.08.003) and [George 2021 (JOSPT)](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2021.0304). [Peacock 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/10669817.2022.2065599) doses could not be reopened in the citation check, so confirm them before use in product text. One [Dove 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-022-07356-y) subgroup was omitted as internally inconsistent.
- **Inferred grades.** [George 2021 (JOSPT)](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2021.0304) grade letters are inferred from obligation wording and its Table 2.
- **Secondary reading.** [Stochkendahl 2018 (Danish)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-017-5099-2) was read in a third-party reprint without its grade tables. [Briggs 2025 (WHO commentary)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41256-025-00426-w) stands in for the WHO guideline, so individual recommendations and certainty ratings are unconfirmed. NICE's 2026 deletion rationale came from a secondary summary, and [Aldera 2026 (Saudi)](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm15020528) still follows the deleted wording.

### Citation check

An independent checker opened 101 of the 102 sources. Only NICE NG59 could not be opened at all.

**Removed.**

- avoidOrModify: Neural mobilisation taken to the pain threshold, or performed immediately before strength work. Hitier 2026 tested asymptomatic adults and found flossing at the pain threshold reduced nerve stiffness, with no harm reported. Strength fell across all conditions including the control, with no condition by time interaction, so the fall cannot be attributed to the stretching.
- Swinton 2011: hexagonal bar still loads the spine substantially. The abstract reports lower peak lumbar moment and does not make this statement.
- Shacklock 2023: the lead author developed the method. Not stated in the abstract read.
- Trap bar deadlift at the lightest possible load. VanGelder 2013 gives the lightest-load instruction for the kettlebell deadlift. No such instruction for the trap bar was found in the text read, so the exercise was renamed.
- Todd 2025 as a source for the sexual dysfunction red flag. It names sexual function only in passing, for the chronic presentation, and not as a referral trigger. The York pathway still supports the item.
- Briggs 2025 as a source for traction and belts. The commentary does not mention them in its text. Replaced by the WHO news release, which was opened and does.

**Corrected.**

- Shaheen 2025 slider volume: 150 to 100 reps per session (10+15+20+25+30).
- Pradhan 2024 neurodynamic dose: 3 sets of 10 belonged to the conventional exercises in both groups. The glides were therapist-delivered, 5 glides then 1 minute rest for 5 minutes, 10 sessions in 14 days. Sample corrected from not captured to 58.
- Cat-cow: dose changed from not established to 3 sets of 10, because Pradhan 2024 names cat-and-camel, as a co-intervention in both groups.
- Side plank: VanGelder 2013 does name an advanced side plank in phase I, still without a dose.
- Luites 2022: behavioural treatment is considered after 6 weeks, not 12. Multidisciplinary rehabilitation is the 12-week step.
- Luijsterburg 2007: physical therapy vs inactive treatment rests on 1 high-quality trial, not 30.
- Lin 2023 table: Morsi 2022 frequency is 3 days per week for 2 weeks. Vijayalakshmi 2022 is slump position, 10 sessions over 3 weeks, with tensioners of 5-7 repetitions of 15-25 seconds.
- Khorami 2021: its table lists 7 guidelines each for physical activity and exercise, while its text cites 8 and 6.
- Stynes 2018: reference standards differ in agreement, rather than conflict.
- Nordberg 2021: higher compression grades were for paracentral herniations; size increased generally.
- Neutral spine review: applies to trained lifters; the paper does not say uninjured.
- Sjoberg 2023: participants described as resistance-trained, since the abstract does not state symptom status. Ellis 2021: described as in vivo, since the abstract does not state healthy.
- Citations: first authors corrected for Hennemann (V), Shinde (AP), Shaheen (HM), Zhu acute (Z), Ferreira protocol (GE), Toribe (A); added for Zhang, Alshanqity, Ramirez, Cherni, von Arx, Hahne (STOPS). Eight missing DOIs added.
- Access levels: Todd 2025 and York pathway to full_text. Konstantinou 2008, van der Windt 2010 and Stynes 2018 to abstract_only. Kreiner 2014 to registry_or_summary. NICE NG59, Pangarkar 2019, Peacock 2023 and Danazumi 2023 to not_reconfirmed.

**Could not be verified.**

- NICE NG59: nice.org.uk returned HTTP 403 and no NICE copy could be opened, so its wording on traction, belts, normal activity, medication and alternative diagnoses was not reconfirmed. The 29 July 2026 update date was seen only in a vendor blog.
- Pangarkar 2019, Peacock 2023 and Danazumi 2023: PMC returned a reCAPTCHA page and only abstracts could be read. VA/DoD traction, support, weakness and medication statements, Peacock doses and its 322 total, and Danazumi's adverse event, hamstring and dose statements were not reconfirmed.
- Kreiner 2014: the NASS PDF returned no readable text, so grades were confirmed on the Guideline Central summary. The absence of neural mobilisation was not reconfirmed.
- Delitto 2012, George 2021, Khorami 2021 and VanGelder 2013 were read through a summarising fetch, and only part of each long page.
- Abstract-only sources: anything beyond the abstract, such as Luijsterburg 2008 content, was not rechecked unless its full text is listed as read. Primary slider trials were checked only as tabulated in Lin 2023.
- WHO guideline full text and UK national cauda equina documents were not opened.

### Earlier verification, retained

An earlier citation check removed three items. Nerve-tensioning movements were dropped from the restrictions because [Lin 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/life13122255) does not advise it. A supine straight-leg-raise slider was dropped because Lin lists Ahmed 2013 only as "SLR technique, slider". [Stochkendahl 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00586-017-5099-2) was dropped because its abstract did not support the trunk categories it was cited for. It returns here for its surgical referral and supervised exercise statements, read in a reprint, and it is still not used for trunk categories.

That check also relabelled two items as expert opinion: "anything that increases leg pain", because the advice went to both trial arms, and walking, because neither guideline recommends it for sciatica. The new searches did not change either label.
