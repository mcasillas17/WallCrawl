# Lumbar radiculopathy (sciatica)

**Overall evidence grade for exercise guidance: low.** This is general information for planning exercise around a condition. It is not diagnosis or treatment.

## Bottom line

Staying active is well supported as general advice, but no exercise type is established as better than the others. Neural sliders have the most specific dose data, but those trials are small and mostly high risk of bias. Sources conflict on how much structured exercise adds beyond advice.

| Question | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Physiotherapy vs control | 18 trials, all high or unclear risk of bias; inadequate evidence for clinical recommendations | Dove 2023 |
| Exercise vs advice to stay active | Small short-term leg pain benefit (low quality); no long-term difference (moderate quality) | Fernandez 2015 |
| Non-surgical options, chronic sciatica | 50 trials; very low confidence; none shown superior by high-quality evidence | Zhu 2025 |
| Neural mobilisation | 20 trials; large pooled effect on pain (g -1.097), but 65% high risk of bias and publication bias found | Lin 2023 |
| Symptom-guided vs sham exercise | Symptom-guided better for most outcomes (181 patients) | Albert 2012 |
| McKenzie vs guideline-based advice | No difference at 24 months; small trial (66 patients) | Kilpikoski 2024 |
| Individualised functional restoration vs advice | Less activity limitation; no leg pain difference (54 patients) | Hahne 2017 |
| Physical therapy added to GP care | Better only on patient-rated global effect at 12 months | Luijsterburg 2008 |

## Is the diagnosis contested?

Partly. The condition is accepted, but its definition is not standard. One review found definitions varied widely across 23 prevalence studies, with prevalence from 1.2% to 43% (Konstantinou 2008). Most physical tests perform poorly in isolation (van der Windt 2010), and reference standards conflict (Stynes 2018). A person's self-reported "sciatica" may not be true nerve-root pain.

## Avoid or modify

| Movement | Action | Why | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anything that increases leg pain | Modify | Trial advice was to stay active but reduce activity if leg pain increased; not tested separately (Albert 2012) | Expert opinion |
| Extended bed rest in place of normal activity | Modify | NICE encourages normal activity; Cochrane finds little or no difference in sciatica, so no added benefit rather than proven harm | Guideline |
| Neural mobilisation with cauda equina lesions, cord signs, or conditions such as Guillain-Barre syndrome or multiple sclerosis | Avoid | Listed as contraindications (Lin 2023) | Systematic review |
| One fixed direction of repeated flexion or extension for everyone | Modify | Direction is set by response; flexion can be considered in some older people (JOSPT 2021) | Guideline |
| Mechanical spinal traction | Avoid | NICE and JOSPT both advise against it; gym hanging was not assessed | Guideline |

The catalog has no neural slider or direction-guided movement, so those are marked new. Slider doses come from single small trials, as tabulated in Lin 2023, and were usually added to other treatment. Self-directed use is not established. The expert-opinion items are mappings onto broad categories, because no opened source names those exercises.

## Red flags

Stop exercising and get medical help for any of these.

**Emergency care the same day:**
- New bladder change: altered pattern, hesitancy, frequency, dribbling, incomplete emptying, loss of the sensation of passing urine, retention, or incontinence
- Any change in saddle sensation: numbness or tingling in the genital or perianal area
- Faecal incontinence

**Urgent medical assessment:**
- Sciatica in both legs
- Significant or worsening leg weakness, foot drop, or new difficulty walking
- New sexual dysfunction

**See a clinician:**
- New or changed symptoms raising concern for cancer, infection, trauma, or inflammatory disease

Sources: Todd 2025, the York pathway, and NICE NG59.

## Limitations

- **Evidence quality:** trials are mostly small, heterogeneous and at high or unclear risk of bias, and the conclusions conflict.
- **No dose for most exercises:** `sets` is null in the JSON where no dose was found. Doses were not invented.
- **Lifting, sitting and loaded flexion:** no opened source gave direct evidence for restricting them, so none is listed. This absence is not evidence that they are safe.
- **Population mismatch:** much JOSPT guidance covers low back pain with leg pain, not confirmed radiculopathy, and its walking review covered chronic low back pain.
- **Red flag sources:** these are UK-based, including a local pathway that says it is under review and a primary-care article summarising national guidance. The national documents were not opened, and thresholds may differ elsewhere.
- **Depth of reading:** Fernandez 2015, Zhu 2025, Albert 2012, Kilpikoski 2024, Hahne 2017, Konstantinou 2008, van der Windt 2010 and Stynes 2018 were read as abstracts only. Cochrane CD007612 was read as its evidence page only. The BMJ 2019 sciatica review could not be opened and is not used.

## Verification

An independent citation check opened all 17 sources.

**Removed:**
- Nerve-tensioning movements (avoid or modify): Lin 2023 does not advise modifying tensioners. Its tensioner subgroup showed a significant benefit, the confidence intervals overlap with sliders, and its discussion says neural mobilisation helped regardless of technique.
- Supine straight-leg-raise slider: Lin 2023 lists Ahmed 2013 only as "SLR technique, slider". The supine position is not stated, and the trial report could not be retrieved.
- Stochkendahl 2018: its abstract says only "different types of supervised exercise", which does not support the trunk categories it was cited for. The full text was unavailable and nothing else used it.

**Corrected:**
- "Anything that increases leg pain": RCT to expert opinion, because the advice went to both trial arms and was never randomised.
- Walking: guideline to expert opinion, because neither guideline recommends walking for sciatica.
- Neural mobilisation treatment length in the JSON limitations: 2 to 6 weeks to 6 days to 6 weeks.

**Could not be fully checked:**
- Slider trials were checked only as tabulated in Lin 2023.
- JOSPT 2021 was read through extracts of its PMC page, not by direct text search.
- Sources listed under "Depth of reading" were read only at that depth.
- The UK national cauda equina documents behind Todd 2025 were not opened.
