# Patellofemoral pain

General exercise information for planning around a condition. This is not diagnosis or treatment.

**Overall evidence grade: low.** Structured data is in `patellofemoral_pain.json`.

## Scope and search log

This review covers exercise, load advice and safety for adults and adolescents with patellofemoral pain: pain around or behind the kneecap, worse with loading on a bent knee. Passive treatments, orthoses, taping and surgery are included where they bear on exercise choice or are tested alongside it.

Three independent searchers ran 38 searches across PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, the web and direct fetches of NICE, Cochrane and BMJ. Every PMID was then checked against PubMed for title, DOI and PMCID. The result is 90 sources with PMIDs and 2 AAFP articles without, carried over from the earlier version.

Gaps in the search:

- **The APTA guideline itself was not read.** jospt.org returned HTTP 403, a browser fallback failed and Unpaywall reports no open copy. Its PubMed abstract ([Willy 2019](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2019.0302)) holds no recommendations, so every grade below comes through [Connell 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815180/) or [Marra 2020](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2020/1001/p442.html).
- **NICE:** NICE CKS returned HTTP 403, and a NICE site search reported 4 results whose titles were not extracted.
- **AAOS:** web searches found no patellofemoral guideline from AAOS or WHO, but the AAOS index was not checked. This is a null search and not proof of absence.
- **BMJ:** [Collins 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2018-099397) and [Neal 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2024-108110) were blocked at full text and read at abstract level.
- **Cochrane:** the Cochrane Library returned HTTP 403, so reviews were reached through PubMed and PMC.
- Several large listings were screened only in part, as the log shows.

## Evidence summary

Exercise probably helps, with low certainty, and no specific programme is established. Effects are clearest at about 3 months. [Neal 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.11359) found no intervention adequately tested beyond that.

### Diagnosis

Not treated as contested, with caveats. [Crossley 2016 Part 1](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096384) defines the condition as pain around or behind the kneecap, aggravated by at least one activity loading the joint on a flexed, weight-bearing knee. No test is definitive; the best available is anterior knee pain during squatting. It remains a clinical diagnosis with no structural finding [Gaitonde 2019](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2019/0115/p88). The APTA criteria, as restated by [Connell 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815180/), add exclusion of other conditions (grade B). Terminology differs: [Crossley 2016 Part 1](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096384) treats chondromalacia patella as a synonym, and [Gaitonde 2019](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2019/0115/p88) lists it as a differential.

[Wallis 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/ptj/pzab021) reviewed four guidelines and found them consistent on exercise, orthoses, taping, education and combined care, and against electrotherapy. [Blazey 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-107552) is critical of this base: 2 of 22 consensus panels included a patient and 7 of 22 reported an established consensus method. Whether following guidelines improves outcomes is not yet settled. [Farrokhi 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/pmrj.13298) is a retrospective pilot, with AKPS meaningful improvement at 1 month in 13/23 against 1/17, and missing data. [Beisheim-Ryan 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/ptj/pzaf138) is a protocol for a 440-person RCT. [Willy 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ptsp.2022.04.009) found most of 494 surveyed therapists held inaccurate beliefs about risk factors and prognosis.

### Exercise against control

- [van der Heijden 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010387.pub2) (31 trials, 1,690 participants, GRADE very low throughout): pain during activity short term MD -1.46 on a 0 to 10 scale (95% CI -2.39 to -0.54). Function SMD 1.10 (0.58 to 1.63), re-expressed as AKPS 12.21 higher (6.44 to 18.09; MCID 10). Long-term recovery RR 1.35 (0.99 to 1.84). No clear difference between supervised and home exercise.
- [Neal 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.11359) (65 RCTs): knee-targeted exercise pain SMD 1.16 (0.66 to 1.66), function SMD 1.19 (0.51 to 1.88). Combined interventions pain SMD 0.79 (0.26 to 1.29), function SMD 0.98 (0.47 to 1.49).
- [Winters 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102819) (22 RCTs, 1,472 patients): any improvement at 3 months against wait-and-see, education OR 9.6 (95% CrI 2.2 to 48.8), exercise OR 13.0 (2.4 to 83.5), education plus exercise plus taping or mobilisations OR 25.2 (5.7 to 130.3). No treatment was superior on worst pain at 3 months. At 12 months, education and combinations gave similar rates (OR 1.5, 0.9 to 2.4).
- [Saltychev 2018](https://doi.org/10.2340/16501977-2295) dissents: over 80% of 37 adult trials showed no clinically significant benefit, and most were underpowered.
- [Park 2026](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2025-110863) (216 adults, 10 hospitals): 8-week smartphone exercise plus CBT, usual pain -12.5 mm (-17.1 to -7.8) against a 20 mm MCID, AKPS +5.2 (2.7 to 7.7). No intervention-related serious adverse events. The package cannot be credited to exercise alone.

### Which muscles to target

- **For hip plus knee over knee alone:** [van der Heijden 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010387.pub2) pain MD -2.20 (-3.80 to -0.60). [Nascimento 2018](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2018.7365) pain MD -1.5 (-2.3 to -0.8), activity SMD 0.7 (0.2 to 1.3). [Halabi 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/msc.70059) pain SMD -1.29 (-1.98 to -0.59), function SMD 0.99 (0.22 to 1.76), I² 87 to 88%, almost all women. [Neal 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.11359) pain SMD 1.02 (0.58 to 1.46). [Rogan 2019](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002658) pain SMD -0.99 (-1.99 to 0.02), crossing zero; function SMD 0.70 (0.25 to 1.14). [Fukuda 2012](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2012.4184) favoured knee plus hip by 20.8 to 22.0 LEFS points over 12 months in 54 women. [Khayambashi 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2013.12.022) favoured hip over quadriceps at 6 months in 36 people.
- **Against any clear preference:** [Hansen 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-106197) (200 people) found quadriceps and hip programmes equivalent, AKPS difference 0.6 (-2.0 to 3.2), neither exceeding the minimal important change. [Hott 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546519830644) (112 people, 6 weeks, all with education) found no difference at 3 months: knee against free activity 0.2 (-5.5 to 6.0), hip against free activity 1.0 (-4.6 to 6.6). [Hott 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.13613) found the same at 12 months. [Contreras-Mora 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/muscles5030066) (15 RCTs, 1,002 people, moderate certainty, abstract) finds evidence insufficient to name a best strategy.
- **Who might respond to what:** [Hansen 2024](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2024.12503) found pain catastrophizing favoured hip exercise (8.3 AKPS points, 1.6 to 15.0) and severe baseline pain favoured quadriceps (-9.1, -15.7 to -2.6), as exploratory subgroups.
- **Trunk and others:** [Wang 2024](https://doi.org/10.1097/PHM.0000000000002513) reports core training short-term pain SMD -0.60 (-0.95 to -0.25; very low). [Xiong 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13018-025-05482-z) and [Xiao 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-026-09914-7) report small trials of functional and foot core training. [Kamel 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13018-024-04688-x) added short foot work in pronated feet, VAS difference 2.6 (1.8 to 3.4).
- **Exercise mix:** [Dischiavi 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsams.2020.09.001) found programmes mostly sagittal, concentric and non-weight-bearing, with single-leg, multiplanar and rotational work under-represented. [Gunhamn 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fresc.2025.1641054) found only 25 of 79 studies fully described training variables.

### Mechanism

Strength gain may not be how exercise works. [Nascimento 2018](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2018.7365) found no significant strength change despite benefit, and [Holden 2021](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2021.10674) found hip strength did not mediate improvement in pain or pain-free squats (abduction strength on pain-free squats, indirect effect -0.13, -0.49 to 0.23). [Nunes 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-026-02473-w) (82 trials, 4,023 people) found strong correlations, r -0.75 for knee extension and -0.91 for hip abduction, which cannot show cause. [Peng 2015](https://doi.org/10.1589/jpts.27.3873) found pain fell with no change in patellar tilt or offset. [Vasconcelos 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-78074-2) found adding power training gave no benefit (usual pain p=0.85), although it stopped at 37 of a required 74.

### Dose

No dose is established. [Young 2018](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2018.7637) found no dosing trend for this condition. [Holden 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2017-097547) found no trial reporting a complete prescription among 38. [de Oliveira 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ptsp.2023.07.006)'s exploratory regression in women reports, for function only, 'Most favorable results were achieved with 17 to 27 sets per session and >45 sets per week', with no volume association for pain, while [Severo-Silveira 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjpt.2026.101599) found one set matched three: AKPS +16.14 against +16.97, NPRS -4.67 against -3.58, in 42 women. [Gunhamn 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fresc.2025.1641054) describes typical programmes as low load and moderate volume, with a median of 6 weeks and 3 sessions per week.

### Education and load management

[Winters 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102819) and [Hott 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546519830644) show education alone or with free activity performing close to exercise at some time points. [Esculier 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096988) (69 runners) found no added symptom benefit from exercise or gait retraining beyond education on symptoms and training loads, though strength and step rate changed. [Pazzinatto 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rehab.2026.102157) is less favourable: education was worse than exercise short term, pain MD 1.46 (CI printed as 0.6 to 2.56) and function SMD -0.87 (-1.57 to -0.17), with no difference when delivered by a health professional (pain MD 0.80, -0.19 to 1.78), and educational material alone was worse (2.53, 0.54 to 4.53). All low to very low certainty. [de Oliveira Silva 2020](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2020.9400) points the same way. [Rathleff 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546519843915) reports 81% success at 12 months in 151 adolescents aged 10 to 14 with activity modification first, then exercise, without a control group.

### Stretching

No trial shows stretching alone helps. [Harvie 2011](https://doi.org/10.2147/JMDH.S24595) found stretching in 8 of 10 positive trials, always bundled, and reports the one isolated-stretching RCT then available showed no significant pain or function improvement. [Lee 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/1941738121993777) found no difference between static and dynamic quadriceps stretching, with no no-stretch control. [Jellad 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/2325967121989729) (109 patients) found added hip strengthening plus hip internal rotator stretching improved outcomes, but the two are combined. [Lopes 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jshs.2023.08.004) found reduced hamstring flexibility against pain-free controls, SMD -0.76 (-1.15 to -0.36), but not against the unaffected limb, SMD 0.25 (-0.14 to 0.64). [Culvenor 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102973) and [Briani 2026](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2025.13489) found only a single preventive stretching study, with no significant effect.

### Adjuncts

[Souto 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2024-108145) (45 RCTs): taping, vibration, EMG biofeedback and brace added to exercise did not differ from exercise alone (very low certainty). NMES pain SMD -0.27 (-0.53 to -0.02). [Smith 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010513.pub2): orthosis plus exercise short-term pain MD -0.46 (-1.16 to 0.24). [Callaghan 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006717.pub2): taping pain MD -0.15 (-1.15 to 0.85). [Hossain 2011](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008402.pub2): foot orthoses against flat insoles, RR 1.48 (1.11 to 1.99) for improvement at six weeks and not at one year, with more minor rubbing and blistering, RR 1.87 (1.21 to 2.91). [Martimbianco 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011289.pub2): NMES plus exercise pain MD -1.63 (-2.23 to -1.02), very low quality. [Nunes 2023](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2023.11956) found exercise with internal feedback changed frontal knee movement most (SMD -2.66), with no effect from single brace or tape applications. [Rabelo 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2017.08.018) found motor control training added little to strengthening in 34 women at 4 weeks.

Blood flow restriction needs a cuff and is not obviously usable in an unsupervised app. [Hou 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2026.1859305) reports longer-term pain SMD 0.37 and no function difference. [Giles 2017](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096329) found reduced daily-activity pain and no difference in Kujala or worst pain, [Kong 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2025.06.003) no conclusive difference (-8.75, -21.18 to 3.69), and [Lee 2026](https://doi.org/10.12659/MSM.950516) greater strength gain without a clinical pain outcome.

### Risk factors, course and psychology

[Neal 2019](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2017-098890) (18 studies, 4,818 people): age, height, weight, BMI and Q angle were not risk factors, and neither was hip weakness (SMD -0.09 to -0.20). Quadriceps weakness was a risk factor in the military (SMD -0.69, -1.02 to -0.35). Higher hip abduction strength was a risk factor in adolescents (SMD 0.71, 0.39 to 1.04). [Crossley 2016 Part 1](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096384) gives adolescent prevalence of 7 to 28%. Longer symptoms, higher baseline pain and poorer function predict worse recovery per [Connell 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815180/), and [Neal 2025](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2025.13491) shows prognostic research is thin on psychological and behavioural factors. [Vicenzino 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.10647) names catastrophizing, fear-avoidance and self-efficacy as the clinically important pain and psychological factors, on professional consensus. [Glaviano 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsams.2022.01.004) found pain greater than controls across 10 of 11 tasks.

## What to avoid or modify

Nothing here is a strict ban. All are modifications, and most rest on modelled joint stress in uninjured people, not on outcomes.

## Red flags

Exercise guidance should not continue past these without clinician input. They come from clinical reviews and guideline restatements and are not validated screening rules.

- **Large knee swelling, redness or increased warmth:** Stop exercising the knee and get prompt medical evaluation. These are not features of patellofemoral pain and can indicate infection, acute trauma or inflammatory joint disease. [Gaitonde 2019](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2019/0115/p88), [Connell 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815180/)
- **Knee locking or clicking suggesting something is caught in the joint:** Do not attribute this to patellofemoral pain; have a clinician assess the knee. It can indicate internal derangement such as a meniscal tear or loose body. [Gaitonde 2019](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2019/0115/p88)
- **Kneecap pain after an injury or direct trauma:** Have a clinician assess the knee before training it. Patellar fracture needs to be considered, using radiographs. [Gaitonde 2019](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2019/0115/p88), [Connell 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815180/)
- **Kneecap dislocation, a sense of the kneecap slipping out, or apprehension when it is pushed sideways:** Have a clinician assess the knee. These point to patellar instability, which consensus treats as a separate subgroup and the Dutch guideline places outside its scope. [Crossley 2016 Part 1](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096384), [Marra 2020](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2020/1001/p442.html), [Ophey 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/ksa.12367)
- **Pain and tenderness isolated to below the kneecap or the bump on the shin, especially in children and adolescents:** Have a clinician assess the knee. This fits tendinopathy or apophysitis more than patellofemoral pain. [Marra 2020](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2020/1001/p442.html), [Gaitonde 2019](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2019/0115/p88)
- **Knee pain in an adolescent, particularly with a presentation that does not fit:** Have a clinician assess the hip as well as the knee. The APTA guideline, as restated by Connell 2020, names slipped capital femoral epiphysis as a possible cause of knee pain. [Connell 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815180/)
- **Symptoms that do not fit the expected pattern or are not resolving, or any concern about tumour or infection:** Refer to a clinician. The APTA guideline, as restated by Connell 2020, asks clinicians to consider red flags (tumours, infection, fracture), yellow flags and other musculoskeletal conditions in this situation. [Connell 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815180/)
- **Knee swelling or substantially increasing pain caused by the exercise:** Stop that exercise and get it reviewed. Cochrane defines this as an adverse event of exercise therapy. The Dutch patient survey also records increased knee pain with exercise therapy in a few people. [van der Heijden 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010387.pub2), [Ophey 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/ksa.12367)
- **No improvement after a period of conservative care:** Return to a clinician. Timing varies: an AAFP review says imaging may help after four to eight weeks of conservative care, while the Dutch guideline reassesses at 6 weeks and reconsiders the diagnosis if there is no improvement after 12. Radiographs can help exclude osteoarthritis in people over 50. [Gaitonde 2019](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2019/0115/p88), [Ophey 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/ksa.12367)

## Contradictions and limitations

### Where sources disagree

- **Hip plus knee:** favoured by [van der Heijden 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010387.pub2), [Crossley 2016 Part 2](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096268), [Collins 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2018-099397), [Nascimento 2018](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2018.7365), [Halabi 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/msc.70059) and [Neal 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.11359). Not supported by [Hansen 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-106197), [Hott 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546519830644), [Hott 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.13613) or [Winters 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102819), and [Neal 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2024-108110) frames care as knee with or without hip. [Khayambashi 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2013.12.022) favours hip against Hansen's equivalence.
- **Starting order:** [Bolgla 2018](https://doi.org/10.4085/1062-6050-231-15) recommends gluteal work for 3 weeks first (SOR B), while [Hansen 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-106197) found the two programmes equivalent.
- **Mechanism:** [Holden 2021](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2021.10674) and [Nascimento 2018](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2018.7365) against [Nunes 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-026-02473-w).
- **Hip weakness:** not a risk factor per [Neal 2019](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2017-098890) and [Bolgla 2018](https://doi.org/10.4085/1062-6050-231-15), although hip strengthening is recommended.
- **Education:** close to exercise in [Winters 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102819) and [Esculier 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096988); worse than exercise overall in [Pazzinatto 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rehab.2026.102157).
- **Volume:** [de Oliveira 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ptsp.2023.07.006) and [Marra 2020](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2020/1001/p442.html) favour higher volume, de Oliveira for function only. [Severo-Silveira 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjpt.2026.101599) and [Young 2018](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2018.7637) do not.
- **Dry needling and manual therapy:** grade A against per [Connell 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815180/); uncertain in [Collins 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2018-099397); [Neal 2022](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2022.11359) reports function SMD 2.30 (1.60 to 3.00) for lower-quadrant manual therapy and [Neal 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2024-108110) keeps it as a support.
- **Foot orthoses:** grade A short term per [Connell 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815180/), later and more restricted in [Ophey 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/ksa.12367), and no added benefit over physiotherapy in [Hossain 2011](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008402.pub2).
- **Braces:** against as treatment, [Connell 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815180/) and [Smith 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010513.pub2); lowered onset in prevention, RR 0.40 (0.22 to 0.73), [Culvenor 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102973).
- **Taping:** tailored use grade B, [Connell 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815180/); null in [Souto 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2024-108145) and [Callaghan 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006717.pub2).
- **Visual feedback:** SOR A in [Bolgla 2018](https://doi.org/10.4085/1062-6050-231-15); grade B against during exercise in [Connell 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815180/).
- **Depth:** [Powers 2014](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2014.4936) and [Bolgla 2018](https://doi.org/10.4085/1062-6050-231-15) against the deep-range programme in [Severo-Silveira 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjpt.2026.101599).
- **Adding hip adduction:** [Chen 2026](https://doi.org/10.12659/MSM.953994) positive, [Song 2009](https://doi.org/10.2522/ptj.20080195) null.
- **Dutch certainty:** [Ophey 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/ksa.12367) is described as low GRADE in its text and very low by one searcher's reading of its abstract.

### Reliability notes

- **Secondary sources:** all APTA grades are restated by [Connell 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815180/) or [Marra 2020](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2020/1001/p442.html), not read in [Willy 2019](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2019.0302). They should be checked against the original before being quoted as primary.
- **Trial quality:** [Hasan 2025](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.19381) has an implausibly large effect and inconsistent statistics, so its doses are low confidence. [Xiong 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13018-025-05482-z) and [Xiao 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-026-09914-7) list the same registration number, ChiCTR2400087664, unresolved.
- **Printed figures:** [van der Heijden 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010387.pub2) gives hip plus knee as -2.20 in its abstract and -2.02 in its summary table, with the same CI, which fits -2.20. [Pazzinatto 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rehab.2026.102157)'s CI is asymmetric as printed in PubMed.
- **Harms:** [Winters 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102819) dropped adverse events for lack of data. In [van der Heijden 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010387.pub2) only two trials said they actively recorded them. [Ophey 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/ksa.12367)'s survey of 52 patients (43 with patellofemoral pain, 9 with patellar tendinopathy) reports 12 (40%, denominator not stated) of those with patellofemoral pain dissatisfied with exercise therapy, and 3 of those reporting more knee pain.
- **Biomechanics:** nearly all from uninjured people and modelled stress. [Hart 2022](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-104686) found no discernible force differences between healthy people and those with pain during everyday activity.
- **Partial reading:** [van der Heijden 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010387.pub2) was read to about 200,000 of 502,300 characters in this round. [Winters 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102819), [Ophey 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/ksa.12367), [Crossley 2016 Part 2](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096268), [Gunhamn 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fresc.2025.1641054) and [Blazey 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-107552) were read in part or by targeted passage.
- **Populations:** [Rathleff 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546519843915) is ages 10 to 14 and uncontrolled. [Severo-Silveira 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjpt.2026.101599) and [de Oliveira 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ptsp.2023.07.006) are women only.
- **Dosing:** [Holden 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2017-097547) means any app-level dose is an extrapolation.
- **Pain rules:** no evidence-based threshold exists.

### Verification

An independent check opened all 92 sources, 90 through their PubMed records and 2 on the AAFP site, and every DOI redirects.

Removed:

- **Wall mini-squat to 45 degrees: "Keep the heels away from the wall" cue, with its escamilla2023 citation:** escamilla2023 found the heel-distance difference only at 90 and 100 degrees on ascent, and escamilla2009 found farther feet loading more at 60 degrees. Nothing supports the cue for a squat limited to 45 degrees.
- **Activities row: sitting provokes pain "despite low load":** glaviano2022 reports greater pain in sitting on limited evidence and says nothing about load; no cited source does.

Corrected:

- gunhamn2025 knee extension median intensity: 70 to 80% of 1RM changed to 70% of 1RM, range bodyweight to 80%.
- ophey2025 additional treatments: after 6 to 12 weeks changed to after 6 weeks, as the guideline states for patellofemoral pain.

- holden2021 -0.13 (-0.49 to 0.23) relabelled: it is abduction strength on pain-free squats, not pain.
- deoliveira2023 17 to 27 sets relabelled as function only; the paper found no volume association with pain.
- ishida2025 9% relabelled as peak stress; the at-90-degrees figure in the paper is reaction force, 11%.
