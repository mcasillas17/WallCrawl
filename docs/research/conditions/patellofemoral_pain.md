# Patellofemoral pain

Evidence summary for a private workout app. This is general information, not diagnosis or treatment. Structured data is in `patellofemoral_pain.json`.

**Overall evidence grade for exercise guidance: low.**

## Bottom line

Exercise probably helps, but the certainty is low and no specific programme is established.

- **Cochrane 2015** (31 trials, 1,690 participants): exercise may produce clinically important reductions in pain and improvements in function. Every main outcome was rated very low quality. Hip plus knee work may reduce pain more than knee work alone, also on very low quality evidence. [cochrane2015]
- **2018 consensus**: supports exercise, especially hip- and knee-focused work combined. [collins2018]
- **2021 network meta-analysis**: no exercise type was superior, and the body region exercised may not matter. Exercise beat wait-and-see for any improvement at 3 months, but not education alone, and no active treatment beat wait-and-see on pain intensity. Certainty was mostly low to very low. [winters2021]

The hip-plus-knee preference is therefore not fully settled.

## Diagnosis

Not treated as contested, though there are caveats. Consensus defines the condition as pain around or behind the kneecap, aggravated by at least one activity that loads the joint on a flexed, weight-bearing knee. [crossley2016] It remains a clinical diagnosis with no structural finding. [gaitonde2019] Trial criteria vary considerably. [cochrane2015] Terminology also differs: the consensus treats chondromalacia patella as a synonym, while the AAFP review lists it as a differential. [crossley2016, gaitonde2019]

## Movements to modify

Nothing is listed as a strict avoid. All four items are modifications.

| Movement | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Squatting beyond about 45 degrees of knee flexion | Higher modelled joint stress at 60 to 90 degrees; 10 healthy people; no outcome data [powers2014]. One trial's programme progressed to 90 degrees [ferber2015] | Biomechanical |
| Resisted seated knee extension near full straightening (about 45 to 0 degrees) | Higher modelled stress at 0 to 30 degrees [powers2014]; weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing work appear similarly effective [marra2020, cochrane2015] | Biomechanical |
| Activities that reproduce the pain: squats, stairs, running, jumping, prolonged sitting with bent knees | Avoid aggravators while staying active [gaitonde2019]; relative rest then gradual return [marra2020]; uncontrolled cohort of adolescents aged 10 to 14 [rathleff2019] | Observational |
| High running load, such as hills or high mileage | Listed option for runners [marra2020]; gait retraining evidence uncertain [collins2018] | Guideline summary |

Ferber prescribed exercise at least 6 days per week for 6 weeks, and about 4.8 to 4.9 days per week were achieved. Trials in the Cochrane review averaged 3 times per week. The guideline summary says higher volumes work best, for example 3 sets of 30 or more reps 3 times weekly as tolerated, without tying that volume to one exercise. Its preceding sentence lists single-leg squats, step downs, squats, lunges and band hip exercises, and it says the guideline does not recommend specific programmes. [marra2020]

## Red flags

Exercise guidance should not continue past these without clinician input.

- **Large swelling, redness or warmth:** stop and get prompt medical evaluation. Possible infection, trauma or inflammatory joint disease. [gaitonde2019]
- **Locking or clicking suggesting something caught in the joint:** clinician assessment. [gaitonde2019]
- **Kneecap pain after injury:** clinician assessment, as patellar fracture needs considering. [gaitonde2019]
- **Dislocation, slipping sensation or apprehension:** clinician assessment for patellar instability. [crossley2016, marra2020]
- **Pain isolated below the kneecap or at the shin bump, especially in young people:** clinician assessment for tendinopathy or apophysitis. [marra2020, gaitonde2019]
- **No improvement after four to eight weeks of conservative measures:** return to a clinician. [gaitonde2019]

## Limitations

- The full APTA guideline (HTTP 403) and full 2018 consensus (HTTP 403) were not read. Guideline content comes from its PubMed record and an AAFP summary, so individual recommendation grades are unverified.
- No NICE page for this condition was opened.
- No evidence-based pain threshold for continuing exercise was found.
- The bodyweight side-lying abduction and shallow-range adaptations were not tested as such.
- Stretch-specific evidence is sparse, and two exercises have null sets because no usable per-exercise value was found.
- The Powers depth limits come from 10 healthy people and estimate stress, not outcomes.
- The Rathleff cohort was uncontrolled and covers ages 10 to 14 only.
- Red flags come from clinical reviews, not validated screening rules.
- Parts of the trial data were extracted by an automated page-reading tool and not checked line by line. The final 2,300 characters of the Cochrane page were not read.

- **Strength labels and evidence grade:** no change needed.
- **Details corrected:** rathleff2019 citation; hamstring stretch schedule; the marra2020 volume statement; the winters2021 wait-and-see summary, which applies to any improvement and not pain intensity.
