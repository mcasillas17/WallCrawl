# WallCrawl authored exercise supplement

The four neutral exercise descriptions and newly generated illustrations in this
supplement are WallCrawl contributor work licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0:
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/. The app code and its MIT license
remain separate. The source-resolution SVGs were traced from retained native PNGs;
three frame slots per variant deliberately reuse one held pose. This artwork was
created from text descriptions, except the selected PHT bridge variants, which are
native edits of earlier generated images to make head/plinth contact visible.
Publisher figures were inspected privately for execution facts and were not supplied
to image generation or packaged into the app. Their availability is not a legal
clearance assertion. No study author, publisher or public agency endorses this art.

Execution references (the listed locations inform the neutral pose descriptions,
not a treatment prescription):

- Shamsi, Mirzaei and Hopayian, “A controlled clinical trial investigating the
  effects of stretching and compression exercises on electromyography of calf
  muscles in chronic LBP patients with a deep gluteal syndrome” (2024),
  doi:10.1186/s13102-023-00802-4, Methods > Stretching exercise and Figure 2.
- Rich et al., “Treatment of Proximal Hamstring Tendinopathy with Individualized
  Physiotherapy: A Clinical Commentary” (2025), doi:10.26603/001c.138308,
  Stage 1 > Figure 3(a,b). Only the calf-supported single-leg held variant is shown.
- State Insurance Regulatory Authority (NSW), “Guidelines for the management of
  acute whiplash-associated disorders – for health professionals,” third edition
  (2014), Appendix 3, Exercise 9 (printed p. 45 / PDF p. 49). The source places
  strengthening later in recovery; the catalog cannot assess that stage.
- Naderi, Fallah Mohammadi, Heidaralizadeh and Moen, “Effects of Integrating Lower-Leg Exercises Into a Multimodal Therapeutic
  Approach on Medial Tibial Stress Syndrome Management Among Recreational
  Runners: A Randomized Controlled Study” (2025),
  doi:10.1177/23259671241311849, Methods > Lower-Leg Exercise Intervention,
  Table 1. Only the seated short-foot stage is shown. This multimodal study did
  not establish added pain or severity benefit from the lower-leg exercises.
